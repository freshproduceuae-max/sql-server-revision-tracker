# G11-T06 — Profiling | Temporal Profile

## ID
G11-T06

## Group
11 — Profiling

## Type
Temporal Profile — Understanding How Data Volume and Characteristics Change Over Time

## Live Scenario
A business intelligence analyst is building a customer activity model. Before modelling, they profile the temporal distribution of orders. The profile reveals: 78% of orders are from January–March 2024, 20% from April–June 2024, and only 2% from July 2024 (the current month). This looks like a seasonal pattern until the analyst notices the data was loaded on July 8 — the 2% of July orders are the first 8 days only. More critically, a data gap appears: no orders exist for February 14–28 — 15 days of missing data from a warehouse connectivity outage. Including this data in a model without awareness of the gap would bias the February-average model downwards by 46%. The temporal profile makes both issues immediately visible.

## The Question
> A temporal profile shows how data volume, value distributions, and characteristics change over time. SQL produces temporal profiles by aggregating by time period (day, week, month, quarter) and comparing period-level metrics to detect trends, seasonality, gaps, and anomalies.
> **"Produce a monthly temporal profile of Orders and Transactions: show record count, total value, average value, and the month-over-month change for each metric, flagging any months with unusual volume or value."**

## Table Reference
**Orders** — `schemas/02_Orders.csv`
**Transactions** — `schemas/05_Transactions.csv`

## Solution Query

```sql
-- Step 1: Monthly order profile with MoM change
WITH MonthlyOrders AS (
    SELECT
        YEAR(OrderDate)  AS Yr,
        MONTH(OrderDate) AS Mo,
        COUNT(*)         AS OrderCount,
        SUM(TotalAmount) AS MonthlyTotal,
        AVG(TotalAmount) AS AvgOrderValue,
        MIN(TotalAmount) AS MinOrder,
        MAX(TotalAmount) AS MaxOrder,
        COUNT(DISTINCT CustomerID) AS UniqueCustomers,
        COUNT(DISTINCT ProductID)  AS UniqueProducts
    FROM Orders
    WHERE OrderDate IS NOT NULL
    GROUP BY YEAR(OrderDate), MONTH(OrderDate)
)
SELECT
    Yr AS Year,
    Mo AS Month,
    OrderCount,
    MonthlyTotal,
    ROUND(AvgOrderValue, 2) AS AvgOrderValue,
    UniqueCustomers,
    -- Month-over-month change
    LAG(OrderCount)    OVER (ORDER BY Yr, Mo) AS PrevOrderCount,
    LAG(MonthlyTotal)  OVER (ORDER BY Yr, Mo) AS PrevMonthlyTotal,
    OrderCount - LAG(OrderCount) OVER (ORDER BY Yr, Mo) AS OrderCountChange,
    ROUND(MonthlyTotal - LAG(MonthlyTotal) OVER (ORDER BY Yr, Mo), 2) AS ValueChange,
    ROUND(100.0 * (MonthlyTotal - LAG(MonthlyTotal) OVER (ORDER BY Yr, Mo))
          / NULLIF(LAG(MonthlyTotal) OVER (ORDER BY Yr, Mo), 0), 1) AS ValueChangePct,
    CASE
        WHEN LAG(OrderCount) OVER (ORDER BY Yr, Mo) IS NULL
             THEN 'First period'
        WHEN OrderCount = 0
             THEN 'ZERO ORDERS: Gap or non-trading period'
        WHEN OrderCount < LAG(OrderCount) OVER (ORDER BY Yr, Mo) * 0.5
             THEN 'SIGNIFICANT DROP: Less than 50% of prior month'
        WHEN OrderCount > LAG(OrderCount) OVER (ORDER BY Yr, Mo) * 2
             THEN 'SIGNIFICANT SPIKE: More than 2× prior month'
        ELSE 'Normal variation'
    END AS TemporalAnomaly
FROM MonthlyOrders
ORDER BY Yr, Mo;

-- Step 2: Weekly volume profile — detect day-of-week and week patterns
SELECT
    DATEPART(WEEKDAY, OrderDate) AS DayOfWeek,
    DATENAME(WEEKDAY, OrderDate) AS DayName,
    COUNT(*) AS OrderCount,
    SUM(TotalAmount) AS DailyTotal,
    AVG(TotalAmount) AS AvgDailyValue,
    ROUND(100.0 * COUNT(*) / SUM(COUNT(*)) OVER (), 1) AS PctOfTotal
FROM Orders
WHERE OrderDate IS NOT NULL
GROUP BY DATEPART(WEEKDAY, OrderDate), DATENAME(WEEKDAY, OrderDate)
ORDER BY DayOfWeek;

-- Step 3: Quarterly aggregation — higher-level trend
SELECT
    YEAR(OrderDate) AS Yr,
    DATEPART(QUARTER, OrderDate) AS Quarter,
    COUNT(*) AS OrderCount,
    SUM(TotalAmount) AS QuarterlyTotal,
    AVG(TotalAmount) AS AvgOrderValue,
    COUNT(DISTINCT CustomerID) AS UniqueCustomers,
    SUM(TotalAmount) - LAG(SUM(TotalAmount)) OVER (ORDER BY YEAR(OrderDate), DATEPART(QUARTER, OrderDate))
        AS QoQChange,
    ROUND(100.0 * (SUM(TotalAmount) - LAG(SUM(TotalAmount)) OVER (ORDER BY YEAR(OrderDate), DATEPART(QUARTER, OrderDate)))
          / NULLIF(LAG(SUM(TotalAmount)) OVER (ORDER BY YEAR(OrderDate), DATEPART(QUARTER, OrderDate)), 0), 1)
        AS QoQChangePct
FROM Orders
WHERE OrderDate IS NOT NULL
GROUP BY YEAR(OrderDate), DATEPART(QUARTER, OrderDate)
ORDER BY Yr, Quarter;

-- Step 4: Date range coverage statistics
SELECT
    MIN(CAST(OrderDate AS DATE)) AS EarliestOrder,
    MAX(CAST(OrderDate AS DATE)) AS LatestOrder,
    DATEDIFF(DAY, MIN(OrderDate), MAX(OrderDate)) + 1 AS TotalDaysInRange,
    COUNT(DISTINCT CAST(OrderDate AS DATE)) AS DaysWithOrders,
    DATEDIFF(DAY, MIN(OrderDate), MAX(OrderDate)) + 1
        - COUNT(DISTINCT CAST(OrderDate AS DATE)) AS DaysWithoutOrders,
    ROUND(100.0 * COUNT(DISTINCT CAST(OrderDate AS DATE))
          / NULLIF(DATEDIFF(DAY, MIN(OrderDate), MAX(OrderDate)) + 1, 0), 1) AS DayCoveragePct,
    MIN(MONTH(OrderDate)) AS EarliestMonth,
    MAX(MONTH(OrderDate)) AS LatestMonth,
    COUNT(DISTINCT YEAR(OrderDate) * 100 + MONTH(OrderDate)) AS MonthsWithData
FROM Orders;
```

## Explanation

**LAG for month-over-month change**
`LAG(MonthlyTotal) OVER (ORDER BY Yr, Mo)` returns the previous month's value. The MoM change is `CurrentTotal - PrevTotal`; the MoM change percentage is `(CurrentTotal - PrevTotal) / PrevTotal × 100`. The `NULLIF(PrevTotal, 0)` prevents division by zero for months that follow a zero-value period.

**Window functions over time-ordered partitions**
All period-over-period calculations require `ORDER BY Yr, Mo` (not just `ORDER BY Mo`) to correctly handle data spanning multiple years. A common mistake is `ORDER BY Mo` which incorrectly places January 2025 before February 2024.

**Day-of-week analysis for operational patterns**
Step 2's weekday breakdown reveals operational patterns: B2B companies see lower weekend volumes; retail sees higher weekend spikes. Unexpected patterns (e.g., Tuesday being the busiest day by far) may indicate a batch load running on Tuesdays, not a genuine business pattern.

**Coverage statistics for temporal completeness**
Step 4's `DayCoveragePct` (days with orders / total days in range) is the temporal equivalent of column completeness. 100% means orders every day; 60% means orders on 3 of 5 days (expected for weekday-only business). Values below the expected rate indicate data gaps.

## Severity
**Medium** — Temporal profiling is essential before any time-series analysis, forecasting, or trend reporting. Without it, undetected gaps bias averages (missing days lower the monthly count), seasonal patterns are mistaken for data quality issues, and models trained on incomplete time ranges produce biased forecasts.
