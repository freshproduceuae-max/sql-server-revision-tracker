# G19-T17 — Cleansing Operations | Email and URL Format Validation

## ID
G19-T17

## Group
19 — Cleansing Operations

## Type
Email and URL Format Validation — Detecting Malformed Email Addresses and URLs That Will Fail Delivery or Parsing

## Live Scenario
An email marketing platform sends a campaign to 45,000 customers. The delivery report shows 3,200 hard bounces — emails that could not be delivered because the address is structurally invalid. Investigation reveals: 400 addresses missing the @ sign ("alicea tcompany.com" — space instead of @), 800 addresses with a double dot in the domain ("alice@company..com"), 600 with no TLD ("alice@company"), 900 with the domain part empty ("alice@"), and 500 with trailing whitespace ("alice@company.com "). All could have been caught before the campaign with a format validation query. The email validation script prevents hard bounces, protects sender reputation (high bounce rates cause ISPs to blacklist the sending domain), and flags addresses requiring correction before the next send.

## The Question
> Email format validation uses LIKE pattern matching and CHARINDEX to verify the structural components of an email address: presence and position of the @ symbol, non-empty local part (before @), non-empty domain part (after @), presence of at least one dot in the domain, no leading or trailing whitespace, and no double dots. URL validation checks for a valid scheme (http:// or https://), a non-empty host, and absence of spaces. Neither check confirms deliverability — only an SMTP handshake or DNS lookup can confirm the address is live.
> **"Validate email addresses in the Customers table and any email-like fields: check for the presence and correct position of @, verify the domain contains at least one dot, detect leading/trailing whitespace, double dots, and missing TLD. Produce a validation scorecard categorising addresses as Valid Format, Correctable, or Requires Manual Review."**

## Table Reference
**Customers** — `schemas/01_Customers.csv`

## Solution Query

```sql
-- Step 1: Structural email validation — component-by-component
SELECT
    CustomerID,
    Email,
    LEN(ISNULL(Email,''))                         AS EmailLength,
    CHARINDEX('@', ISNULL(Email,''))               AS AtPosition,
    LEN(ISNULL(Email,'')) - CHARINDEX('@', ISNULL(Email,''))  AS DomainLength,
    CHARINDEX('.', REVERSE(ISNULL(Email,'')))      AS DotsFromEnd,
    -- Check each structural requirement
    CASE WHEN Email IS NULL THEN 'NULL'
         WHEN Email <> LTRIM(RTRIM(Email)) THEN 'WHITESPACE: Leading/trailing spaces'
         WHEN CHARINDEX('@', Email) = 0 THEN 'NO AT-SIGN: Missing @ character'
         WHEN CHARINDEX('@', Email) = 1 THEN 'EMPTY LOCAL: Nothing before @'
         WHEN CHARINDEX('@', Email) = LEN(Email) THEN 'EMPTY DOMAIN: Nothing after @'
         WHEN CHARINDEX('@', Email) <> LEN(Email) - CHARINDEX('@', REVERSE(Email)) + 1
              AND CHARINDEX('@', Email, CHARINDEX('@', Email)+1) > 0
              THEN 'MULTIPLE AT-SIGNS: More than one @ in address'
         WHEN CHARINDEX('.', SUBSTRING(Email, CHARINDEX('@', Email)+1, LEN(Email))) = 0
              THEN 'NO DOT IN DOMAIN: Domain part has no dot — missing TLD'
         WHEN Email LIKE '%..'  OR Email LIKE '%..%'
              THEN 'DOUBLE DOT: Consecutive dots are not valid in an email address'
         WHEN Email LIKE '%@.' OR Email LIKE '%.@%'
              THEN 'DOT ADJACENT TO @: "." cannot be immediately before or after @'
         WHEN LEN(Email) - LEN(REPLACE(SUBSTRING(Email, CHARINDEX('@',Email)+1, LEN(Email)), '.', ''))
              > 0 AND CHARINDEX('.', REVERSE(SUBSTRING(Email, CHARINDEX('@',Email)+1, LEN(Email)))) < 2
              THEN 'SHORT TLD: TLD appears to have fewer than 2 characters'
         ELSE 'STRUCTURE OK'
    END AS StructuralFlag
FROM Customers
WHERE Email IS NOT NULL
ORDER BY StructuralFlag DESC;

-- Step 2: Domain-level checks
SELECT
    CustomerID,
    Email,
    -- Extract domain part (after @)
    CASE WHEN CHARINDEX('@', ISNULL(Email,'')) > 0
         THEN SUBSTRING(Email, CHARINDEX('@', Email)+1, LEN(Email))
         ELSE NULL END AS DomainPart,
    -- Extract TLD (after last dot)
    CASE WHEN CHARINDEX('@', ISNULL(Email,'')) > 0
         THEN REVERSE(LEFT(REVERSE(Email), CHARINDEX('.', REVERSE(Email))-1))
         ELSE NULL END AS TLD,
    CASE
        WHEN Email IS NULL THEN 'NULL'
        WHEN CHARINDEX('@', ISNULL(Email,'')) = 0 THEN 'SKIP: No @ found'
        -- Common test/disposable domains
        WHEN LOWER(SUBSTRING(Email, CHARINDEX('@',Email)+1, LEN(Email)))
             IN ('example.com','test.com','mailinator.com','guerrillamail.com',
                 'tempmail.com','throwaway.email','yopmail.com')
             THEN 'TEST/DISPOSABLE DOMAIN: "' + SUBSTRING(Email, CHARINDEX('@',Email)+1, LEN(Email))
                  + '" is a known test or disposable mail domain'
        -- Internal/reserved domains
        WHEN LOWER(SUBSTRING(Email, CHARINDEX('@',Email)+1, LEN(Email)))
             IN ('localhost','internal','local','example.org','example.net')
             THEN 'RESERVED DOMAIN: "' + SUBSTRING(Email, CHARINDEX('@',Email)+1, LEN(Email))
                  + '" is a reserved/internal domain not routable on the internet'
        -- Very short TLD
        WHEN LEN(REVERSE(LEFT(REVERSE(Email), CHARINDEX('.', REVERSE(Email))-1))) < 2
             THEN 'VERY SHORT TLD: TLD appears to be 1 character — likely invalid'
        ELSE 'Domain format OK'
    END AS DomainFlag
FROM Customers
WHERE Email IS NOT NULL;

-- Step 3: Correctable email issues — auto-fix candidates
SELECT
    CustomerID,
    Email AS OriginalEmail,
    -- Auto-corrections
    LTRIM(RTRIM(Email)) AS WhitespaceCorrected,
    LOWER(LTRIM(RTRIM(Email))) AS LowercaseAndTrimmed,
    CASE
        WHEN Email <> LTRIM(RTRIM(Email))
             THEN 'AUTO-CORRECTABLE: Remove whitespace → "'
                  + LTRIM(RTRIM(Email)) + '"'
        WHEN Email <> LOWER(Email)
             THEN 'AUTO-CORRECTABLE: Lowercase → "' + LOWER(Email) + '"'
        ELSE 'Cannot auto-correct: manual review required'
    END AS AutoCorrectSuggestion,
    CASE
        WHEN Email <> LTRIM(RTRIM(Email)) OR Email <> LOWER(Email)
             THEN 'CORRECTABLE'
        WHEN CHARINDEX('@', ISNULL(Email,'')) = 0
          OR Email LIKE '%..'
          OR Email LIKE '%@.'
             THEN 'MANUAL REVIEW'
        ELSE 'VALID'
    END AS CorrectionCategory
FROM Customers
WHERE Email IS NOT NULL
  AND (Email <> LTRIM(RTRIM(Email))
    OR Email <> LOWER(Email)
    OR CHARINDEX('@', Email) = 0
    OR Email LIKE '%..'
    OR Email LIKE '%@.');

-- Step 4: Email quality scorecard with category breakdown
WITH EmailAssessment AS (
    SELECT
        CustomerID,
        Email,
        CASE
            WHEN Email IS NULL THEN 'NULL'
            WHEN Email <> LTRIM(RTRIM(Email))
              OR CHARINDEX('@', ISNULL(Email,'')) = 0
              OR CHARINDEX('.', SUBSTRING(ISNULL(Email,''), CHARINDEX('@', ISNULL(Email,''))+1, LEN(ISNULL(Email,'')))) = 0
              OR Email LIKE '%..'
              OR CHARINDEX('@', Email) = 1
              OR CHARINDEX('@', Email) = LEN(Email)
                 THEN 'INVALID FORMAT'
            WHEN Email <> LOWER(Email)
                 THEN 'CORRECTABLE (case)'
            ELSE 'VALID FORMAT'
        END AS Category
    FROM Customers
)
SELECT
    Category,
    COUNT(*) AS RecordCount,
    ROUND(100.0 * COUNT(*) / SUM(COUNT(*)) OVER (), 1) AS PctOfTotal
FROM EmailAssessment
GROUP BY Category
ORDER BY RecordCount DESC;

-- Step 5: Full email validation pass / fail summary
SELECT
    'Customers.Email' AS FieldName,
    COUNT(*) AS TotalRecords,
    SUM(CASE WHEN Email IS NULL THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS NullCount,
    SUM(CASE
        WHEN Email IS NOT NULL
         AND Email = LTRIM(RTRIM(Email))
         AND CHARINDEX('@', Email) > 1
         AND CHARINDEX('@', Email) < LEN(Email)
         AND CHARINDEX('.', SUBSTRING(Email, CHARINDEX('@', Email)+1, LEN(Email))) > 0
         AND Email NOT LIKE '%..'
         AND Email NOT LIKE '%@.'
         AND Email NOT LIKE '%.@%'
        THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS ValidFormatCount,
    SUM(CASE
        WHEN Email IS NOT NULL
         AND (Email <> LTRIM(RTRIM(Email))
           OR CHARINDEX('@', Email) = 0
           OR CHARINDEX('@', Email) = 1
           OR CHARINDEX('@', Email) = LEN(Email)
           OR CHARINDEX('.', SUBSTRING(ISNULL(Email,''), CHARINDEX('@',ISNULL(Email,''))+1, LEN(ISNULL(Email,'')))) = 0
           OR Email LIKE '%..'
           OR Email LIKE '%@.')
        THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS InvalidFormatCount,
    ROUND(100.0 * SUM(CASE
        WHEN Email IS NOT NULL
         AND Email = LTRIM(RTRIM(Email))
         AND CHARINDEX('@', Email) > 1
         AND CHARINDEX('@', Email) < LEN(Email)
         AND CHARINDEX('.', SUBSTRING(Email, CHARINDEX('@', Email)+1, LEN(Email))) > 0
         AND Email NOT LIKE '%..'
        THEN 1 ELSE 0 END)
        / NULLIF(COUNT(*) - SUM(CASE WHEN Email IS NULL THEN 1 ELSE 0 END), 0), 1)
        AS ValidFormatPct
FROM Customers;
```

## Explanation

**Structural validation vs deliverability validation**
SQL's LIKE and CHARINDEX checks validate email structure: that the address has the right shape (local@domain.tld). They cannot confirm that the address exists and can receive mail — that requires an SMTP probe or real email send. A structurally valid email ("alice@nonexistent-domain-xyz123.com") will hard bounce. Structural validation eliminates obviously wrong addresses; deliverability validation (via an email verification API) eliminates structurally valid but undeliverable ones.

**CHARINDEX for @ position vs counting occurrences**
`CHARINDEX('@', Email)` returns the position of the first @. To check for multiple @ signs, compare the position of the first @ with the position of the last @: `CHARINDEX('@', Email) <> LEN(Email) - CHARINDEX('@', REVERSE(Email)) + 1`. If both positions are different, there are multiple @ signs. Alternatively, `LEN(Email) - LEN(REPLACE(Email, '@', '')) > 1` counts the occurrences.

**Disposable and test email domains**
Structurally valid addresses at known disposable domains (mailinator.com, guerrillamail.com, yopmail.com) are almost always temporary and will not be accessible within hours or days. Including them in an email list wastes send capacity and may generate bounces when the temporary mailbox expires. Step 2 flags a curated list of known disposable domains.

**Why lowercase normalisation matters**
Email addresses are technically case-sensitive in the local part (RFC 5321), but virtually all providers treat them case-insensitively. Storing emails in mixed case creates deduplication failures and suppression list mismatches. Always normalise to lowercase before storing or comparing email addresses. This is safe for all major email providers.

## Severity
**Medium to High** — Invalid email addresses cause hard bounces that damage sender reputation. Email service providers (ESPs) automatically block senders whose hard bounce rate exceeds 2–5%. Once blacklisted, all subsequent campaign emails are rejected — affecting deliverability to all customers, not just those with invalid addresses. Pre-send validation is a routine data quality gate for any email programme.
