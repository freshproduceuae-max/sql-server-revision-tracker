# G08-T11 — Reconciliation | Unallocated Cash

## ID
G08-T11

## Group
8 — Reconciliation

## Type
Unallocated Cash — Payments Received That Cannot Be Matched to an Invoice

## Live Scenario
A professional services firm receives a BACS payment of £7,200 from a client. The payment reference contains only the client's company name, not an invoice number. The AR team cannot match it to any specific invoice and parks it in a "suspense" account pending clarification. Two months later, the suspense account holds £43,700 of unallocated receipts. Some are partial payments split across multiple invoices; some have incorrect references; one turns out to be a payment from the wrong entity (subsidiary paid instead of parent). Without invoice-level allocation, the firm cannot confirm which invoices are settled, continues to send payment reminders for already-paid invoices, and understates its cash position in aged debtor reports.

## The Question
> Unallocated cash exists when a transaction has no valid OrderID linkage — the payment arrived but was not matched to a specific invoice or order. SQL identifies these orphan transactions and quantifies the unmatched balance.
> **"Identify all Transactions with no matching OrderID in the Orders table, group them by currency and time period, and calculate the total unallocated cash balance."**

## Table Reference
**Transactions** — `schemas/05_Transactions.csv`
**Orders** — `schemas/02_Orders.csv`

## Solution Query

```sql
-- Step 1: Transactions with no parent order (unallocated payments)
SELECT
    t.TransactionID,
    t.OrderID,
    t.Amount,
    t.Currency,
    t.TransactionDate,
    t.BankAccountNumber,
    t.CorrelationID,
    DATEDIFF(DAY, t.TransactionDate, GETDATE()) AS AgeDays,
    CASE
        WHEN t.OrderID IS NULL
             THEN 'NULL ORDER: No reference provided'
        ELSE 'INVALID ORDER: OrderID ' + CAST(t.OrderID AS VARCHAR) + ' does not exist'
    END AS UnallocatedReason
FROM Transactions t
WHERE NOT EXISTS (
    SELECT 1 FROM Orders o WHERE o.OrderID = t.OrderID
)
ORDER BY t.Amount DESC;

-- Step 2: Unallocated balance by currency and age band
SELECT
    t.Currency,
    CASE
        WHEN DATEDIFF(DAY, t.TransactionDate, GETDATE()) <= 7   THEN '0-7 days'
        WHEN DATEDIFF(DAY, t.TransactionDate, GETDATE()) <= 30  THEN '8-30 days'
        WHEN DATEDIFF(DAY, t.TransactionDate, GETDATE()) <= 90  THEN '31-90 days'
        ELSE '90+ days'
    END AS AgeBand,
    COUNT(*)      AS UnallocatedCount,
    SUM(t.Amount) AS UnallocatedTotal,
    MIN(t.TransactionDate) AS OldestTransaction,
    MAX(t.TransactionDate) AS NewestTransaction
FROM Transactions t
WHERE NOT EXISTS (
    SELECT 1 FROM Orders o WHERE o.OrderID = t.OrderID
)
GROUP BY
    t.Currency,
    CASE
        WHEN DATEDIFF(DAY, t.TransactionDate, GETDATE()) <= 7   THEN '0-7 days'
        WHEN DATEDIFF(DAY, t.TransactionDate, GETDATE()) <= 30  THEN '8-30 days'
        WHEN DATEDIFF(DAY, t.TransactionDate, GETDATE()) <= 90  THEN '31-90 days'
        ELSE '90+ days'
    END
ORDER BY t.Currency, AgeBand;

-- Step 3: Potential matches — unallocated payments close to an existing order amount
SELECT
    t.TransactionID,
    t.Amount AS UnallocatedAmount,
    t.Currency,
    t.TransactionDate,
    o.OrderID AS PotentialMatchOrder,
    o.TotalAmount AS OrderAmount,
    o.CustomerID,
    o.OrderDate,
    ABS(t.Amount - o.TotalAmount) AS AmountDifference,
    DATEDIFF(DAY, o.OrderDate, t.TransactionDate) AS DaysAfterOrder,
    CASE
        WHEN t.Amount = o.TotalAmount        THEN 'EXACT AMOUNT MATCH'
        WHEN ABS(t.Amount - o.TotalAmount) < o.TotalAmount * 0.02
                                             THEN 'NEAR MATCH: Within 2%'
        ELSE 'PARTIAL MATCH: Investigate'
    END AS MatchConfidence
FROM Transactions t
JOIN Orders o
    ON  NOT EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM Orders ox WHERE ox.OrderID = t.OrderID)
    AND t.Amount BETWEEN o.TotalAmount * 0.95 AND o.TotalAmount * 1.05
    AND t.Currency = 'GBP'
    AND DATEDIFF(DAY, o.OrderDate, t.TransactionDate) BETWEEN 0 AND 60
    AND NOT EXISTS (
        SELECT 1 FROM Transactions t2
        WHERE t2.OrderID = o.OrderID
    )  -- Only suggest orders that also have no transaction yet
ORDER BY ABS(t.Amount - o.TotalAmount);

-- Step 4: Summary — total unallocated vs total outstanding orders
SELECT
    'Total unallocated receipts'       AS Category,
    COUNT(*)                           AS Count,
    SUM(t.Amount)                      AS TotalAmount
FROM Transactions t
WHERE NOT EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM Orders o WHERE o.OrderID = t.OrderID)
  AND t.Amount > 0

UNION ALL

SELECT
    'Total orders awaiting payment',
    COUNT(*),
    SUM(o.TotalAmount)
FROM Orders o
WHERE NOT EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM Transactions t WHERE t.OrderID = o.OrderID)
  AND o.Status NOT IN ('Pending', 'Cancelled');
```

## Explanation

**NOT EXISTS for unallocated detection**
`NOT EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM Orders WHERE OrderID = t.OrderID)` catches two cases: transactions with `OrderID IS NULL` (no reference given) and transactions with an OrderID that doesn't match any order (invalid reference). A LEFT JOIN alternative (`WHERE o.OrderID IS NULL`) works but NOT EXISTS is typically more efficient.

**Age-band analysis for prioritisation**
Unallocated cash aged over 90 days is the hardest to resolve — the payer may have moved on, the invoice may have been disputed, or the reference may be lost. Age bands in Step 2 direct the AR team's effort: new unallocated items first (easiest to match) then escalate aged items.

**Fuzzy matching for likely allocations**
Step 3 finds orders with no transaction whose amount is within 5% of an unallocated payment and whose date falls 0–60 days before the payment. This surfaces probable matches that the AR team can confirm — the "near match" pattern is particularly useful for catching partial payments and minor invoice amendments.

**Two-sided view of the problem**
Step 4 shows both sides: unallocated cash (money received, not matched to an invoice) and orders awaiting payment (invoices issued, not yet settled). A finance team reconciling both lists against the bank statement can identify where the unallocated receipts should be applied.

## Severity
**Medium** — Unallocated cash overstates the suspense account and understates AR collections, causing incorrect aged debtor reports and chasing of already-paid invoices. Aged items may be written off incorrectly or trigger unnecessary credit control actions. The risk is primarily operational; the financial impact becomes material if unallocated balances grow large.
