# G08-T08 — Reconciliation | Cutoff Error (Wrong Period)

## ID
G08-T08

## Group
8 — Reconciliation

## Type
Cutoff Error (Wrong Period) — Transactions Recorded in the Wrong Accounting Period

## Live Scenario
A retail company closes its books on the last calendar day of each month. An overnight batch process posts sales transactions from the point-of-sale system into the accounting system. On 31 January, the batch runs at 23:58 and processes sales from 09:00–23:00. Forty-seven transactions from 23:01–23:58 are captured by the next day's batch and posted with a timestamp of 1 February — even though the revenue belongs to January. The January period closes with revenue understated by £8,340, and February opens overstated by the same amount. The auditor requires a manual cutoff adjustment journal for £8,340.

## The Question
> Cutoff errors occur when transactions are recorded in a period that does not match the economic activity date. SQL detects cutoff errors by comparing the transaction's economic date (when the activity occurred) to the posting date (when it was recorded) and flagging mismatches that cross a period boundary.
> **"Identify Transactions where the TransactionDate falls outside the period of the related Order's OrderDate (different year-month), and quantify the cutoff exposure by period."**

## Table Reference
**Transactions** — `schemas/05_Transactions.csv`
**Orders** — `schemas/02_Orders.csv`

## Solution Query

```sql
-- Step 1: Identify transactions posted in a different period than their order
SELECT
    t.TransactionID,
    t.OrderID,
    o.OrderDate,
    t.TransactionDate,
    YEAR(o.OrderDate)        AS OrderYear,
    MONTH(o.OrderDate)       AS OrderMonth,
    YEAR(t.TransactionDate)  AS TxYear,
    MONTH(t.TransactionDate) AS TxMonth,
    t.Amount,
    DATEDIFF(DAY, o.OrderDate, t.TransactionDate) AS DayLag,
    CASE
        WHEN YEAR(o.OrderDate) = YEAR(t.TransactionDate)
         AND MONTH(o.OrderDate) = MONTH(t.TransactionDate)
             THEN 'Same period — no cutoff issue'
        WHEN t.TransactionDate < o.OrderDate
             THEN 'EARLY POSTING: Transaction before order date — impossible'
        WHEN DATEDIFF(DAY, o.OrderDate, t.TransactionDate) <= 5
             THEN 'MINOR CUTOFF: Posted within 5 days — likely timing only'
        ELSE 'CUTOFF ERROR: Transaction in different period to order ('
             + CAST(DATEDIFF(DAY, o.OrderDate, t.TransactionDate) AS VARCHAR)
             + ' days lag)'
    END AS CutoffFlag
FROM Transactions t
JOIN Orders o ON t.OrderID = o.OrderID
WHERE NOT (YEAR(o.OrderDate) = YEAR(t.TransactionDate)
       AND MONTH(o.OrderDate) = MONTH(t.TransactionDate))
ORDER BY ABS(DATEDIFF(DAY, o.OrderDate, t.TransactionDate)) DESC;

-- Step 2: Period-level cutoff exposure — how much revenue is mis-posted?
WITH CutoffItems AS (
    SELECT
        YEAR(o.OrderDate)        AS EconomicYear,
        MONTH(o.OrderDate)       AS EconomicMonth,
        YEAR(t.TransactionDate)  AS PostedYear,
        MONTH(t.TransactionDate) AS PostedMonth,
        t.Amount,
        t.TransactionID
    FROM Transactions t
    JOIN Orders o ON t.OrderID = o.OrderID
    WHERE NOT (YEAR(o.OrderDate) = YEAR(t.TransactionDate)
           AND MONTH(o.OrderDate) = MONTH(t.TransactionDate))
)
SELECT
    EconomicYear,
    EconomicMonth,
    PostedYear,
    PostedMonth,
    COUNT(*)      AS AffectedTransactions,
    SUM(Amount)   AS MispostedAmount,
    CASE
        WHEN PostedYear > EconomicYear
          OR (PostedYear = EconomicYear AND PostedMonth > EconomicMonth)
             THEN 'UNDER-ACCRUAL: Revenue recognised too late — adjust prior period'
        ELSE 'OVER-ACCRUAL: Revenue recognised too early — reverse current period'
    END AS CutoffDirection
FROM CutoffItems
GROUP BY EconomicYear, EconomicMonth, PostedYear, PostedMonth
ORDER BY EconomicYear, EconomicMonth;

-- Step 3: Month-end transactions at highest cutoff risk (last 3 days of each month)
SELECT
    t.TransactionID,
    t.OrderID,
    o.OrderDate,
    t.TransactionDate,
    t.Amount,
    DAY(t.TransactionDate)                                AS DayOfMonth,
    DAY(EOMONTH(t.TransactionDate))                       AS DaysInMonth,
    DAY(EOMONTH(t.TransactionDate)) - DAY(t.TransactionDate) AS DaysFromMonthEnd,
    'HIGH RISK: Near month-end — verify period allocation' AS CutoffRisk
FROM Transactions t
JOIN Orders o ON t.OrderID = o.OrderID
WHERE DAY(EOMONTH(t.TransactionDate)) - DAY(t.TransactionDate) <= 2
ORDER BY t.TransactionDate DESC;

-- Step 4: Year-end cutoff — highest-impact cutoff errors (cross-year)
SELECT
    t.TransactionID,
    t.OrderID,
    o.OrderDate,
    t.TransactionDate,
    t.Amount,
    YEAR(o.OrderDate) AS EconomicYear,
    YEAR(t.TransactionDate) AS PostedYear,
    'YEAR-END CUTOFF: Cross-year posting requires audit journal' AS Severity
FROM Transactions t
JOIN Orders o ON t.OrderID = o.OrderID
WHERE YEAR(o.OrderDate) <> YEAR(t.TransactionDate)
ORDER BY t.Amount DESC;
```

## Explanation

**Economic date vs posting date**
The cutoff principle requires revenue and expense to be recognised in the period of the economic activity, not the period of the system entry. The `OrderDate` represents when the economic activity occurred; `TransactionDate` represents when the system recorded the payment. A mismatch crossing a period boundary is a cutoff error.

**EOMONTH for month-end proximity**
`DAY(EOMONTH(TransactionDate)) - DAY(TransactionDate)` counts days remaining in the month. Transactions within 2 days of month-end are the highest risk because batch processes often have the longest lag at period close, and revenue near the boundary is most likely to cross over.

**Direction matters for adjustment**
Step 2 identifies whether revenue is posted too early (over-accrual, reverse in current period) or too late (under-accrual, book in prior period via adjusting journal). The adjustment direction determines whether the correction increases or decreases the current period's P&L.

**Year-end cutoff as a separate priority**
Step 4 elevates cross-year cutoffs because they affect the annual accounts — the primary basis for tax filings, dividend decisions, and regulatory reporting. A £8,340 cutoff error in January vs February is a monthly issue; the same error in December vs January requires a restatement of the annual accounts.

## Severity
**High** — Cutoff errors violate the accrual basis of accounting (IAS 1, ASC 606). They shift revenue between periods, distorting quarterly and annual performance metrics. External auditors specifically test for cutoff errors at year-end as a standard substantive procedure.
