# G02-T03 — Uniqueness | Duplicate Natural / Business Key

## ID
G02-T03

## Group
2 — Uniqueness / Duplicates

## Type
Duplicate Natural / Business Key

## Live Scenario
A hospital system uses NHS number as the natural key for patients. The surrogate PatientID is unique — the PK constraint is intact. But two patients share the same NHS number: data entry error at registration. Every report that groups by NHS number now combines two different people's medical records. A clinician reviewing patient history sees appointments, prescriptions, and diagnoses from both patients merged into one view.

## The Question
> Your surrogate primary key is unique but the real-world business identifier — the one users actually search by — has duplicates.
> **"Find all customers who share the same Email address (the natural business key). Email must be unique per customer. Return the duplicates, how many exist per email, and flag which record appears to be the original vs the duplicate."**

## Table Reference
**Customers** — `schemas/01_Customers.csv`

## Solution Query

```sql
-- Step 1: Which email addresses appear more than once?
SELECT
    Email,
    COUNT(*) AS CustomerCount,
    MIN(CustomerID) AS EarliestCustomerID,
    MAX(CustomerID) AS LatestCustomerID,
    MIN(CreatedDate) AS FirstCreated,
    MAX(CreatedDate) AS LastCreated
FROM Customers
WHERE Email IS NOT NULL
GROUP BY Email
HAVING COUNT(*) > 1
ORDER BY CustomerCount DESC;

-- Step 2: Full detail of duplicated email rows with suggested action
WITH EmailDuplicates AS (
    SELECT *,
        ROW_NUMBER() OVER (
            PARTITION BY Email
            ORDER BY CustomerID ASC   -- earliest CustomerID = original
        ) AS EmailRank,
        COUNT(*) OVER (PARTITION BY Email) AS TotalSharing
    FROM Customers
    WHERE Email IS NOT NULL
)
SELECT
    CustomerID,
    FirstName + ' ' + LastName AS CustomerName,
    Email,
    Phone,
    CreatedDate,
    EmailRank,
    TotalSharing,
    CASE
        WHEN EmailRank = 1 THEN 'Original — KEEP'
        ELSE                    'Duplicate #' + CAST(EmailRank AS VARCHAR) + ' — REVIEW'
    END AS RecommendedAction
FROM EmailDuplicates
WHERE TotalSharing > 1
ORDER BY Email, EmailRank;

-- Step 3: Also check Phone as a second natural key
SELECT
    Phone,
    COUNT(*) AS CustomerCount,
    STRING_AGG(CAST(CustomerID AS VARCHAR), ', ') AS CustomerIDs
FROM Customers
WHERE Phone IS NOT NULL
  AND LTRIM(RTRIM(Phone)) <> ''
GROUP BY Phone
HAVING COUNT(*) > 1
ORDER BY CustomerCount DESC;
```

## Expected Output

**Step 1:**
| Email | CustomerCount | EarliestCustomerID | FirstCreated |
|---|---|---|---|
| james.carter@email.com | 2 | 1 | 2022-03-15 |

**Step 2:**
| CustomerID | CustomerName | Email | EmailRank | RecommendedAction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | James Carter | james.carter@email.com | 1 | Original — KEEP |
| 11 | James Carter | james.carter@email.com | 2 | Duplicate #2 — REVIEW |

## Explanation

**Natural key vs surrogate key**
A surrogate key (CustomerID, an auto-increment integer) is generated by the system and guarantees row uniqueness. A natural key (Email) is a real-world identifier that is supposed to uniquely identify an entity in the business domain. Surrogate key uniqueness does NOT guarantee natural key uniqueness — you must check both separately.

**COUNT(*) OVER (PARTITION BY Email) — window function for TotalSharing**
Adding `COUNT(*) OVER (PARTITION BY Email)` to the CTE gives every row in a duplicate group the total count of how many customers share that email. This avoids a separate subquery and lets you filter (`WHERE TotalSharing > 1`) and display the count in the same step.

**STRING_AGG for seeing all affected IDs**
In Step 3, `STRING_AGG(CAST(CustomerID AS VARCHAR), ', ')` concatenates all CustomerIDs that share the same phone number into one readable string — useful for a quick audit report where you need to see which records are involved without expanding every row.

**Which duplicate to keep?**
The convention of keeping the lowest CustomerID (earliest created) is a starting point — not a rule. In practice, the decision depends on which record has more complete data, which was most recently active, or which system of record is authoritative. The validation query surfaces the candidates; a human or a business rule makes the final call.

## Severity
**Critical** — Duplicate natural keys cause joined reports to merge unrelated entities, break authentication systems (two users with the same login email), and corrupt any process that uses the natural key as a lookup.
