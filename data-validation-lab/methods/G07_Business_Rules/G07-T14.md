# G07-T14 — Business Rules | Round-Number Bias Detection

## ID
G07-T14

## Group
7 — Business Rules

## Type
Round-Number Bias Detection — Suspicious Clustering at Round Amounts

## Live Scenario
An expense management system allows employees to submit expenses up to £250 without receipts. A data scientist runs a distribution analysis on expense claims and finds a suspicious clustering: 847 claims are for exactly £250.00 and another 312 are for £249.99 — far more than the Benford's Law distribution would predict. Investigation reveals that 23 employees have been systematically splitting larger expenses into multiple claims at or just below the £250 approval threshold to avoid the receipt requirement. The pattern — known as "structuring" in financial regulation — is a compliance violation. In financial crime contexts, the same pattern used to avoid currency transaction reporting thresholds (e.g., $10,000 in the US) is a federal offence.

## The Question
> Round-number clustering and just-below-threshold structuring are fraud indicators. Legitimate amounts are distributed across all values; fraudulent manipulation creates statistically improbable peaks at round numbers or just under control thresholds.
> **"Find all Transactions where the Amount is suspiciously round (ends in 000, or is an exact multiple of 100), and detect structuring patterns where amounts cluster just below control thresholds."**

## Table Reference
**Transactions** — `schemas/05_Transactions.csv`
**Orders** — `schemas/02_Orders.csv`

## Solution Query

```sql
-- Step 1: Round-number bias detection in transaction amounts
SELECT
    Amount,
    COUNT(*) AS TxCount,
    SUM(Amount) AS TotalValue,
    ROUND(100.0 * COUNT(*) / SUM(COUNT(*)) OVER (), 2) AS PctOfAllTx,
    CASE
        WHEN Amount = ROUND(Amount, -3)         THEN 'ROUND THOUSANDS: Exactly X,000'
        WHEN Amount = ROUND(Amount, -2)         THEN 'ROUND HUNDREDS: Exactly X00'
        WHEN Amount = ROUND(Amount, -1)         THEN 'ROUND TENS: Exactly X0'
        WHEN Amount = FLOOR(Amount)             THEN 'WHOLE NUMBER: No pence component'
        ELSE 'Has pence — normal'
    END AS RoundnessCategory
FROM Transactions
WHERE Amount > 0
GROUP BY Amount
ORDER BY TxCount DESC;

-- Step 2: Just-below-threshold structuring detection
-- Threshold: £1000 (common approval threshold)
DECLARE @Threshold1 DECIMAL(10,2) = 1000.00;
DECLARE @Threshold2 DECIMAL(10,2) = 500.00;
DECLARE @Threshold3 DECIMAL(10,2) = 250.00;
DECLARE @TolerancePct DECIMAL(5,2) = 0.01;  -- within 1% of threshold

SELECT
    Amount,
    COUNT(*) AS TxCount,
    CASE
        WHEN Amount BETWEEN @Threshold1 * (1 - @TolerancePct) AND @Threshold1
             THEN 'STRUCTURING FLAG: Just below £1000 threshold'
        WHEN Amount BETWEEN @Threshold2 * (1 - @TolerancePct) AND @Threshold2
             THEN 'STRUCTURING FLAG: Just below £500 threshold'
        WHEN Amount BETWEEN @Threshold3 * (1 - @TolerancePct) AND @Threshold3
             THEN 'STRUCTURING FLAG: Just below £250 threshold'
        ELSE 'No threshold concern'
    END AS StructuringFlag
FROM Transactions
WHERE Amount > 0
  AND (
    Amount BETWEEN @Threshold1 * (1 - @TolerancePct) AND @Threshold1
    OR Amount BETWEEN @Threshold2 * (1 - @TolerancePct) AND @Threshold2
    OR Amount BETWEEN @Threshold3 * (1 - @TolerancePct) AND @Threshold3
  )
GROUP BY Amount
ORDER BY TxCount DESC;

-- Step 3: Individual-level structuring — same customer with multiple just-below-threshold transactions
WITH JustBelowThreshold AS (
    SELECT
        t.OrderID,
        t.Amount,
        o.CustomerID,
        t.TransactionDate
    FROM Transactions t
    JOIN Orders o ON t.OrderID = o.OrderID
    WHERE t.Amount BETWEEN 990 AND 1000   -- just below £1000
       OR t.Amount BETWEEN 495 AND 500    -- just below £500
       OR t.Amount BETWEEN 248 AND 250    -- just below £250
)
SELECT
    CustomerID,
    COUNT(*) AS StructuredTxCount,
    SUM(Amount) AS TotalStructuredAmount,
    MIN(TransactionDate) AS FirstStructuredTx,
    MAX(TransactionDate) AS LastStructuredTx,
    'ALERT: Multiple just-below-threshold transactions from same customer' AS Flag
FROM JustBelowThreshold
GROUP BY CustomerID
HAVING COUNT(*) >= 3
ORDER BY TotalStructuredAmount DESC;

-- Step 4: Benford's Law first-digit analysis
-- In a natural distribution, digit 1 leads ~30% of amounts, digit 9 leads ~5%
-- Deviation from Benford's Law indicates manipulation
SELECT
    FirstDigit,
    COUNT(*) AS ActualCount,
    ROUND(100.0 * COUNT(*) / SUM(COUNT(*)) OVER (), 1) AS ActualPct,
    CASE FirstDigit
        WHEN '1' THEN 30.1
        WHEN '2' THEN 17.6
        WHEN '3' THEN 12.5
        WHEN '4' THEN 9.7
        WHEN '5' THEN 7.9
        WHEN '6' THEN 6.7
        WHEN '7' THEN 5.8
        WHEN '8' THEN 5.1
        WHEN '9' THEN 4.6
    END AS BenfordExpectedPct,
    ROUND(100.0 * COUNT(*) / SUM(COUNT(*)) OVER (), 1) -
    CASE FirstDigit
        WHEN '1' THEN 30.1 WHEN '2' THEN 17.6 WHEN '3' THEN 12.5
        WHEN '4' THEN 9.7  WHEN '5' THEN 7.9  WHEN '6' THEN 6.7
        WHEN '7' THEN 5.8  WHEN '8' THEN 5.1  WHEN '9' THEN 4.6
    END AS DeviationFromBenford
FROM (
    SELECT LEFT(CAST(FLOOR(ABS(Amount)) AS VARCHAR), 1) AS FirstDigit
    FROM Transactions
    WHERE Amount > 0 AND Amount >= 1
) digits
WHERE FirstDigit BETWEEN '1' AND '9'
GROUP BY FirstDigit
ORDER BY FirstDigit;

-- Step 5: Round-number concentration summary
SELECT
    'Exactly round hundreds' AS Category,
    COUNT(*) AS TxCount,
    SUM(Amount) AS TotalValue
FROM Transactions WHERE Amount > 0 AND Amount = ROUND(Amount, -2)
UNION ALL
SELECT 'Just below £1000', COUNT(*), SUM(Amount)
FROM Transactions WHERE Amount BETWEEN 990 AND 999.99
UNION ALL
SELECT 'Exactly £250 or £500 or £1000', COUNT(*), SUM(Amount)
FROM Transactions WHERE Amount IN (250, 500, 1000)
ORDER BY TxCount DESC;
```

| 999.00 | 23 | STRUCTURING FLAG: Just below £1000 threshold |

**Step 4 — Benford's Law:**
| FirstDigit | ActualPct | BenfordExpectedPct | DeviationFromBenford |

## Explanation

**Round number clustering**
In organic transaction data, amounts have a roughly uniform pence distribution. A spike at exactly £250.00, £500.00, or £1,000.00 indicates amounts were entered at round numbers rather than derived from actual costs. This is the first indicator of fabricated or manipulated data.

**Just-below-threshold structuring**
Clustering just below a control threshold (£249.99 when the threshold is £250.00) is a specific fraud pattern — the actor knows the threshold and deliberately stays just below it. The 1% tolerance band (£247.50–£250.00) in Step 2 catches this pattern.

**Benford's Law**
In naturally occurring numeric datasets, leading digit 1 appears ~30% of the time, digit 9 only ~4.6% (Benford's distribution). Financial fraud often produces digit 9 over-representation (amounts chosen to be just below round thresholds) and digit 5 or 1 over-representation (fabricated amounts starting with those digits). Significant deviation from expected Benford distribution is a red flag for forensic accountants.

**ROUND(amount, -2)**
`ROUND(1800, -2) = 1800`, `ROUND(1812, -2) = 1800`. So `Amount = ROUND(Amount, -2)` is true only when the last two digits are both zero — the amount is an exact multiple of 100. Using negative precision in ROUND tests for roundness at different magnitudes.

## Severity
**High** — Round-number bias and structuring are indicators of fraudulent expense manipulation, money laundering (structuring below reporting thresholds is a criminal offence), and data fabrication. Confirmed structuring must be reported as a Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) to the National Crime Agency under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002.
