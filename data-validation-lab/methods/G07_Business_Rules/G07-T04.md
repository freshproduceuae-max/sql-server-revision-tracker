# G07-T04 — Business Rules | Customer Age Eligibility

## ID
G07-T04

## Group
7 — Business Rules

## Type
Customer Age Eligibility — Customer Does Not Meet Age Requirement for Product or Service

## Live Scenario
An online retailer sells age-restricted products (alcohol, tobacco, gambling top-ups, over-18 entertainment). The age verification system checks the customer's stated `DateOfBirth` at registration. A bug in a European market deployment ignores the day and month, comparing only the year: a customer born on 31 December 2006 is considered to have turned 18 on 1 January 2024 (correct calendar year, wrong day) and is allowed to purchase alcohol from 1 January 2024 even though they are 18 only from 31 December 2024. The error allows 11 months of premature age-verified purchases for customers born late in their eligibility year.

## The Question
> Age eligibility rules require exact date comparison — year, month, and day all matter. Checking only the birth year produces a systematic off-by-up-to-364-days error for customers born in the second half of the year. SQL enforces the exact birthday threshold using DATEDIFF or DATEADD.
> **"Find all Customers whose DateOfBirth indicates they are under 18 years old as of today, and flag orders they have placed — demonstrating correct age calculation vs year-only comparison."**

## Table Reference
**Customers** — `schemas/01_Customers.csv`
**Orders** — `schemas/02_Orders.csv`

## Solution Query

```sql
-- Step 1: Correct age calculation using DATEDIFF(YEAR) with birthday adjustment
-- DATEDIFF(YEAR) counts year transitions, not exact age
-- Adjustment: subtract 1 if birthday hasn't occurred yet this year
SELECT
    CustomerID,
    FirstName,
    LastName,
    DateOfBirth,
    GETDATE() AS CheckDate,
    -- Correct age: DATEDIFF gives years elapsed; subtract 1 if birthday not yet reached this year
    DATEDIFF(YEAR, DateOfBirth, GETDATE())
    - CASE
        WHEN MONTH(DateOfBirth) > MONTH(GETDATE()) THEN 1
        WHEN MONTH(DateOfBirth) = MONTH(GETDATE()) AND DAY(DateOfBirth) > DAY(GETDATE()) THEN 1
        ELSE 0
      END AS ExactAge,
    -- Incorrect year-only calculation (demonstrates the bug)
    YEAR(GETDATE()) - YEAR(DateOfBirth) AS YearOnlyAge,
    CASE
        WHEN DATEDIFF(YEAR, DateOfBirth, GETDATE())
             - CASE
                 WHEN MONTH(DateOfBirth) > MONTH(GETDATE()) THEN 1
                 WHEN MONTH(DateOfBirth) = MONTH(GETDATE()) AND DAY(DateOfBirth) > DAY(GETDATE()) THEN 1
                 ELSE 0
               END < 18
             THEN 'UNDER 18: Not eligible for age-restricted products'
        ELSE 'Age-verified: 18 or over'
    END AS AgeEligibility
FROM Customers
WHERE DateOfBirth IS NOT NULL
ORDER BY DateOfBirth DESC;

-- Step 2: Customers who are flagged as under-18 but have placed orders
SELECT
    c.CustomerID,
    c.FirstName,
    c.LastName,
    c.DateOfBirth,
    DATEDIFF(YEAR, c.DateOfBirth, GETDATE())
    - CASE
        WHEN MONTH(c.DateOfBirth) > MONTH(GETDATE()) THEN 1
        WHEN MONTH(c.DateOfBirth) = MONTH(GETDATE()) AND DAY(c.DateOfBirth) > DAY(GETDATE()) THEN 1
        ELSE 0
      END AS ExactAge,
    COUNT(o.OrderID) AS OrderCount,
    SUM(o.TotalAmount) AS TotalSpend,
    MIN(o.OrderDate) AS FirstOrderDate
FROM Customers c
JOIN Orders o ON c.CustomerID = o.CustomerID
WHERE c.DateOfBirth IS NOT NULL
  AND DATEDIFF(YEAR, c.DateOfBirth, GETDATE())
      - CASE
          WHEN MONTH(c.DateOfBirth) > MONTH(GETDATE()) THEN 1
          WHEN MONTH(c.DateOfBirth) = MONTH(GETDATE()) AND DAY(c.DateOfBirth) > DAY(GETDATE()) THEN 1
          ELSE 0
        END < 18
GROUP BY c.CustomerID, c.FirstName, c.LastName, c.DateOfBirth
ORDER BY ExactAge;

-- Step 3: Year-only vs exact-age discrepancy — who would be wrongly approved?
SELECT
    CustomerID,
    DateOfBirth,
    YEAR(GETDATE()) - YEAR(DateOfBirth) AS YearOnlyAge,
    DATEDIFF(YEAR, DateOfBirth, GETDATE())
    - CASE
        WHEN MONTH(DateOfBirth) > MONTH(GETDATE()) THEN 1
        WHEN MONTH(DateOfBirth) = MONTH(GETDATE()) AND DAY(DateOfBirth) > DAY(GETDATE()) THEN 1
        ELSE 0
      END AS ExactAge,
    CASE
        WHEN YEAR(GETDATE()) - YEAR(DateOfBirth) >= 18
         AND DATEDIFF(YEAR, DateOfBirth, GETDATE())
             - CASE
                 WHEN MONTH(DateOfBirth) > MONTH(GETDATE()) THEN 1
                 WHEN MONTH(DateOfBirth) = MONTH(GETDATE()) AND DAY(DateOfBirth) > DAY(GETDATE()) THEN 1
                 ELSE 0
               END < 18
             THEN 'FALSE APPROVAL: Year-only says 18+, exact age says under 18'
        ELSE 'Both methods agree'
    END AS MethodDiscrepancy
FROM Customers
WHERE DateOfBirth IS NOT NULL
  AND YEAR(GETDATE()) - YEAR(DateOfBirth) >= 18
  AND DATEDIFF(YEAR, DateOfBirth, GETDATE())
      - CASE
          WHEN MONTH(DateOfBirth) > MONTH(GETDATE()) THEN 1
          WHEN MONTH(DateOfBirth) = MONTH(GETDATE()) AND DAY(DateOfBirth) > DAY(GETDATE()) THEN 1
          ELSE 0
        END < 18;

-- Step 4: Missing DateOfBirth — cannot verify age
SELECT
    CustomerID,
    FirstName,
    LastName,
    'RISK: No DateOfBirth — age eligibility cannot be verified' AS Issue
FROM Customers
WHERE DateOfBirth IS NULL;

-- Step 5: Age distribution summary
SELECT
    CASE
        WHEN ExactAge < 18  THEN 'Under 18'
        WHEN ExactAge < 25  THEN '18–24'
        WHEN ExactAge < 35  THEN '25–34'
        WHEN ExactAge < 50  THEN '35–49'
        WHEN ExactAge < 65  THEN '50–64'
        ELSE '65+'
    END AS AgeBand,
    COUNT(*) AS CustomerCount
FROM (
    SELECT
        DATEDIFF(YEAR, DateOfBirth, GETDATE())
        - CASE
            WHEN MONTH(DateOfBirth) > MONTH(GETDATE()) THEN 1
            WHEN MONTH(DateOfBirth) = MONTH(GETDATE()) AND DAY(DateOfBirth) > DAY(GETDATE()) THEN 1
            ELSE 0
          END AS ExactAge
    FROM Customers
    WHERE DateOfBirth IS NOT NULL
) ages
GROUP BY
    CASE
        WHEN ExactAge < 18  THEN 'Under 18'
        WHEN ExactAge < 25  THEN '18–24'
        WHEN ExactAge < 35  THEN '25–34'
        WHEN ExactAge < 50  THEN '35–49'
        WHEN ExactAge < 65  THEN '50–64'
        ELSE '65+'
    END
ORDER BY MIN(ExactAge);
```

## Explanation

**Why DATEDIFF(YEAR, ...) is not exact age**
`DATEDIFF(YEAR, '2006-12-31', '2024-07-01')` returns 18 — it counts year boundaries crossed, not complete years of life. A person born 31 December 2006 has not yet had their 18th birthday on 1 July 2024 — they are still 17. The birthday-adjustment correction (`CASE WHEN MONTH(dob) > MONTH(today) OR ...`) fixes this.

**The correct exact-age formula**
The standard SQL Server exact-age pattern:
```sql
DATEDIFF(YEAR, dob, today)
- CASE WHEN MONTH(dob) > MONTH(today) OR
            (MONTH(dob) = MONTH(today) AND DAY(dob) > DAY(today))
       THEN 1 ELSE 0 END
```
This gives the number of completed years of life — the legal definition of age in UK law.

**Regulatory consequence**
Selling age-restricted products to under-18s is a criminal offence in the UK (Licensing Act 2003, Children and Young Persons Acts). An age verification bug causing systematic false approvals exposes the business to prosecution, fines, and licence revocation.

**Missing DateOfBirth as a separate risk**
A customer with no DateOfBirth cannot have their age verified. Step 4 flags these records as a risk — they should either be blocked from age-restricted purchases or required to complete age verification before checkout.

## Severity
**Critical** — Age eligibility violations involving age-restricted goods (alcohol, tobacco, gambling) are criminal offences with direct legal liability. A systematic bug affecting hundreds or thousands of transactions compounds the severity and demonstrates a failed compliance control.
