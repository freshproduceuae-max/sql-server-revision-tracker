# G07-T02 — Business Rules | Credit Limit Exceeded

## ID
G07-T02

## Group
7 — Business Rules

## Type
Credit Limit Exceeded — Total Exposure Surpasses Approved Credit Ceiling

## Live Scenario
A B2B wholesaler extends credit to trade customers with individually approved credit limits stored in the `Customers` table. The order management system checks credit at order placement but does not re-check when partial deliveries are created — each partial delivery creates a new `Orders` record. A customer with a £50,000 credit limit places one large order that is split into five partial deliveries of £12,000 each. The first four pass the credit check (each individual order is £12,000 < £50,000), but the total outstanding balance after all four is £48,000. The fifth delivery of £12,000 brings the outstanding balance to £60,000 — £10,000 over the approved limit. The order management system approves it because it checks per-order rather than cumulative exposure.

## The Question
> Credit limit validation must compare the customer's total outstanding exposure (sum of all unpaid/pending orders) against their approved limit — not the value of any single order. A per-transaction check is insufficient; the cumulative check is the correct business rule.
> **"Find all Customers where the sum of their outstanding Orders (Status not in Delivered/Cancelled) exceeds their approved credit limit."**

## Table Reference
**Customers** — `schemas/01_Customers.csv`
**Orders** — `schemas/02_Orders.csv`

## Solution Query

```sql
-- Step 1: Customer credit exposure vs limit
-- Assuming Customers table has a CreditLimit column
WITH CustomerExposure AS (
    SELECT
        o.CustomerID,
        SUM(o.TotalAmount) AS OutstandingBalance,
        COUNT(*) AS OutstandingOrders
    FROM Orders o
    WHERE o.Status NOT IN ('Delivered', 'Cancelled', 'Refunded')
    GROUP BY o.CustomerID
)
SELECT
    c.CustomerID,
    c.FirstName + ' ' + c.LastName AS CustomerName,
    c.Country,
    -- CreditLimit not in schema; simulated as a derived value for demonstration
    CAST(10000 AS DECIMAL(10,2)) AS CreditLimit,  -- replace with c.CreditLimit in real table
    ISNULL(ce.OutstandingBalance, 0) AS OutstandingBalance,
    ISNULL(ce.OutstandingOrders, 0) AS OutstandingOrders,
    ISNULL(ce.OutstandingBalance, 0) - 10000 AS OverLimitAmount,
    CASE
        WHEN ISNULL(ce.OutstandingBalance, 0) > 10000 * 1.00
             THEN 'VIOLATION: Over credit limit'
        WHEN ISNULL(ce.OutstandingBalance, 0) > 10000 * 0.90
             THEN 'WARNING: >90% of credit limit utilised'
        WHEN ISNULL(ce.OutstandingBalance, 0) > 10000 * 0.75
             THEN 'ALERT: >75% of credit limit utilised'
        ELSE 'Within credit limit'
    END AS CreditStatus
FROM Customers c
LEFT JOIN CustomerExposure ce ON c.CustomerID = ce.CustomerID
ORDER BY ISNULL(ce.OutstandingBalance, 0) DESC;

-- Step 2: Would adding a new order breach the limit?
-- Simulate: if a new £3,000 order is placed by CustomerID 3
DECLARE @ProposedCustomerID INT = 3;
DECLARE @ProposedOrderAmount DECIMAL(10,2) = 3000.00;
DECLARE @CustomerCreditLimit DECIMAL(10,2) = 10000.00;

SELECT
    @ProposedCustomerID AS CustomerID,
    @ProposedOrderAmount AS ProposedOrderAmount,
    @CustomerCreditLimit AS CreditLimit,
    (SELECT ISNULL(SUM(TotalAmount), 0)
     FROM Orders
     WHERE CustomerID = @ProposedCustomerID
       AND Status NOT IN ('Delivered','Cancelled')) AS CurrentOutstanding,
    (SELECT ISNULL(SUM(TotalAmount), 0)
     FROM Orders
     WHERE CustomerID = @ProposedCustomerID
       AND Status NOT IN ('Delivered','Cancelled'))
    + @ProposedOrderAmount AS ProjectedExposure,
    CASE
        WHEN (SELECT ISNULL(SUM(TotalAmount), 0)
              FROM Orders
              WHERE CustomerID = @ProposedCustomerID
                AND Status NOT IN ('Delivered','Cancelled'))
             + @ProposedOrderAmount > @CustomerCreditLimit
             THEN 'REJECT: Would breach credit limit'
        ELSE 'APPROVE: Within credit limit'
    END AS CreditDecision;

-- Step 3: Credit utilisation distribution
SELECT
    CASE
        WHEN ISNULL(ce.OutstandingBalance, 0) = 0          THEN '0% — No outstanding orders'
        WHEN ISNULL(ce.OutstandingBalance, 0) <= 10000 * 0.50 THEN '1–50% utilised'
        WHEN ISNULL(ce.OutstandingBalance, 0) <= 10000 * 0.75 THEN '51–75% utilised'
        WHEN ISNULL(ce.OutstandingBalance, 0) <= 10000 * 0.90 THEN '76–90% utilised'
        WHEN ISNULL(ce.OutstandingBalance, 0) <= 10000 * 1.00 THEN '91–100% utilised'
        ELSE 'Over limit'
    END AS UtilisationBand,
    COUNT(*) AS CustomerCount,
    SUM(ISNULL(ce.OutstandingBalance, 0)) AS TotalExposure
FROM Customers c
LEFT JOIN (
    SELECT CustomerID, SUM(TotalAmount) AS OutstandingBalance
    FROM Orders WHERE Status NOT IN ('Delivered','Cancelled')
    GROUP BY CustomerID
) ce ON c.CustomerID = ce.CustomerID
GROUP BY
    CASE
        WHEN ISNULL(ce.OutstandingBalance, 0) = 0          THEN '0% — No outstanding orders'
        WHEN ISNULL(ce.OutstandingBalance, 0) <= 10000 * 0.50 THEN '1–50% utilised'
        WHEN ISNULL(ce.OutstandingBalance, 0) <= 10000 * 0.75 THEN '51–75% utilised'
        WHEN ISNULL(ce.OutstandingBalance, 0) <= 10000 * 0.90 THEN '76–90% utilised'
        WHEN ISNULL(ce.OutstandingBalance, 0) <= 10000 * 1.00 THEN '91–100% utilised'
        ELSE 'Over limit'
    END
ORDER BY TotalExposure DESC;
```

## Expected Output

**Step 1 — Credit exposure:**
| CustomerID | CustomerName | CreditLimit | OutstandingBalance | OverLimitAmount | CreditStatus |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | Alice Brown | 10000.00 | 12500.00 | 2500.00 | VIOLATION: Over credit limit |
| 7 | James Lee | 10000.00 | 9200.00 | -800.00 | WARNING: >90% of credit limit utilised |

**Step 3 — Utilisation distribution:**
| UtilisationBand | CustomerCount | TotalExposure |
|---|---|---|
| 0% — No outstanding orders | 5 | 0.00 |
| 1–50% utilised | 3 | 12000.00 |
| Over limit | 1 | 12500.00 |

## Explanation

**Cumulative vs per-transaction check**
The correct credit check is `SUM(outstanding orders) > CreditLimit`, not `new_order > CreditLimit`. Per-transaction checks allow a customer to circumvent the limit by placing many small orders, each individually below the limit but collectively over it.

**Outstanding balance definition**
The `OutstandingBalance` includes only orders in active states (`Pending`, `Processing`, `Shipped`). Delivered orders represent fulfilled obligations (typically invoiced and awaiting payment — depending on the system's accounting model, these may also count as exposure until paid). The definition must match the business's credit policy.

**Early warning thresholds**
Steps 1 and 3 classify exposure into bands (75%, 90%, 100%) to enable proactive credit management. Flagging a customer at 90% utilisation allows the credit team to review their limit or require upfront payment on new orders, preventing the actual breach.

**Proposed order simulation**
Step 2 demonstrates a pre-order credit check: computing `CurrentOutstanding + ProposedAmount > CreditLimit` before the order is committed. This is the pattern that the order management system's credit check should implement — checking cumulative exposure, not just the new order amount.

## Severity
**High** — Credit limit breaches expose the business to bad debt risk. In B2B wholesale, a customer over their limit who then becomes insolvent creates a write-off equal to the excess exposure. In regulated financial services, a credit limit is a risk control mandated by credit policy — breaches must be reported.
