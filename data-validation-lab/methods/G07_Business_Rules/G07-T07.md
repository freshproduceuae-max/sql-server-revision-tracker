# G07-T07 — Business Rules | Salary Band Compliance

## ID
G07-T07

## Group
7 — Business Rules

## Type
Salary Band Compliance — Employee Salary Outside Approved Grade Band

## Live Scenario
A public sector organisation has approved salary bands for each grade: Grade 3 (£28,000–£34,000), Grade 4 (£34,001–£42,000), Grade 5 (£42,001–£52,000). An HR system migration converts `HourlyRate` correctly but miscalculates annualised salary — the conversion uses 37.5 hours/week × 52 weeks = 1,950 hours/year instead of the correct 37 hours/week × 52 = 1,924 hours/year. Seventeen employees have annualised salaries that exceed their grade's ceiling by £500–£900 as a result. A pay audit by the National Audit Office identifies the discrepancy and requires retrospective justification for each overpayment — a 40-hour investigation.

## The Question
> Salary compliance validation requires converting stored values (HourlyRate) to a comparable form (annual salary) and checking against each employee's grade band. SQL performs this multi-step calculation in a single query, detecting employees paid outside their authorised band.
> **"Find all Employees where their HourlyRate, annualised to a full-time equivalent salary, falls outside the approved band for their EmploymentType."**

## Table Reference
**Employees** — `schemas/04_Employees.csv`

## Solution Query

```sql
-- Step 1: Define salary bands by employment type (would normally be in a reference table)
DECLARE @SalaryBands TABLE (
    EmploymentType VARCHAR(20),
    MinHourlyRate  DECIMAL(8,2),
    MaxHourlyRate  DECIMAL(8,2),
    BandLabel      VARCHAR(50)
);
INSERT INTO @SalaryBands VALUES
    ('Full-Time',  12.00, 50.00, 'Full-Time Band (£12–£50/hr)'),
    ('Part-Time',  12.00, 35.00, 'Part-Time Band (£12–£35/hr)'),
    ('Contract',   15.00, 100.00, 'Contract Band (£15–£100/hr)'),
    ('Intern',      8.00, 15.00, 'Intern Band (£8–£15/hr)');

-- Step 2: Check each employee against their employment type band
SELECT
    e.EmployeeID,
    e.FullName,
    e.Department,
    e.EmploymentType,
    e.HourlyRate,
    sb.MinHourlyRate,
    sb.MaxHourlyRate,
    -- Annualised FTE salary (37 hours/week × 52 weeks)
    ROUND(e.HourlyRate * 37 * 52, 2) AS AnnualisedSalary,
    CASE
        WHEN sb.EmploymentType IS NULL
             THEN 'UNKNOWN: EmploymentType not in band table'
        WHEN e.HourlyRate < sb.MinHourlyRate
             THEN 'BELOW BAND: Rate below minimum for ' + e.EmploymentType
        WHEN e.HourlyRate > sb.MaxHourlyRate
             THEN 'ABOVE BAND: Rate above maximum for ' + e.EmploymentType
        ELSE 'WITHIN BAND'
    END AS BandCompliance,
    e.HourlyRate - CASE
        WHEN e.HourlyRate < sb.MinHourlyRate THEN sb.MinHourlyRate
        WHEN e.HourlyRate > sb.MaxHourlyRate THEN sb.MaxHourlyRate
        ELSE e.HourlyRate
    END AS RateVariance
FROM Employees e
LEFT JOIN @SalaryBands sb ON e.EmploymentType = sb.EmploymentType
WHERE e.HourlyRate IS NOT NULL
  AND e.TerminationDate IS NULL  -- active employees only
  AND (e.HourlyRate < sb.MinHourlyRate OR e.HourlyRate > sb.MaxHourlyRate OR sb.EmploymentType IS NULL)
ORDER BY ABS(e.HourlyRate - ISNULL(sb.MaxHourlyRate, e.HourlyRate)) DESC;

-- Step 3: National Minimum Wage compliance (UK 2024 rates)
DECLARE @NMW_23Plus  DECIMAL(8,2) = 11.44;  -- 23 and over
DECLARE @NMW_21_22   DECIMAL(8,2) = 10.18;  -- 21–22
DECLARE @NMW_18_20   DECIMAL(8,2) =  7.49;  -- 18–20
DECLARE @NMW_Under18 DECIMAL(8,2) =  5.28;  -- Under 18

SELECT
    e.EmployeeID,
    e.FullName,
    e.HourlyRate,
    -- Age-based NMW threshold
    @NMW_23Plus AS ApplicableNMW,  -- simplified: using adult rate for all
    CASE
        WHEN e.HourlyRate < @NMW_23Plus
             THEN 'NMW VIOLATION: Rate below National Minimum Wage (£' +
                  CAST(@NMW_23Plus AS VARCHAR) + '/hr)'
        ELSE 'NMW compliant'
    END AS NMWCompliance
FROM Employees e
WHERE e.HourlyRate IS NOT NULL
  AND e.TerminationDate IS NULL
  AND e.HourlyRate < @NMW_23Plus
ORDER BY e.HourlyRate;

-- Step 4: Salary band distribution
SELECT
    EmploymentType,
    COUNT(*) AS EmployeeCount,
    MIN(HourlyRate) AS MinRate,
    MAX(HourlyRate) AS MaxRate,
    AVG(HourlyRate) AS AvgRate,
    SUM(CASE WHEN HourlyRate < sb.MinHourlyRate THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS BelowBand,
    SUM(CASE WHEN HourlyRate > sb.MaxHourlyRate THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS AboveBand,
    SUM(CASE WHEN HourlyRate BETWEEN sb.MinHourlyRate AND sb.MaxHourlyRate THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS InBand
FROM Employees e
LEFT JOIN @SalaryBands sb ON e.EmploymentType = sb.EmploymentType
WHERE e.TerminationDate IS NULL AND e.HourlyRate IS NOT NULL
GROUP BY e.EmploymentType, sb.MinHourlyRate, sb.MaxHourlyRate
ORDER BY AvgRate DESC;

-- Step 5: Pay equity check — gender/department rate comparison (simplified)
SELECT
    Department,
    AVG(HourlyRate) AS AvgRate,
    MIN(HourlyRate) AS MinRate,
    MAX(HourlyRate) AS MaxRate,
    STDEV(HourlyRate) AS StdDevRate,
    COUNT(*) AS EmployeeCount
FROM Employees
WHERE TerminationDate IS NULL
  AND HourlyRate IS NOT NULL
GROUP BY Department
ORDER BY AvgRate DESC;
```

## Explanation

**Band check pattern: LEFT JOIN + filter**
Joining employees to the band table on EmploymentType and filtering where `HourlyRate < MinHourlyRate OR HourlyRate > MaxHourlyRate` finds all out-of-band employees in a single query. The LEFT JOIN also catches employees with an EmploymentType not in the band table (`sb.EmploymentType IS NULL`).

**Annualisation for comparable reporting**
`HourlyRate × 37 hours × 52 weeks` converts hourly rate to annual FTE salary. This is the correct basis for comparing against salary grades that are expressed in annual terms. The hours assumption (37) must match the organisation's working week definition.

**National Minimum Wage as an absolute floor**
NMW is a legal minimum that overrides any internal band. An intern band starting at £8/hr is itself illegal if it is below the applicable NMW rate for the employee's age. Step 3 checks the absolute legal floor independently of the internal band check.

**STDEV for pay equity monitoring**
Step 5's standard deviation of rates per department does not directly check compliance but surfaces departments with wide pay dispersion — which may indicate pay equity issues requiring further analysis under the Equality Act 2010.

## Severity
**High** — Employees paid below the National Minimum Wage creates immediate legal liability (Employment Rights Act, HMRC enforcement). Employees paid outside their approved grade band creates HR governance failures and may require retrospective remediation under public sector pay frameworks. Both are audit findings that require formal response.
