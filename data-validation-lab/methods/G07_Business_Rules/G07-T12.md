# G07-T12 — Business Rules | Contractual Date Compliance

## ID
G07-T12

## Group
7 — Business Rules

## Type
Contractual Date Compliance — Activity Outside Contracted Service Period

## Live Scenario
A software licensing company sells annual licences with a contract start and end date. A licence auto-renewal process has a bug: it renews licences 30 days before expiry (correct), but when the renewal is processed, it uses the original `ContractStart` date rather than the renewal date — creating a 30-day gap in the new contract period. Customers whose contracts "renewed" on January 1 actually have contracts dated November 2 (original start) to November 1 (original end, one year later), but their access continues through December 31 under the assumption of renewal. If a customer cancels on November 15, the system believes their contract expired on November 1 and provides 44 days of free access.

## The Question
> Activities (orders, transactions, service usage) must fall within the contracted service period. An activity before the contract start or after the contract end violates the contractual date compliance rule.
> **"Find all Customers where their Orders were placed outside their contract period (before ContractStart or after ContractEnd), and flag contracts where the start date is after the end date."**

## Table Reference
**Customers** — `schemas/01_Customers.csv`
**Orders** — `schemas/02_Orders.csv`

## Solution Query

```sql
-- Step 1: Orders placed outside customer contract period
-- Using Customers.ContractEnd and a derived ContractStart from the schema
SELECT
    o.OrderID,
    o.CustomerID,
    o.OrderDate,
    o.TotalAmount,
    c.FirstName + ' ' + c.LastName AS CustomerName,
    c.ContractEnd,
    -- Derive contract start as 1 year before ContractEnd (illustrative)
    DATEADD(YEAR, -1, c.ContractEnd) AS DerivedContractStart,
    CASE
        WHEN c.ContractEnd IS NULL
             THEN 'No contract end date — open-ended or missing'
        WHEN o.OrderDate > c.ContractEnd
             THEN 'VIOLATION: Order after contract end date'
        WHEN o.OrderDate < DATEADD(YEAR, -1, c.ContractEnd)
             THEN 'WARNING: Order predates derived contract start'
        ELSE 'Within contract period'
    END AS ContractCompliance
FROM Orders o
JOIN Customers c ON o.CustomerID = c.CustomerID
WHERE c.ContractEnd IS NOT NULL
  AND o.OrderDate > c.ContractEnd
ORDER BY o.OrderDate DESC;

-- Step 2: Contracts expiring soon — proactive compliance monitoring
SELECT
    CustomerID,
    FirstName + ' ' + c.LastName AS CustomerName,
    ContractEnd,
    DATEDIFF(DAY, GETDATE(), ContractEnd) AS DaysUntilExpiry,
    CASE
        WHEN ContractEnd < GETDATE()              THEN 'EXPIRED'
        WHEN ContractEnd < DATEADD(DAY, 7, GETDATE())  THEN 'Expires within 7 days'
        WHEN ContractEnd < DATEADD(DAY, 30, GETDATE()) THEN 'Expires within 30 days'
        WHEN ContractEnd < DATEADD(DAY, 90, GETDATE()) THEN 'Expires within 90 days'
        ELSE 'Active — more than 90 days remaining'
    END AS ContractStatus
FROM Customers c
WHERE ContractEnd IS NOT NULL
ORDER BY ContractEnd;

-- Step 3: Post-expiry order count by customer
WITH ExpiredCustomers AS (
    SELECT CustomerID, ContractEnd
    FROM Customers
    WHERE ContractEnd IS NOT NULL AND ContractEnd < GETDATE()
)
SELECT
    ec.CustomerID,
    ec.ContractEnd,
    COUNT(o.OrderID) AS PostExpiryOrders,
    SUM(o.TotalAmount) AS PostExpiryRevenue,
    MIN(o.OrderDate) AS FirstPostExpiryOrder,
    'Orders placed after contract expiry' AS Issue
FROM ExpiredCustomers ec
JOIN Orders o ON ec.CustomerID = o.CustomerID
WHERE o.OrderDate > ec.ContractEnd
GROUP BY ec.CustomerID, ec.ContractEnd
ORDER BY PostExpiryOrders DESC;

-- Step 4: Contract gap detection — period between contracts
SELECT
    a.CustomerID,
    a.ContractEnd AS FirstContractEnd,
    -- In a real system, join to a ContractHistory table for next contract start
    -- Simplified: show expired contracts with no renewal indication
    DATEDIFF(DAY, a.ContractEnd, GETDATE()) AS DaysSinceExpiry,
    CASE
        WHEN DATEDIFF(DAY, a.ContractEnd, GETDATE()) > 30
             THEN 'LAPSED: Contract expired > 30 days ago with no evident renewal'
        WHEN DATEDIFF(DAY, a.ContractEnd, GETDATE()) > 0
             THEN 'RECENTLY EXPIRED: Possible grace period or pending renewal'
        ELSE 'Not yet expired'
    END AS ContractGapStatus
FROM Customers a
WHERE a.ContractEnd IS NOT NULL
  AND a.ContractEnd < GETDATE()
ORDER BY a.ContractEnd;

-- Step 5: Inverted contract dates (ContractEnd before ContractStart would be stored)
-- Checking available column for sign of inversion
SELECT
    CustomerID,
    ContractEnd,
    GETDATE() AS CurrentDate,
    CASE
        WHEN ContractEnd < '2020-01-01'
             THEN 'WARNING: Very old ContractEnd — possibly stale or erroneous'
        WHEN ContractEnd > DATEADD(YEAR, 10, GETDATE())
             THEN 'WARNING: ContractEnd more than 10 years in future — verify'
        ELSE 'Contract end date plausible'
    END AS ContractEndPlausibility
FROM Customers
WHERE ContractEnd IS NOT NULL
  AND (ContractEnd < '2020-01-01' OR ContractEnd > DATEADD(YEAR, 10, GETDATE()));
```

## Expected Output

**Step 1 — Post-contract orders:**
| OrderID | CustomerID | OrderDate | ContractEnd | ContractCompliance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1008 | 4 | 2024-03-20 | 2024-02-28 | VIOLATION: Order after contract end date |

**Step 2 — Contract status:**
| CustomerID | CustomerName | ContractEnd | DaysUntilExpiry | ContractStatus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | Robert Chen | 2024-02-28 | -152 | EXPIRED |
| 7 | Alice Brown | 2024-08-15 | 15 | Expires within 30 days |

## Explanation

**Contract period as a date range constraint**
A valid activity must satisfy `ActivityDate >= ContractStart AND ActivityDate <= ContractEnd`. Step 1 checks the upper bound (`OrderDate > ContractEnd`). A full implementation also checks the lower bound, requiring knowledge of `ContractStart`.

**Proactive vs reactive compliance**
Step 2 (days-until-expiry) is proactive: it surfaces contracts about to expire so the renewal team can act before the constraint is violated. Step 3 (post-expiry orders) is reactive: it detects violations that have already occurred. Both are needed — proactive prevents; reactive remedies.

**Grace period modelling**
Some contracts include a grace period after expiry during which the customer retains access. The SQL model must account for this: `WHERE OrderDate > ContractEnd + GracePeriodDays`. Without this adjustment, valid grace-period activities will be flagged as violations.

**Auto-renewal gap bug**
The scenario's auto-renewal bug (30-day gap in renewal period) would be detected by a contract continuity check: `WHERE ContractStart of renewal row > (ContractEnd of previous row + 1 day)`. This requires a contract history table to track successive contract periods per customer.

## Severity
**High** — Post-contract activity represents either uncontrolled access (the customer is using services they are no longer paying for) or an invoicing failure (services are being provided but revenue is not being collected). Both are direct financial and legal risks. In regulated industries, providing services post-licence-expiry may create regulatory liability.
