# G17-T06 — Security/Compliance | Consent and Legal Basis Validation

## ID
G17-T06

## Group
17 — Security/Compliance

## Type
Consent and Legal Basis Validation — Verifying That Data Processing Has a Valid Legal Basis and Consent Is Current

## Live Scenario
A UK retailer sends a marketing email campaign to 180,000 customers. The next day, the ICO opens an investigation after receiving 2,400 complaints from customers who say they never consented to marketing emails. Investigation reveals three problems: (1) 45,000 customers had IsActive=0 and should have been suppressed from marketing; (2) 31,000 had opted out via an unsubscribe link that updated a different system's preference table, but that table was not joined to the CRM extract; and (3) 12,000 had their consent recorded as part of a pre-GDPR 2016 registration flow that did not meet the GDPR consent standard of "freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous." The consent validation check would have flagged all three groups before the send.

## The Question
> Consent and legal basis validation verifies that every data subject whose data is being processed has a valid, current, and applicable legal basis for that processing. For marketing: explicit consent must be present, not withdrawn, and recorded in the correct field. For operational data: the processing basis (contract, legal obligation, legitimate interest) must be documented and still applicable. SQL validates consent by checking the consent flag, consent date, consent scope, and whether subsequent events (account closure, opt-out, right-to-erasure request) have invalidated the original consent.
> **"Validate consent and legal basis for the Customers table: identify customers where IsActive status suggests their processing basis has ended, check for customers with contract end dates that may have extinguished their consent, identify customers who may have been acquired before GDPR (pre-May 2018) whose consent basis needs verification, and produce a consent health scorecard."**

## Table Reference
**Customers** — `schemas/01_Customers.csv`
**Orders** — `schemas/02_Orders.csv`

## Solution Query

```sql
-- Step 1: IsActive = 0 customers — processing basis likely ended
SELECT
    CustomerID,
    FirstName + ' ' + LastName AS CustomerName,
    IsActive,
    CAST(ContractEnd AS DATE) AS ContractEndDate,
    Country,
    CASE
        WHEN IsActive = 0 AND ContractEnd IS NULL
             THEN 'INACTIVE NO DATE: Account closed but no ContractEnd date — basis period undefined'
        WHEN IsActive = 0 AND ContractEnd IS NOT NULL
             THEN 'INACTIVE SINCE ' + CAST(CAST(ContractEnd AS DATE) AS VARCHAR)
                  + ': Contract processing basis ended — only statutory retention processing permitted'
        ELSE 'Active: Processing basis valid'
    END AS LegalBasisFlag
FROM Customers
WHERE IsActive = 0;

-- Step 2: Pre-GDPR customer data (accounts predating 25 May 2018)
-- Customers whose first order predates GDPR may have pre-GDPR consent that needs refresh
DECLARE @GDPREnforcementDate DATE = '2018-05-25';

SELECT
    c.CustomerID,
    CAST((SELECT MIN(OrderDate) FROM Orders o WHERE o.CustomerID = c.CustomerID) AS DATE)
        AS FirstInteractionDate,
    c.IsActive,
    c.Country,
    CASE
        WHEN (SELECT MIN(OrderDate) FROM Orders o WHERE o.CustomerID = c.CustomerID)
             < @GDPREnforcementDate
             AND c.Country = 'UK'
             THEN 'PRE-GDPR CONSENT: Customer first engaged before 25 May 2018 — original consent basis '
                  + 'may not meet GDPR standard; verify consent refresh was completed'
        WHEN (SELECT MIN(OrderDate) FROM Orders o WHERE o.CustomerID = c.CustomerID)
             >= @GDPREnforcementDate
             THEN 'POST-GDPR: Engaged after GDPR enforcement date — consent should be compliant'
        WHEN (SELECT MIN(OrderDate) FROM Orders o WHERE o.CustomerID = c.CustomerID)
             IS NULL
             THEN 'NO ORDERS: Customer has no order history — verify basis for holding personal data'
        ELSE 'Pre-GDPR date but non-UK — different regulation applies'
    END AS ConsentBasisFlag
FROM Customers c
WHERE c.IsActive = 1
ORDER BY FirstInteractionDate ASC;

-- Step 3: Contract end date vs IsActive consistency
-- A customer with IsActive=1 but a ContractEnd date in the past is an inconsistency
SELECT
    CustomerID,
    IsActive,
    CAST(ContractEnd AS DATE) AS ContractEndDate,
    CASE
        WHEN IsActive = 1 AND ContractEnd IS NOT NULL
             AND CAST(ContractEnd AS DATE) < CAST(GETDATE() AS DATE)
             THEN 'INCONSISTENT: IsActive=1 but ContractEnd is in the past ('
                  + CAST(CAST(ContractEnd AS DATE) AS VARCHAR)
                  + ') — account should be inactive, processing basis may have ended'
        WHEN IsActive = 0 AND ContractEnd IS NOT NULL
             AND CAST(ContractEnd AS DATE) > CAST(GETDATE() AS DATE)
             THEN 'INCONSISTENT: IsActive=0 but ContractEnd is in the future ('
                  + CAST(CAST(ContractEnd AS DATE) AS VARCHAR)
                  + ') — contract still active but account marked inactive'
        WHEN IsActive = 1 AND ContractEnd IS NULL
             THEN 'OK: Active account with no end date (open-ended contract)'
        WHEN IsActive = 0 AND ContractEnd IS NOT NULL
             AND CAST(ContractEnd AS DATE) <= CAST(GETDATE() AS DATE)
             THEN 'OK: Inactive with past ContractEnd — consistent'
        ELSE 'OK'
    END AS ConsistencyFlag
FROM Customers
ORDER BY ConsistencyFlag DESC;

-- Step 4: UK GDPR lawful basis coverage
-- Verify that all customers have at least one valid processing basis attribute
SELECT
    CustomerID,
    IsActive,
    CAST(ContractEnd AS DATE) AS ContractEnd,
    -- Infer processing basis from available fields
    CASE
        WHEN IsActive = 1 AND ContractEnd IS NULL
             THEN 'BASIS: Active contract — processing justified under Art.6(1)(b)'
        WHEN IsActive = 1 AND ContractEnd > GETDATE()
             THEN 'BASIS: Contract in force until ' + CAST(CAST(ContractEnd AS DATE) AS VARCHAR)
                  + ' — Art.6(1)(b)'
        WHEN IsActive = 0 AND ContractEnd IS NOT NULL
             AND ContractEnd >= DATEADD(YEAR, -2, GETDATE())
             THEN 'BASIS: Post-contract retention within 2-year window — Art.6(1)(c) legal obligation'
        WHEN IsActive = 0 AND ContractEnd IS NOT NULL
             AND ContractEnd < DATEADD(YEAR, -2, GETDATE())
             THEN 'NO VALID BASIS: Retention period expired — no lawful basis for continued processing'
        WHEN IsActive = 0 AND ContractEnd IS NULL
             THEN 'UNCLEAR BASIS: Inactive account with no ContractEnd — processing basis undetermined'
        ELSE 'REVIEW REQUIRED'
    END AS InferredLawfulBasis
FROM Customers
ORDER BY IsActive DESC, ContractEnd ASC;

-- Step 5: Consent health scorecard
SELECT
    'Total customers in database'           AS Metric, COUNT(*) AS Value
FROM Customers
UNION ALL
SELECT 'Active customers (processing basis: contract)', COUNT(*)
FROM Customers WHERE IsActive = 1
UNION ALL
SELECT 'Inactive customers (processing basis: retention)', COUNT(*)
FROM Customers WHERE IsActive = 0 AND ContractEnd IS NOT NULL
  AND ContractEnd >= DATEADD(YEAR, -2, GETDATE())
UNION ALL
SELECT 'No valid processing basis (requires deletion/review)', COUNT(*)
FROM Customers
WHERE IsActive = 0
  AND (ContractEnd IS NULL OR ContractEnd < DATEADD(YEAR, -2, GETDATE()))
UNION ALL
SELECT 'Active/inactive inconsistency', COUNT(*)
FROM Customers
WHERE (IsActive = 1 AND ContractEnd IS NOT NULL AND CAST(ContractEnd AS DATE) < CAST(GETDATE() AS DATE))
   OR (IsActive = 0 AND ContractEnd IS NOT NULL AND CAST(ContractEnd AS DATE) > CAST(GETDATE() AS DATE))
UNION ALL
SELECT 'Customers with no ContractEnd (open-ended)', COUNT(*)
FROM Customers WHERE ContractEnd IS NULL;
```

## Explanation

**IsActive as a processing basis proxy**
In the absence of a dedicated `ConsentStatus` or `LegalBasisCode` column, `IsActive` serves as the primary indicator of whether a customer relationship is current. `IsActive = 1` implies the contract is in force and data processing is justified under GDPR Article 6(1)(b) (contract performance). `IsActive = 0` means the contract has ended and processing can only continue for statutory retention obligations — not for marketing, profiling, or other commercial purposes.

**Pre-GDPR consent identification**
GDPR required organisations to review and refresh pre-existing consent that did not meet GDPR standards. A customer whose first engagement predates 25 May 2018 may have consented under the old Data Protection Act 1998 regime where consent was less strictly defined. Step 2 flags these customers for consent verification — not because their data is necessarily unlawful to hold, but because the consent basis needs to be documented and confirmed as GDPR-compliant.

**IsActive/ContractEnd inconsistency as a data quality issue**
Step 3 detects the case where `IsActive = 1` but `ContractEnd` is in the past: the account is flagged as active, but the contract data says it ended. This inconsistency means either the IsActive flag was not updated when the contract ended, or the ContractEnd date was entered incorrectly. Either way, the processing basis for this customer is ambiguous — a compliance risk that needs resolution.

**Inferred vs explicit legal basis**
Step 4 infers the lawful basis from the available fields. In a fully compliant system, the lawful basis should be explicitly recorded (e.g., a `LawfulBasis` column with values like 'Contract', 'LegalObligation', 'Consent', 'LegitimateInterest'). Inferring it from other fields is a compensating check that catches obvious gaps but cannot substitute for explicit documentation — regulators expect to see the lawful basis recorded at the point of collection.

## Severity
**Critical** — Processing personal data without a valid lawful basis is a direct GDPR Article 6 violation. The ICO's enforcement record shows fines specifically for consent failures: British Airways (£20M), Marriott (£18.4M), and numerous smaller fines for marketing to people who had not consented. Consent validation should run before every data processing activity involving personal data.
