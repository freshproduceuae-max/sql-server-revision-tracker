# G17-T05 — Security/Compliance | Retention Policy and Data Expiry Validation

## ID
G17-T05

## Group
17 — Security/Compliance

## Type
Retention Policy and Data Expiry Validation — Verifying That Data Is Retained for Exactly the Required Period and Deleted When the Retention Limit Is Reached

## Live Scenario
A UK telecoms company's GDPR compliance team discovers during an audit that customer personal data for accounts closed before 2018 still exists in the production CRM — over 6 years after those customers left. The company's retention policy states personal data must be deleted within 2 years of contract end. Additionally, active customer data created before 2016 includes marketing consent records that predate GDPR's 2018 consent requirements — those consent records should have been refreshed but weren't. The retention validation check identifies all 14,300 records in breach of the 2-year deletion window and flags the 8,200 active customers whose consent basis needs to be re-validated.

## The Question
> Retention policy validation checks that: (1) records whose retention period has expired have been deleted or anonymised; (2) records approaching their retention limit are flagged for upcoming deletion; (3) data collected under a specific legal basis has not outlived that basis; and (4) records that should have been deleted are not present. SQL validates retention by comparing record creation/event dates against GETDATE() minus the retention period, and by checking for records beyond their expected data lifecycle.
> **"Validate data retention compliance for the Customers table: identify customers whose contract has ended and whose personal data should have been deleted (retention period: 2 years post-contract-end), flag customers approaching their retention expiry, verify that no data exists beyond the maximum retention period, and identify records where the data lifecycle event (contract end) date is missing."**

## Table Reference
**Customers** — `schemas/01_Customers.csv`
**Orders** — `schemas/02_Orders.csv`
**Employees** — `schemas/04_Employees.csv`

## Solution Query

```sql
-- Step 1: Customer data retention check
-- Retention rule: personal data must be deleted 2 years after ContractEnd
-- Warning window: 6 months before deletion deadline
DECLARE @RetentionYears        INT  = 2;
DECLARE @WarningMonths         INT  = 6;
DECLARE @DeletionDeadline      DATE = DATEADD(YEAR, -@RetentionYears, CAST(GETDATE() AS DATE));
DECLARE @WarningWindowStart    DATE = DATEADD(MONTH, -@WarningMonths, @DeletionDeadline);

SELECT
    CustomerID,
    FirstName + ' ' + LastName                         AS CustomerName,
    IsActive,
    CAST(ContractEnd AS DATE)                           AS ContractEndDate,
    @DeletionDeadline                                   AS DeletionDeadline,
    DATEDIFF(DAY, CAST(ContractEnd AS DATE), CAST(GETDATE() AS DATE)) AS DaysSinceContractEnd,
    DATEDIFF(DAY, @DeletionDeadline, CAST(ContractEnd AS DATE))      AS DaysUntilDeadline,
    CASE
        WHEN IsActive = 1
             THEN 'ACTIVE: Retention clock not started — data retained under active contract basis'
        WHEN ContractEnd IS NULL
             THEN 'NULL CONTRACT END: Retention period cannot be calculated — data lifecycle undefined'
        WHEN CAST(ContractEnd AS DATE) <= @DeletionDeadline
             THEN 'BREACH: Contract ended ' +
                  CAST(DATEDIFF(YEAR, CAST(ContractEnd AS DATE), CAST(GETDATE() AS DATE)) AS VARCHAR)
                  + ' years ago — personal data must be deleted (deadline was '
                  + CAST(@DeletionDeadline AS VARCHAR) + ')'
        WHEN CAST(ContractEnd AS DATE) <= DATEADD(MONTH, @WarningMonths, @DeletionDeadline)
             THEN 'WARNING: Contract ends ' + CAST(ContractEnd AS DATE, 120)
                  + ' — approaching ' + CAST(@RetentionYears AS VARCHAR)
                  + '-year deletion deadline'
        ELSE 'WITHIN RETENTION: ' + CAST(DATEDIFF(DAY, CAST(GETDATE() AS DATE),
             DATEADD(YEAR, @RetentionYears, CAST(ContractEnd AS DATE))) AS VARCHAR)
             + ' days until retention period expires'
    END AS RetentionStatus
FROM Customers
WHERE IsActive = 0 OR ContractEnd IS NOT NULL
ORDER BY ContractEndDate ASC;

-- Step 2: Active customers with data older than expected
-- Active customers' data should be reviewed after 5 years even without contract end
SELECT
    CustomerID,
    CAST(DateOfBirth AS DATE) AS DateOfBirth,
    Country,
    IsActive,
    CASE
        WHEN DateOfBirth IS NULL
             THEN 'NULL: Date of birth not recorded — cannot assess data age'
        WHEN DATEDIFF(YEAR, CAST(DateOfBirth AS DATE), CAST(GETDATE() AS DATE)) < 18
             THEN 'UNDER 18 AT DOB AGE: Possible child data — enhanced GDPR protections apply'
        ELSE 'Adult customer'
    END AS DOBFlag,
    -- For active customers: when was their account created? Use earliest order as proxy
    (SELECT CAST(MIN(OrderDate) AS DATE) FROM Orders o WHERE o.CustomerID = c.CustomerID) AS FirstOrderDate,
    DATEDIFF(YEAR,
        (SELECT MIN(OrderDate) FROM Orders o WHERE o.CustomerID = c.CustomerID),
        GETDATE()) AS YearsSinceFirstOrder,
    CASE
        WHEN (SELECT MIN(OrderDate) FROM Orders o WHERE o.CustomerID = c.CustomerID)
             < DATEADD(YEAR, -5, GETDATE())
             THEN 'LONG-STANDING: Customer active for 5+ years — consider consent refresh review'
        ELSE 'Within normal review period'
    END AS DataAgeFlag
FROM Customers c
WHERE IsActive = 1;

-- Step 3: Employee data retention check
-- Retention rule: employee data retained for 6 years post-termination (HMRC requirement)
DECLARE @EmployeeRetentionYears INT = 6;

SELECT
    EmployeeID,
    FullName,
    CAST(HireDate AS DATE)        AS HireDate,
    CAST(TerminationDate AS DATE) AS TerminationDate,
    DATEDIFF(YEAR, CAST(TerminationDate AS DATE), CAST(GETDATE() AS DATE)) AS YearsPostTermination,
    CASE
        WHEN TerminationDate IS NULL
             THEN 'ACTIVE EMPLOYEE: Retain all data during employment'
        WHEN DATEDIFF(YEAR, CAST(TerminationDate AS DATE), CAST(GETDATE() AS DATE))
             > @EmployeeRetentionYears
             THEN 'RETENTION BREACH: Terminated ' +
                  CAST(DATEDIFF(YEAR, CAST(TerminationDate AS DATE), CAST(GETDATE() AS DATE)) AS VARCHAR)
                  + ' years ago — must be deleted after ' + CAST(@EmployeeRetentionYears AS VARCHAR)
                  + ' years'
        WHEN DATEDIFF(YEAR, CAST(TerminationDate AS DATE), CAST(GETDATE() AS DATE))
             >= @EmployeeRetentionYears - 1
             THEN 'APPROACHING EXPIRY: Termination date is within 1 year of retention limit'
        ELSE 'Within retention: ' +
             CAST(@EmployeeRetentionYears - DATEDIFF(YEAR, CAST(TerminationDate AS DATE),
                  CAST(GETDATE() AS DATE)) AS VARCHAR) + ' years remaining'
    END AS RetentionStatus
FROM Employees
WHERE TerminationDate IS NOT NULL
ORDER BY TerminationDate ASC;

-- Step 4: Retention policy compliance summary
SELECT
    'Customers: active (retention running)' AS Category,
    COUNT(*) AS RecordCount,
    'Retain until contract end + 2 years' AS Policy
FROM Customers WHERE IsActive = 1
UNION ALL
SELECT
    'Customers: inactive, within retention window',
    COUNT(*),
    '< 2 years since ContractEnd'
FROM Customers
WHERE IsActive = 0 AND ContractEnd IS NOT NULL
  AND CAST(ContractEnd AS DATE) > DATEADD(YEAR, -2, CAST(GETDATE() AS DATE))
UNION ALL
SELECT
    'Customers: inactive, retention expired',
    COUNT(*),
    '2+ years since ContractEnd — DELETE REQUIRED'
FROM Customers
WHERE IsActive = 0 AND ContractEnd IS NOT NULL
  AND CAST(ContractEnd AS DATE) <= DATEADD(YEAR, -2, CAST(GETDATE() AS DATE))
UNION ALL
SELECT
    'Customers: no ContractEnd date (undefined retention)',
    COUNT(*),
    'Retention period cannot be calculated'
FROM Customers
WHERE ContractEnd IS NULL AND IsActive = 0;
```

## Expected Output

**Step 4 — Retention compliance summary:**
| Category | RecordCount | Policy |
|---|---|---|
| Customers: active (retention running) | 8 | Retain until contract end + 2 years |
| Customers: inactive, within retention window | 2 | < 2 years since ContractEnd |
| Customers: inactive, retention expired | 0 | 2+ years since ContractEnd — DELETE REQUIRED |

## Explanation

**Retention deadline as a derived date**
The deletion deadline for any record is `EventDate + RetentionPeriod` — here, `ContractEnd + 2 years`. Comparing this against `GETDATE()` gives a simple test: if the deadline is in the past, the record should have been deleted. The `DECLARE` variables at the top make the retention period configurable — different data categories (customer data, employee data, financial records) have different regulatory retention requirements and the thresholds should be parameterised.

**Warning window for proactive deletion**
Step 1's warning window flags records whose retention deadline is within 6 months of expiry. This provides the operational team with advance notice to schedule deletion runs, reducing the likelihood of a retention breach where data survives past its deadline because no one noticed. The warning window should be longer for large datasets that require significant processing time to purge.

**NULL ContractEnd as an undefined retention horizon**
A record with no ContractEnd date has no defined start for its post-contract retention clock. This is a data quality failure that compounds into a compliance failure: if the system cannot determine when the contract ended, it cannot determine when to delete the data. Step 4 counts these separately, as they require manual investigation rather than automated deletion.

**Employee data vs customer data retention**
Employment law in the UK requires employers to retain certain employee records for 6 years after employment ends (HMRC payroll records) while GDPR requires personal data to be deleted when the processing purpose ends. These two obligations conflict — the resolution is to retain minimal data (payroll records only) for 6 years and delete all other personal data (home address, emergency contacts, health information) much sooner. Step 3 simplifies this to a single 6-year retention rule for illustration.

## Severity
**Critical** — Retention breaches are directly actionable by regulators: the ICO has issued fines specifically for excessive retention of personal data. Unlike other data quality issues, retention breaches are certain (the calculation is deterministic) and time-sensitive (every day of non-compliance extends the breach). Automated retention validation should run daily and be escalated immediately to a named compliance officer.
