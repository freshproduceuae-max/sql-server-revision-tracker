# G12-T06 — Statistical/Analytical | Percentile Rank and Concentration Analysis

## ID
G12-T06

## Group
12 — Statistical/Analytical

## Type
Percentile Rank and Concentration Analysis — Measuring How Value and Activity Concentrate Among Entities

## Live Scenario
A commercial director at a wholesale distributor wants to understand revenue concentration. Running a percentile rank analysis on customers by annual revenue reveals: the top 8% of customers (the 92nd percentile and above) generate 73% of total revenue. More critically, the single highest-revenue customer represents 23% of total company revenue — a concentration risk that no one in the business had quantified before. The analysis directly influences strategic decisions: the Key Account team is restructured to dedicate two full-time managers to just the top 12 customers, and a customer concentration risk report is added to the monthly board pack. Without the percentile rank analysis, the business operated on the intuition that "the top customers matter" without knowing that a single customer's loss would threaten the business's viability.

## The Question
> Percentile rank analysis assigns each entity a position in the overall distribution and measures how value concentrates in the top percentiles. The Pareto (80/20) principle is a common benchmark: do 20% of customers generate 80% of revenue? SQL uses PERCENT_RANK(), NTILE(), and cumulative SUM to produce concentration profiles.
> **"Rank all customers by their total order value, compute their percentile rank, identify which customers fall in the top 10% and top 1%, and calculate what percentage of total revenue the top 10% and top 20% of customers generate."**

## Table Reference
**Orders** — `schemas/02_Orders.csv`
**Customers** — `schemas/01_Customers.csv`

## Solution Query

```sql
-- Step 1: Customer revenue ranking with percentile rank
WITH CustomerRevenue AS (
    SELECT
        o.CustomerID,
        c.FirstName,
        c.LastName,
        c.Country,
        COUNT(o.OrderID)     AS OrderCount,
        SUM(o.TotalAmount)   AS TotalRevenue,
        AVG(o.TotalAmount)   AS AvgOrderValue,
        MIN(o.OrderDate)     AS FirstOrder,
        MAX(o.OrderDate)     AS LastOrder
    FROM Orders o
    LEFT JOIN Customers c ON o.CustomerID = c.CustomerID
    WHERE o.TotalAmount IS NOT NULL
    GROUP BY o.CustomerID, c.FirstName, c.LastName, c.Country
)
SELECT
    CustomerID,
    FirstName,
    LastName,
    Country,
    OrderCount,
    ROUND(TotalRevenue, 2)       AS TotalRevenue,
    ROUND(AvgOrderValue, 2)      AS AvgOrderValue,
    -- Percentile rank: 1.0 = highest, 0.0 = lowest
    ROUND(PERCENT_RANK() OVER (ORDER BY TotalRevenue), 4) AS PercentileRank,
    -- Decile (1–10)
    NTILE(10) OVER (ORDER BY TotalRevenue) AS Decile,
    -- Cumulative revenue share
    ROUND(100.0 * SUM(TotalRevenue) OVER (ORDER BY TotalRevenue DESC
                  ROWS BETWEEN UNBOUNDED PRECEDING AND CURRENT ROW)
          / SUM(TotalRevenue) OVER (), 1) AS CumulativeRevenuePct,
    -- Classify
    CASE
        WHEN PERCENT_RANK() OVER (ORDER BY TotalRevenue) >= 0.99
             THEN 'TOP 1%: Critical account'
        WHEN PERCENT_RANK() OVER (ORDER BY TotalRevenue) >= 0.90
             THEN 'TOP 10%: Key account'
        WHEN PERCENT_RANK() OVER (ORDER BY TotalRevenue) >= 0.80
             THEN 'TOP 20%: High-value account'
        WHEN PERCENT_RANK() OVER (ORDER BY TotalRevenue) >= 0.50
             THEN 'Above median'
        ELSE 'Below median'
    END AS RevenueSegment,
    -- Revenue share of this customer
    ROUND(100.0 * TotalRevenue / NULLIF(SUM(TotalRevenue) OVER (), 0), 2) AS RevenueSharePct
FROM CustomerRevenue
ORDER BY TotalRevenue DESC;

-- Step 2: Pareto analysis — revenue concentration by top N%
WITH CustomerRevenue AS (
    SELECT
        o.CustomerID,
        SUM(o.TotalAmount) AS TotalRevenue
    FROM Orders o
    WHERE o.TotalAmount IS NOT NULL
    GROUP BY o.CustomerID
),
Ranked AS (
    SELECT
        CustomerID,
        TotalRevenue,
        PERCENT_RANK() OVER (ORDER BY TotalRevenue) AS Pctile,
        NTILE(100) OVER (ORDER BY TotalRevenue) AS Percentile100
    FROM CustomerRevenue
)
SELECT
    Percentile100 AS PercentileGroup,
    COUNT(*) AS CustomersInGroup,
    ROUND(SUM(TotalRevenue), 2) AS GroupRevenue,
    ROUND(100.0 * SUM(TotalRevenue) / SUM(SUM(TotalRevenue)) OVER (), 1) AS GroupRevenuePct,
    ROUND(SUM(SUM(TotalRevenue)) OVER (ORDER BY Percentile100 DESC
          ROWS BETWEEN UNBOUNDED PRECEDING AND CURRENT ROW)
          / SUM(SUM(TotalRevenue)) OVER () * 100, 1) AS CumulativeFromTopPct
FROM Ranked
GROUP BY Percentile100
ORDER BY Percentile100 DESC;

-- Step 3: Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI) — market concentration
-- HHI = sum of squares of market share percentages
-- HHI < 1500: low concentration; 1500-2500: moderate; >2500: high
WITH CustomerRevenue AS (
    SELECT
        o.CustomerID,
        SUM(o.TotalAmount) AS TotalRevenue
    FROM Orders o
    WHERE o.TotalAmount IS NOT NULL
    GROUP BY o.CustomerID
),
Shares AS (
    SELECT
        CustomerID,
        TotalRevenue,
        100.0 * TotalRevenue / NULLIF(SUM(TotalRevenue) OVER (), 0) AS SharePct
    FROM CustomerRevenue
)
SELECT
    COUNT(*) AS CustomerCount,
    ROUND(SUM(TotalRevenue), 2) AS TotalRevenue,
    ROUND(SUM(POWER(SharePct, 2)), 0) AS HHI,
    CASE
        WHEN SUM(POWER(SharePct, 2)) > 2500
             THEN 'HIGH CONCENTRATION: HHI > 2500 — significant single-customer risk'
        WHEN SUM(POWER(SharePct, 2)) > 1500
             THEN 'MODERATE CONCENTRATION: HHI 1500-2500 — monitor top accounts'
        ELSE 'LOW CONCENTRATION: HHI < 1500 — diversified customer base'
    END AS ConcentrationRating
FROM Shares;

-- Step 4: Top 20% contribution summary (Pareto check)
WITH CustomerRevenue AS (
    SELECT o.CustomerID, SUM(o.TotalAmount) AS TotalRevenue
    FROM Orders o WHERE o.TotalAmount IS NOT NULL GROUP BY o.CustomerID
),
Ranked AS (
    SELECT *,
           PERCENT_RANK() OVER (ORDER BY TotalRevenue) AS Pctile,
           SUM(TotalRevenue) OVER () AS GrandTotal
    FROM CustomerRevenue
)
SELECT
    SUM(CASE WHEN Pctile >= 0.80 THEN TotalRevenue ELSE 0 END)
        / NULLIF(MAX(GrandTotal), 0) * 100 AS Top20PctRevenueShare,
    SUM(CASE WHEN Pctile >= 0.90 THEN TotalRevenue ELSE 0 END)
        / NULLIF(MAX(GrandTotal), 0) * 100 AS Top10PctRevenueShare,
    COUNT(CASE WHEN Pctile >= 0.80 THEN 1 END) AS Top20PctCustomerCount,
    COUNT(*) AS TotalCustomers,
    CASE
        WHEN SUM(CASE WHEN Pctile >= 0.80 THEN TotalRevenue ELSE 0 END)
             / NULLIF(MAX(GrandTotal), 0) > 0.80
             THEN 'PARETO CONFIRMED: Top 20% of customers generate >80% of revenue'
        ELSE 'PARETO NOT MET: Revenue more evenly distributed'
    END AS ParetoCheck
FROM Ranked;
```

## Explanation

**PERCENT_RANK() vs NTILE()**
`PERCENT_RANK()` returns a value between 0.0 and 1.0 representing where an entity falls in the ranked distribution. `NTILE(10)` divides the population into 10 equally-sized buckets (deciles). For small datasets, NTILE can produce unequal bucket sizes; for large datasets (N > 1000), both produce stable, comparable results. PERCENT_RANK is preferred for thresholding (top 10% = PERCENT_RANK >= 0.90); NTILE is preferred for bucketing (group customers into 10 equal segments).

**Cumulative revenue from top**
The `SUM(...) OVER (ORDER BY TotalRevenue DESC ROWS BETWEEN UNBOUNDED PRECEDING AND CURRENT ROW)` window orders from highest to lowest, so the cumulative sum builds from the top customer downward. Dividing by the grand total gives the percentage of total revenue captured by the top N customers — the basis for the Pareto principle check.

**Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI)**
The HHI is the sum of the squares of each entity's market share percentage. A monopoly (one entity with 100% share) has HHI = 10,000. A perfectly fragmented market with N equal entities has HHI = 10,000/N. The US Department of Justice uses HHI > 2,500 as the threshold for "highly concentrated" in antitrust analysis; the same thresholds apply to customer revenue concentration as a risk measure.

**Revenue concentration as a business risk metric**
A customer revenue share above 10% represents a key-account dependency risk — the loss of that customer would require finding equivalent replacement revenue. Above 20%, it represents a material business risk. Above 30%, most risk management frameworks classify this as a concentration risk requiring board-level disclosure and a retention contingency plan.

## Severity
**Medium** — Concentration analysis answers strategic questions that cannot be answered from averages alone. A business with average revenue per customer of £2,000 may have 80% of its revenue from 3 customers — a fact invisible in the average but immediately apparent in a percentile rank profile. Concentration risk quantification enables proactive account management and risk mitigation before a key-account loss becomes an existential crisis.
