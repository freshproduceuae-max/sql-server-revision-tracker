# BA05-T03 — Requirements Life Cycle Management | Prioritising Requirements Under Real Constraint

## ID
BA05-T03

## Chapter
5 — Requirements Life Cycle Management

## Live Scenario
Every requirement in a 120-item backlog is marked high priority by its requesting stakeholder. Delivery capacity covers about 40 items before the regulatory deadline.

## The Question
> How do you produce a defensible priority order when every stakeholder has already declared their item critical?

## Approach
1. Replace individual ratings with a shared basis — value, risk, dependency and regulatory obligation.
2. Force ranking rather than rating, so relative order must be stated.
3. Make dependencies explicit; an item is not deliverable ahead of what it depends on regardless of priority.
4. Take the ranking through governance so it is an organisational decision, not the analyst's opinion.

## Deliverables
- A ranked backlog with the basis for ranking recorded
- A dependency map constraining the order

## Tools
- MoSCoW or weighted scoring in Excel
- Jira backlog ranking
- Aha! or ProductPlan for roadmap views

## Common Pitfall
Rating instead of ranking. If everything can be 'high', nothing is prioritised — forcing a strict order is what surfaces the real trade-off.

## Interview Angle
Know MoSCoW and at least one weighted method, and be honest that MoSCoW collapses when everything becomes Must.
