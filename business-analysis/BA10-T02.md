# BA10-T02 — Techniques | Modelling and Prototyping

## ID
BA10-T02

## Chapter
10 — Techniques

## Live Scenario
Stakeholders sign off a 40-page specification. At UAT they say the screens are not what they imagined, though the build matches the document exactly.

## The Question
> What modelling or prototyping would have exposed the mismatch while it was still cheap to fix?

## Approach
1. Model where the risk of misunderstanding is highest — interfaces and complex branching, not everything.
2. Prototype at the fidelity that answers the question; a wireframe tests layout, not colour.
3. Use models as conversation instruments rather than sign-off artefacts.
4. Be explicit that the prototype is not the build, or it becomes an implied commitment.

## Deliverables
- Models or prototypes covering the highest-ambiguity areas
- Recorded feedback and resulting requirement changes

## Tools
- Balsamiq or Figma for wireframes
- Axure for interactive prototypes
- Lucidchart or Visio for process and data models

## Common Pitfall
High-fidelity prototypes shown too early. Stakeholders review the styling and miss the logic, and executives assume it is nearly built.

## Interview Angle
Knowing when not to prototype matters as much as knowing how. Prototyping a settled back-end rule wastes everyone's time.
