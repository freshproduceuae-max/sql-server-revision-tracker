# BA09-T03 — Underlying Competencies | Communication and Interaction Skills

## ID
BA09-T03

## Chapter
9 — Underlying Competencies

## Live Scenario
You must explain to a non-technical executive why a seemingly small data change requires eight weeks. Previous attempts have used the words schema, referential integrity and migration, and lost the room.

## The Question
> How do you explain technical constraint in business language without oversimplifying to the point of being wrong?

## Approach
1. Lead with the business consequence, not the technical mechanism.
2. Use an analogy that holds up under a follow-up question, and check it does before using it.
3. Quantify in terms the audience already reasons about — cost, risk, customer impact.
4. Offer the technical detail as available rather than delivering it uninvited.

## Deliverables
- An explanation the audience can repeat accurately to someone else
- An agreed decision based on understood trade-offs

## Tools
- PowerPoint for the visual explanation
- Lucidchart for a simplified model

## Common Pitfall
Analogies that break under scrutiny. A simplification that collapses at the first intelligent question costs more credibility than the jargon it replaced.

## Interview Angle
'Explain a technical concept to a non-technical stakeholder' is near-universal. Practise one until it is genuinely clear, not merely shorter.
