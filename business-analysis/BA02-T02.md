# BA02-T02 — Key Concepts | Classifying Requirements So They Stay Useful

## ID
BA02-T02

## Chapter
2 — Key Concepts

## Live Scenario
A requirements document mixes 'the system shall support 5,000 concurrent users' with 'we want to reduce call centre volume by 20%' and 'the login button should be blue'. The development team cannot tell what is negotiable.

## The Question
> How do you reclassify this so that priorities, ownership and traceability all become possible?

## Approach
1. Separate business requirements (the outcome the organisation wants) from stakeholder requirements (what a specific group needs).
2. Separate solution requirements into functional and non-functional.
3. Pull transition requirements out — data migration and training are temporary and get forgotten otherwise.
4. Trace each lower-level requirement up to the business requirement it serves; anything that traces to nothing is a candidate for deletion.

## Deliverables
- A reclassified requirements set with type labels
- A list of orphan requirements that serve no stated business outcome

## Tools
- Jira or Azure DevOps for typed requirement records
- Excel for the traceability matrix

## Common Pitfall
Forgetting transition requirements. They only apply while the change happens, so they are invisible in a steady-state specification and then blow up the cutover.

## Interview Angle
Know the difference between functional and non-functional cold, and be ready to give a non-functional requirement that killed a project. Performance and regulatory retention are strong examples.
