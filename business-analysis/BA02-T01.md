# BA02-T01 — Key Concepts | The Six Core Concepts and Why They Interlock

## ID
BA02-T01

## Chapter
2 — Key Concepts

## Live Scenario
A retail bank proposes replacing its loan origination system. The business case says the current system is 'outdated'. When you ask what value the replacement delivers, you get three incompatible answers from three directors.

## The Question
> How do you use a structured core model to expose that the team has not agreed what the change is actually for?

## Approach
1. Walk the six concepts in turn — change, need, solution, stakeholder, value, context — and ask the room to fill each one in.
2. Note where answers diverge; divergence on value is the usual fault line.
3. Show that 'outdated' is a context observation, not a need.
4. Force a single articulated need before any solution discussion resumes.

## Deliverables
- A completed core-concept model with one agreed statement per concept
- A logged disagreement register where consensus was not reached

## Tools
- Miro for the live workshop
- Excel for the disagreement register

## Common Pitfall
Accepting a solution as a need. 'We need a new system' is a solution; 'we lose 30% of applications to processing delay' is a need. The second one can be solved several ways.

## Interview Angle
You may be asked to distinguish need from solution on the spot. Have a real example ready where reframing the need changed what got built.
