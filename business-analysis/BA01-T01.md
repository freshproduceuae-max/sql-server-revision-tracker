# BA01-T01 — Introduction | What Business Analysis Actually Is

## ID
BA01-T01

## Chapter
1 — Introduction

## Live Scenario
A logistics firm hires its first business analyst after a warehouse system rollout runs 40% over budget. The post-mortem finds nobody ever wrote down what problem the system was meant to solve — the team went straight from 'our warehouse is slow' to picking a vendor.

## The Question
> The CEO asks you: 'We already have project managers and developers. What do you actually add?' How do you answer in a way that is concrete rather than a job description?

## Approach
1. Define business analysis by its output: recommended changes that deliver value to stakeholders, not documents.
2. Separate the three questions that get conflated — what problem are we solving, what change would solve it, and did the change work.
3. Show where the warehouse project skipped straight from symptom to solution with no needs analysis in between.
4. Name the cost of that gap in the firm's own numbers: the 40% overrun is the price of an unexamined requirement.

## Deliverables
- A one-page statement of the business need, separate from any proposed solution
- A stakeholder list with who is affected and who decides

## Tools
- Word or Confluence for the needs statement
- PowerPoint for the framing conversation

## Common Pitfall
Answering the question with a list of deliverables. Stakeholders do not want documents; they want a change that works. Lead with the outcome and mention artefacts only as evidence.

## Interview Angle
Expect 'what is the difference between a BA and a project manager?' The clean answer: a project manager owns delivering the change on time and budget; a business analyst owns whether it is the right change at all.
