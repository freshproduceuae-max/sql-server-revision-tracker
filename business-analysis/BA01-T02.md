# BA01-T02 — Introduction | How the BABOK Framework Fits Together

## ID
BA01-T02

## Chapter
1 — Introduction

## Live Scenario
You join a team where every analyst works differently. One writes 60-page specifications, another writes sticky notes. Requirements get lost between them, and nobody can tell whether a piece of analysis is finished.

## The Question
> How do you introduce a shared framework without imposing bureaucracy on a team that is already delivering?

## Approach
1. Present the six knowledge areas as a checklist of concerns, not a sequence of phases.
2. Map what the team already does onto those areas so the framework validates existing practice rather than replacing it.
3. Identify the genuinely missing areas — usually solution evaluation, because teams stop at go-live.
4. Agree a minimum standard per area rather than a mandatory template.

## Deliverables
- A one-page map of current practice against the six knowledge areas
- An agreed definition of done for a piece of analysis

## Tools
- Confluence for the shared standard
- Miro or Lucidchart for the mapping workshop

## Common Pitfall
Treating the knowledge areas as sequential phases. They are concurrent concerns — you revisit strategy analysis when elicitation surfaces something that changes the problem.

## Interview Angle
Be able to name the six knowledge areas and say which one most teams neglect. Solution evaluation is the honest answer, and saying so shows you have shipped things.
