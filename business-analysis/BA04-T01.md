## Chapter
4 — Elicitation and Collaboration

## Live Scenario
An analyst books two hours with the head of claims, arrives with no agenda, and asks 'so, what do you need?'. The session produces a wish list, no decisions, and a stakeholder who now believes the project is unprepared.

## The Question
> What preparation would have converted that two hours into usable analysis?

## Approach
1. Define the specific decisions or gaps the session must close before booking it.
2. Choose the technique that fits the outcome — interview for depth, workshop for consensus, observation for tacit process.
3. Pre-read existing documentation so stakeholder time is spent on what is not already written down.
4. Send the scope in advance so participants arrive with the right material.

## Deliverables
- A session plan stating the questions to be answered
- Pre-read material and a participant list

## Tools
- Word or Docs for the session plan
- Outlook or Teams for scheduling
- Miro for workshop boards

## Common Pitfall
Open-ended 'tell me what you need' sessions. They generate wish lists rather than requirements, and they transfer the analysis burden onto the stakeholder.

## Interview Angle
You may be asked how you prepare for a requirements workshop. The differentiator is stating the decisions the session must produce.
