# BA10-T04 — Techniques | Evaluation and Measurement Techniques

## ID
BA10-T04

## Chapter
10 — Techniques

## Live Scenario
The sponsor wants to know if a process change worked. Volumes rose 20% in the same period for unrelated commercial reasons, and the process measure moved too.

## The Question
> How do you evaluate the change when the environment did not hold still?

## Approach
1. Choose measures less sensitive to volume — rate and ratio measures rather than absolute counts.
2. Find a comparison group unaffected by the change where possible.
3. Compare against the pre-change trend, not a single pre-change point.
4. State the confounders explicitly rather than claiming attribution you cannot support.

## Deliverables
- An evaluation stating what can and cannot be attributed to the change
- Measures normalised for volume

## Tools
- Power BI or Tableau for trend analysis
- SQL for cohort comparison
- Excel for benchmarking

## Common Pitfall
Claiming attribution from a before-and-after comparison. If anything else changed in the same window, the comparison cannot separate the effects and an overclaim will eventually be found out.

## Interview Angle
Naming a confounder unprompted is a strong signal. It shows you distinguish measurement from proof.
