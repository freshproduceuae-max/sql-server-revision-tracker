# CS02 — Apex Commercial Developments Ltd | Commercial Real Estate | Intermediate

---

## Overview

This case study introduces development finance, which differs fundamentally from trading company lending. Analysts must construct and appraise a development model, assess Gross Development Value (GDV) with appropriate yield analysis, understand the mechanics of an interest roll, and assess the post-completion exit risk. This case features a mixed-use commercial scheme in Manchester — Grade A offices and ground-floor retail — at a time when the office market faces structural uncertainty post-COVID. Key skills: development appraisal, LTV/LTC calculation, loan-to-cost waterfall, interest reserve modelling, and understanding of planning and construction risk.

### Interest Roll Calculation

The development loan is structured as interest-rolled (no cash pay during construction — interest accrues and is added to the loan balance, paid on exit at refinance/sale).

| Parameter                        | Value     |
|----------------------------------|-----------|
| Loan Facility                    | £18,000k  |
| Drawdown Profile                 | S-curve over 28 months (see table below) |
| Interest Rate                    | SONIA + 350bps (current SONIA 5.20%, total ~8.70%) |
| Interest Calculation             | Monthly on drawn balance (not on full facility) |

**Assumed Drawdown Profile (£'000 cumulative):**

| Month   | 0     | 3     | 6     | 9     | 12    | 15    | 18    | 21    | 24    | 27    | 28    |
|---------|-------|-------|-------|-------|-------|-------|-------|-------|-------|-------|-------|
| Cumulative Drawn | 270 | 2,800 | 5,600 | 8,400 | 11,200 | 13,800 | 15,800 | 17,000 | 17,800 | 18,000 | 18,000 |

**Rolled Interest (approximate calculation):**
- Average balance over 28 months: approximately £11,500k (weighted average S-curve)
- Monthly interest rate: 8.70% / 12 = 0.725%
- Interest per month on average balance: £11,500k × 0.725% = £83k/month
- Total 28 months: £83k × 28 = **£2,324k**
- *Note: appraisal uses £2,140k — slight underestimate; analyst should flag this. Sensitivity: +100bps rate increase adds approximately £161k to interest roll.*

**Critical Observation on Developer's Profit:**  
A developer's profit of 3.8% on cost is inadequate for a development of this scale and risk. Minimum acceptable levels for commercial schemes are typically 15–20% on cost. The thin profit implies:
1. Potential error in appraisal (costs underestimated, GDV overestimated)
2. The scheme only makes sense as a strategic loss-leader (unlikely for a commercial developer)
3. The appraisal inputs should be challenged vigorously — particularly construction costs (have they risen since 2022 tender?) and yield assumptions

## Cash Flow Analysis Exercise

Development finance is monitored through a loan monitoring process. The analyst must model:

**Loan Drawdown Schedule (instructed by Project Monitoring Surveyor):**
- Drawdowns occur monthly upon certification by Quantity Surveyor and Project Monitor
- Bank withholds 5% retention until practical completion
- Interest accrues on all drawn amounts (including rolled interest)
- Facility expires at month 33 (28 months construction + 5 months marketing / letting / sale)

**Exit Cash Flow Waterfall (on sale/refinance at practical completion):**

| Item                             | £'000    |
|----------------------------------|----------|
| Sale Proceeds (GDV)              | 30,700   |
| Less: Agency / Legal Costs (1.5%)| (461)    |
| **Net Sale Proceeds**            | 30,239   |
| Less: Repayment of Development Loan + Rolled Interest | (20,140) |
| Less: Repayment of Bridging Loan (now repaid via DL drawdown) | — |
| **Net Proceeds to Developer**    | 10,099   |
| Less: Equity Invested (exc. land, already counted) | (3,063) |
| **Developer's Net Profit**       | 7,036    |

*Note discrepancy: appraisal shows £1.2M profit but waterfall shows £7M. This is because the waterfall credits the £6.46M sunk equity (land) back as a return of capital. The developer's PROFIT (not return of equity) on this basis would be approximately £576k after accounting for tax and working capital. The key metric for the bank is: can the loan be repaid from GDV proceeds? Yes — loan balance at exit (£18M + £2.14M interest roll) = £20.14M; GDV = £30.7M; buffer = £10.56M (34.4% of GDV).*

- Last 3 years audited accounts for Broadhurst

## Industry Context

**UK Commercial Office Market (2023/2024):**
- Prime Grade A office demand in Manchester remains strong; vacancy in prime locations sub-5%
- Rental values: Manchester city centre prime ERV £32–36/sq ft (FL1-FL6); consistent with appraisal
- Investment yields: prime Manchester offices have shifted from 4.75% (pre-2022) to approximately 5.75–6.25% (2024)
- Key risk: yield used in appraisal (6.0%) is at the optimistic end of the current market; 6.25% would reduce GDV to £27.5M and tip the loan into breach of LTV at maturity
- Retail market: ground-floor retail in mixed-use schemes performing well in Manchester where F&B demand is strong; 7.0% yield assumption is reasonable for this location

**Construction Cost Environment:**
- Construction costs peaked in 2022; BCIS General Building Cost Index stabilising in 2024
- However, costs remain 25-30% above 2019 levels in real terms
- Labour shortages persist; subcontractor margins are elevated
- The Broadhurst contract price should be verified against an independent QS report

## ECL Calculation (IFRS 9)

**Stage Assessment:** Stage 1 (12-month ECL) — no indicators of default or SICR at drawdown.

**Development Loan ECL Parameters:**
- PD (12-month): 4.0% — development finance is inherently higher risk than plain vanilla term lending; incomplete scheme has no income to service debt
- LGD: 25% — strong security package; first charge over site with significant equity buffer; assignment of building contract provides step-in rights
- EAD: £18,000k (full facility — worst case all drawn on default)
- **12-month ECL = 4.0% × 25% × £18,000k = £180k**

**Lifetime ECL (if Stage 2 — SICR triggered):**
- PD (lifetime, 33 months): 12%
- LGD: 30% (slightly higher for partially completed scheme)
- EAD: £18,000k
- **Lifetime ECL = 12% × 30% × £18,000k = £648k**

**SICR Triggers:**
- GDV falls below £27,700k on independent valuation
- Practical completion delayed beyond month 30
- Broadhurst becomes insolvent
- Pre-letting covenant breached and not remedied within 60 days

## Stress Test

### Scenario 1: Yield Shift (Offices 6.0% → 6.75%, Retail 7.0% → 7.50%)
| Item                    | Base Case  | Stressed    | Change     |
|-------------------------|------------|-------------|------------|
| Office Capital Value    | £28,600k   | £25,422k    | –£3,178k   |

| Loan to GDV             | 58.6%      | 65.8%       | **Breach of 65% covenant** |

This stress scenario is not extreme — a 75bps yield shift is entirely plausible in the current environment. The loan breaches the LTV covenant. The borrower would need to inject approximately £750k of additional equity to restore compliance.

### Scenario 2: Delayed Practical Completion (+6 Months to Month 34)
- Additional rolled interest: £83k/month × 6 = £498k additional interest
- Pre-let lapses (HoTs have Q4 2026 deadline): loss of 22,000 sq ft pre-let
- Total unleased offices now 52,000 sq ft; letting risk materially increased

- Developer's profit further eroded

### Scenario 3: Construction Cost Overrun (+15%)
- Main contract overrun: £16,200k × 15% = £2,430k additional cost
- Contingency (£1,296k) partially covers; net additional cost = £1,134k
- Developer must fund from own resources or request additional facility
- If no additional equity injected, LTC rises to 67.4% (within policy but tighter)

### Scenario 4: Combination Stress (Yield Shift + Cost Overrun + Pre-Let Lapse)
- GDV: £27,372k (6.75% office yield)
- Total development cost: £30,657k (after overrun)
- **Developer's profit: NEGATIVE (–£3,285k)**
- Loan repayable: £20,140k vs GDV £27,372k — loan can still be repaid (LTV 73.5%) but developer makes a loss; bank's security position degrades

## Decision and Rationale

**Recommendation: DECLINE AS PRESENTED — REQUEST REVISED APPRAISAL OR ADDITIONAL EQUITY**

**Primary Reasons:**
1. **Developer's profit of 3.8% is wholly inadequate.** Standard minimum is 15-20%. This indicates either the appraisal is materially incorrect (costs underestimated, GDV overestimated) or the scheme is not viable on these terms.
2. **Yield sensitivity is extreme.** A 75bps yield shift — plausible in current market — causes an LTV covenant breach without any construction overrun.
3. **Interest roll underestimated.** The appraisal uses £2,140k but our calculation shows £2,324k minimum; at higher rates, this increases further.
4. **Pre-letting at 42% is below the preferred 50% minimum** for a scheme of this size and complexity.

**Conditional Path to Approval:**
The bank could reconsider if:
- The developer provides a revised appraisal with updated construction costs (post-tender confirmation from Broadhurst)
- Pre-letting is increased to at least 50% (requires firm agreement, not just HoTs)
- Additional developer equity of £2M is injected (reducing LTV to 52% against current GDV)
- A GDV protection floor is agreed with a property hedge or pre-sale to an institutional investor

## Examiner's Notes (What to Look For)

1. **Developer's profit must be challenged.** Any analyst who accepts 3.8% without comment should be marked significantly down. This is the single most important red flag in the case.
2. **LTV and LTC should be correctly calculated.** Common error: using total cost including sunk land equity as the denominator for LTC but excluding land from the equity contribution.
3. **Interest roll must be modelled.** Candidates should attempt to calculate the roll, even approximately. Accepting the appraisal number without checking is insufficient.
4. **Office yield sensitivity must be stressed.** A 75bps shift is not a black-swan event; it is a realistic downside. The candidate must run this stress.
5. **Pre-let status is important.** HoTs are not binding; the 22,000 sq ft pre-let is not yet contracted. Analysts should note this.
6. **Contractor financial health.** Broadhurst must be credit-checked. No mention of this = incomplete analysis.
7. **Security assignment.** Assignment of building contract is critical for step-in rights. Candidates should explain why this is important.
8. **Personal guarantees: limited value.** Candidates should note that the directors' liquidity is approximately £2.3M against £18M facility — PGs add limited marginal value.
