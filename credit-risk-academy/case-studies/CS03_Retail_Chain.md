# CS03 — BrightHome Retail Ltd | Retail (Homewares) | Intermediate

---

## Overview

This case study tests the analyst's ability to handle an in-relationship credit under stress: a covenant reset request following EBITDA deterioration. The borrower is a profitable multi-site UK homewares retailer facing structural headwinds from online competition and a lease-heavy cost base. The introduction of IFRS 16 materially changes the appearance of the P&L and balance sheet, and analysts must understand how to strip out / interpret IFRS 16 impacts on key ratios. The secondary challenge is managing the covenant conversation: how to reset covenants fairly without giving away bank protection, and how to assess whether the covenant breach is a structural or cyclical issue.

## Company Background

**Legal Name:** BrightHome Retail Ltd  
**Registered Number:** 03218841  
**Registered Office:** BrightHome House, Watford Business Park, Watford, WD17 4RZ  
**Incorporated:** 1997  
**Ownership:** 74% Brighter Living Group plc (listed holding company); 26% management team (SAYE / options)  
**Employees:** 1,847 (FTE) across head office and stores  
**Sector:** SIC 47591 — Retail of furniture, lighting and household articles  
**Stores:** 45 stores; average 8,500 sq ft; mix of high street (32) and retail park (13)  
**Banking Relationship:** 9 years  
**Auditors:** Deloitte LLP  
**Legal Advisers:** DLA Piper LLP  

**Business Description:**  
BrightHome Retail Ltd operates 45 homewares and home décor stores across England and Wales, targeting the mid-market (30–55 year old homeowners, ABC1 demographic). The product range includes decorative accessories, lighting, soft furnishings, ceramics, and seasonal items. BrightHome competes primarily with Dunelm, The Range, and Next Home, as well as online players including Wayfair and Amazon.

The business grew rapidly between 2012 and 2019 on a store-expansion model, reaching peak revenue of £98M in FY2019. COVID-19 caused forced store closures in FY2020 and FY2021 but simultaneously generated a home improvement boom that limited financial damage. FY2022 saw an initial recovery but FY2023 has been materially affected by consumer spending weakness (cost-of-living pressures), online channel share growth, and a reset of consumer sentiment on discretionary homewares. The company is now a 45-store operation with a dense lease liability.

**IFRS 16 Context:** BrightHome adopted IFRS 16 for FY2020 reporting. All operating leases (predominantly store leases with an average remaining lease term of 6.2 years) are now capitalised on the balance sheet. The right-of-use (ROU) asset and associated lease liability materially affect the balance sheet, and the treatment of lease costs in the P&L (depreciation of ROU + interest on lease liability, replacing what was formerly operating lease expense) significantly inflates EBITDA vs the pre-IFRS 16 treatment.

## Financial Spreading Exercise

Key spreading tasks for this case:

1. **Identify and separately analyse IFRS 16 components.** The ROU asset (£33,890k) and lease liabilities (£25,810k current + £7,220k long-term = total £33,030k) are not financial debt in the traditional sense. However, they represent a firm obligation to make cash lease payments. Analysts must decide how to treat them.

2. **Calculate EBITDA on IFRS 16 basis vs pre-IFRS 16 basis (EBITDAR):**
   - IFRS 16 EBITDA: EBIT + owned D&A + ROU D&A = £4,740k + £1,380k + £7,110k = **£13,230k**
   - EBITDAR (pre-IFRS 16 equivalent): £13,230k – lease payments £9,520k = **£3,710k** (or alternatively EBIT pre-IFRS 16 + owned D&A = £2,330k + £1,380k = £3,710k)

3. **Calculate Net Debt on two bases:**
   - *Excluding IFRS 16 lease liabilities:* Cash (£2,140k) – RCF £10,000k – TL £4,000k = Net Debt £11,860k
   - *Including IFRS 16 lease liabilities:* Net Debt ex-leases £11,860k + Lease liabilities £33,030k = Net Debt including leases £44,890k

4. **Net Debt / EBITDA (two bases):**
   - Ex-IFRS 16 leases: £11,860k / £13,230k = 0.90x (appears fine)
   - Pre-IFRS 16 / incl. leases: £44,890k / £3,710k = 12.1x (alarming)
   - *This illustrates why analysts cannot simply apply a traditional leverage covenant to an IFRS 16 balance sheet.*

The existing RCF covenant is:
- Net Debt / EBITDA ≤ 2.5x (IFRS 16 basis, excluding lease liabilities from net debt)
- Interest Cover (EBITDA / Finance charges, IFRS 16 basis, excl. lease interest) ≥ 3.0x

**FY2023 Actual vs Covenant:**

| Interest Cover (EBIT / bank interest only) | 6.1x  | ≥ 3.0x         | PASS         |

*Surprising result — the company appears to be passing its covenants. Why is BrightHome requesting a covenant reset?*

**Investigation reveals:** The RCF agreement has a subsidiary covenant requiring minimum EBITDAR (pre-IFRS 16 equivalent) of £6,500k. BrightHome's FY2023 EBITDAR = £3,710k — this is the **covenant being breached** (EBITDAR shortfall of £2,790k). The company has approached the bank to either waive this covenant for FY2023 or reset it to a lower threshold of £2,500k.

**DSCR Assessment (cash flow basis):**
- Operating cash flow after lease payments: £2,575k
- Less: Maintenance Capex: £(1,040k)
- Free Cash Flow: £1,535k
- Bank Debt Service: Interest £780k + nil TL principal (TL is bullet maturity) + RCF commitment fee £60k = £840k
- **DSCR (bank debt service): £1,535k / £840k = 1.83x** — adequate for bank debt service alone
- But: the FY2023 dividend of £8,275k vastly exceeded free cash flow — entirely RCF-funded

## Business Risk Assessment

### SWOT Analysis

**Strengths:**
- Established brand; 26-year operating history; loyal customer base
- 45-store footprint provides national presence and brand visibility
- Gross margin of 51.5% reflects strong buying power and proprietary brands
- Online channel growing (8% of revenue) — strategic investment underway
- Parent company (Brighter Living Group plc) provides financial backstop — in principle

**Weaknesses:**
- Lease-heavy cost structure; fixed cost base is very high relative to variable revenues
- LFL sales declining; online competition eroding footfall
- RCF utilisation at 83% — limited liquidity headroom
- FY2023 £8.275M upstream dividend was irresponsible given trading environment
- Slow stock turn (141 days) creates working capital drag

**Opportunities:**
- Store rationalisation — selective closures of underperforming high street locations
- Online channel development to offset structural footfall decline
- Private label / own brand expansion to protect margins
- Cost reduction programme; lease renegotiation on renewal

**Threats:**
- Structural shift to online homewares (Wayfair, Amazon, ASOS Home, Instagram shopping)
- Consumer spending weakness — homewares is discretionary
- Landlord pressures — rent reviews, lease renewals on less favourable terms
- Competition from Next Home, Dunelm (operating at significantly lower cost base)
- Potential recession: BrightHome is highly operationally geared

### Key Business Risk Flags:
1. **IFRS 16 lease-adjusted leverage is extreme at 12.1x.** The lease commitments represent an enormous fixed cost burden.
2. **FY2023 dividend was reckless.** £8.275M extracted in a deteriorating trading year when the company needed to conserve liquidity.
3. **RCF is being used as a structural facility, not a working capital tool.** RCF utilisation growing from 33% to 83% suggests the business is using it to fund operations, not seasonal working capital peaks.
4. **Fixed Charge Cover below 1.0x** — the business cannot cover its fixed costs from EBITDAR.
5. **Online competition is structural, not cyclical.** The market share shift to online homewares is permanent.

## ECL Calculation (IFRS 9)

**Stage Assessment: Stage 2 — Significant Increase in Credit Risk (SICR)**

Reasons for Stage 2 classification:
- Covenant breach (EBITDAR covenant) — even if waived, a covenant breach is an SICR trigger
- RCF utilisation growing from 33% to 83% (above the 75% threshold used by many banks as an SICR indicator)
- Three-year deteriorating trend in EBITDAR and liquidity
- Fixed Charge Cover below 1.0x

**Stage 2 ECL (Lifetime):**
- Lifetime PD: 15% (reflecting high-risk retail sector, covenant breach, declining earnings)
- LGD: 45% (weaker recovery expected; floating charge over declining value assets; lease liabilities ahead of bank as occupancy claims in insolvency)
- EAD: £16,000k (TL £4M + RCF fully drawn £12M)
- **Lifetime ECL = 15% × 45% × £16,000k = £1,080k**

Compare vs Stage 1 (12-month):
- PD: 5%; LGD: 45%; EAD: £16,000k
- 12-month ECL = 5% × 45% × £16,000k = £360k

**ECL impact on covenant reset approval:** Approving the covenant reset does not prevent the Stage 2 classification — the SICR conditions already exist. However, a formal waiver/reset demonstrates forbearance, which must be disclosed under IFRS 9.

- If parent encounters financial difficulty and extracts further dividends or calls intercompany loans
- BrightHome balance sheet would deteriorate rapidly
- Without formal parent guarantee, bank has no recourse to parent
- **Key conclusion: A formal parent guarantee or support letter is non-negotiable**

---

## Decision and Rationale

**Recommendation: APPROVE WAIVER WITH CONDITIONS (RELUCTANTLY)**

**Rationale:**
- The covenant breach (EBITDAR) is a genuine indicator of credit deterioration, not a technical breach
- However, the bank's exposure is primarily secured against freehold properties (LTV 49%) and this provides meaningful downside protection
- BrightHome is not facing an immediate insolvency risk — it has a profitable parent (Brighter Living plc) and trading, while under pressure, is positive
- Declining a waiver may precipitate a crisis (the business may need to draw down remaining RCF availability quickly and seek refinancing in difficult market conditions)
- Conditions of approval: (a) 50bps margin step-up; (b) enhanced reporting (monthly); (c) formal dividend restriction covenant; (d) formal parent letter of support; (e) updated property valuation; (f) step-up covenant schedule; (g) Stage 2 IFRS 9 classification

**What is NOT acceptable:** Approving a waiver without conditions, without increased pricing, or without a dividend restriction covenant. That would represent the bank giving away protection for nothing in return.

## Examiner's Notes (What to Look For)

1. **Did the analyst understand IFRS 16 impacts?** This is the central technical test. Analysts who do not reconcile IFRS 16 EBITDA to EBITDAR are missing the core issue.
2. **Was the FY2023 dividend flagged?** £8.275M dividend in a year of declining EBITDA is a major red flag. Every analyst should identify and challenge this.
3. **Was the RCF utilisation trend noted?** 33% → 50% → 83% is a clear warning signal that the RCF is being used structurally.
4. **Was Fixed Charge Cover calculated?** FCCR below 1.0x on a pre-IFRS 16 basis is one of the most important findings in this case.
5. **Did the analyst request a parent company guarantee?** Without a formal guarantee from Brighter Living Group plc, the bank has no recourse to the parent despite the parent-owned structure.
6. **Was Stage 2 IFRS 9 classification proposed?** Given covenant breach and deteriorating ratios, Stage 1 classification would be incorrect.
7. **Was the covenant reset conditional on a step-up schedule?** A flat reset with no improvement pathway is not acceptable — the bank should require covenants to tighten over the waiver period.
8. **Was pricing renegotiation recommended?** The bank should seek a margin step-up as compensation for increased risk.
