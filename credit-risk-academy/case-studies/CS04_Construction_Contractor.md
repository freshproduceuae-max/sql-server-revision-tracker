# CS04 — Greenfield Civil Engineering Ltd | Infrastructure / Construction | Advanced

---

## Overview

Construction contractor lending is one of the most technically complex areas of corporate credit. This advanced case requires analysts to understand the unique accounting features of construction companies — work in progress (WIP), retentions, revenue recognition under IFRS 15, contract margin recognition, and the cash flow timing mismatches inherent in the sector. The borrower is a large UK infrastructure contractor requesting bonding facilities and an RCF. The key risks are contract concentration, thin margins, latent defect liability, aggressive revenue recognition, and the structural mismatch between when revenue is earned and when cash is received. Analysts must also understand surety bonds and guarantee facilities — a product set that many generalist credit analysts have limited exposure to.

**Critical Balance Sheet Features to Analyse:**

1. **Gross Amounts Due from Customers (Contract Assets / WIP):** £24,310k — this is the IFRS 15 contract asset representing revenue recognised under percentage-of-completion but not yet billed or certificated. In construction, this is one of the riskiest assets on the balance sheet. It is recognised based on management's assessment of contract progress and margin.

2. **Retentions Held by Clients:** £8,620k — standard in UK construction; 3–5% of contract value is withheld by the client until practical completion (and sometimes a further 12-month defects liability period). These can be very slow to be released and are at risk in client insolvency.

3. **Gross Amounts Due to Customers (Contract Liabilities):** £3,580k — these are advance billings received from clients in excess of work done to date. This is a liability (money received before the performance obligation is satisfied).

4. **Trade Creditors — Subcontractors:** £20,480k — very high relative to EBITDA. The business effectively uses its subcontractor base as a form of supply chain finance, paying them approximately 60 days late.

## Financial Spreading Exercise

Key tasks for construction contractor spreading:

1. **WIP Analysis:** The £24,310k contract asset represents approximately 20% of annual revenue (73 revenue days). Analysts should interrogate the composition of WIP:
   - How many contracts contribute?
   - What is the oldest balance? Any contract > 90 days past expected billing is a red flag.
   - What margin is embedded in WIP? If Greenfield is recognising 10% margin on the WIP value, the embedded profit is £2,431k. If contracts are loss-making, WIP should carry a provision.
   - Key question: Has the auditor reviewed the margin recognition assumptions on each material contract?

2. **Retention Analysis:** £8,620k of retentions on the balance sheet:
   - Average contract retention rate: typically 3-5% of contract value held for 12-24 months post-completion
   - Risk: if a client goes insolvent before releasing retentions, these are lost as an unsecured creditor claim
   - Retention ageing schedule should be requested

3. **Net Debt Calculation:**
   - Cash: £2,840k
   - RCF drawn: £(6,000k)
   - HP/Finance Leases: £(3,480k)
   - **Net Debt: £(6,640k)**
   - Net Debt / EBITDA: £6,640k / £6,420k = **1.03x** — appears acceptable

4. **Working Capital Intensity:**
   - Net working capital = WIP + Retentions + Trade Receivables – Trade Creditors – Contract Liabilities
   - = £24,310k + £8,620k + £16,890k – £20,480k – £3,580k = **£25,760k**
   - This is an enormous amount of working capital for a £121M revenue business — 78 days of revenue equivalent
   - The RCF is being used to fund this structural working capital requirement

## Cash Flow Analysis Exercise

**Pro Forma Debt Service — Post New Facility:**

Existing facilities:
- RCF: £8,000k limit (£6,000k drawn at FY2023) — renewal requested at £8,000k
- HP / Finance Leases: £3,480k

New facility:
- Bonding & Guarantee Facility: £20,000k limit (no drawn balance — this is a contingent liability facility)
- RCF: Increase from £8,000k to £8,000k (no increase — just renewal)

**Bond Facility Economics:**
- Bonding facilities do not typically carry interest (they are contingent liabilities until called)
- Greenfield pays a commission on issued bonds: approximately 0.75% per annum on issued bond value
- Current bonds outstanding: £14,200k (per note 12 of accounts)
- Annual commission: £14,200k × 0.75% = £106.5k per annum
- If the full £20M facility is utilised, annual commission = £150k

**Effective Pro Forma Debt Service:**
- RCF interest (£6,000k @ SONIA + 250bps ~8.45%): £507k
- HP/Finance Lease payments: £1,240k (principal + interest)
- Bond commission: £106.5k
- **Total Pro Forma Debt Service: £1,854k**
- DSCR = £6,420k / £1,854k = **3.46x** — comfortable

*However: the DSCR calculation above excludes dividends (£1,800k). If dividends are included in the payout (which is the effective claim on cash flow), the ratio is: (£6,420k – capex £2,490k – dividends £1,800k) / £1,854k = **1.15x** — very tight.*

## Industry Context

**UK Infrastructure Contracting Market (2023/2024):**
- Government's National Infrastructure and Construction Pipeline (NICP): £600Bn+ planned investment 2023–2033
- AMP8 water investment cycle: £96Bn planned 2025–2030 (versus £60Bn in AMP7)
- Highways: National Roads Investment Strategy remains broadly intact despite government spending reviews
- Sector structural issues: margins are structurally thin across all tiers of contractor
- Material cost inflation: steel, aggregate, and fuel costs 20-30% above 2019 levels
- Labour: shortage of skilled civil engineers and site workers post-Brexit; wage inflation 8-12% p.a.
- Key macro risk: infrastructure spending cuts in fiscal consolidation scenarios
- Peer group: Balfour Beatty (PBT margin ~2-3% on civil works); Kier Civil Engineering; Galliford Try

**Surety Bond Market:**
- Performance bonds: typically 10% of contract value; issued by surety (insurance) or bank
- Advance payment bonds: required when client pays upfront; protects client if contractor fails
- Retention money bonds: substitute for cash retention — client releases cash in exchange for bond
- Banks issue bonds as part of a bonding and guarantee facility; these are off-balance sheet contingent liabilities
- If a bond is called (i.e., the contractor defaults on the underlying contract), the bank pays the beneficiary and seeks reimbursement from Greenfield

## Stress Test

### Scenario 1: National Highways Framework Lost at Re-Tendering (30% Revenue Lost)
- Revenue falls to £84,973k
- Gross profit (assuming same margin %): £8,582k
- EBITDA (assuming overheads rationalised by 20% after 12 months): £3,500k
- Net Debt / EBITDA: £6,640k / £3,500k = **1.90x** — covenant headroom (max 2.5x)
- However, EBITDA covenant (£5,500k minimum): **BREACH** — immediate covenant breach

### Scenario 2: Significant Contract Loss / Cost Overrun (One £15M Contract, 15% Overrun)
- Contract cost overrun: £15M × 15% = £2,250k — immediate hit to EBITDA
- EBITDA falls to: £6,420k – £2,250k = **£4,170k** — below £5,500k covenant — **BREACH**
- Potential WIP write-down: if the contract was being accounted for at 10% margin, a loss situation requires all expected losses to be recognised immediately (IFRS 15 / IAS 37)
- Additional WIP provision: e.g., if £5M WIP on this contract was carried at 10% margin, immediate loss recognition could add another £750k hit
- Combined EBITDA impact: £4,170k – £750k = **£3,420k**

### Scenario 3: Bond Called by Client (£5M Bond)
- Greenfield fails to complete a contract; National Highways calls a £5M performance bond
- Bank pays out £5M to National Highways; creates a loan to Greenfield
- Greenfield's balance sheet: additional loan of £5M; total net debt = £11,640k; Net Debt/EBITDA = 1.81x (within covenant but RCF also likely fully drawn)
- Reputational damage to Greenfield: may be disqualified from future public sector frameworks

### Scenario 4: Subcontractor Chain Collapse
- Several key subcontractors become insolvent simultaneously (realistic in a construction downturn)
- Greenfield must engage replacement contractors at significantly higher rates
- Cost overruns across multiple projects: EBITDA impact of £3M–£4M
- WIP restatement required; potential for goodwill impairment on sub-contracted elements

## Decision and Rationale

**Recommendation: APPROVE — WITH ENHANCED MONITORING AND ADDITIONAL CONDITIONS**

**Rationale:**
- The bonding facility is a contingent liability facility (not funded debt); the credit risk is the counter-indemnity, not a direct loan
- Greenfield's financial performance is consistent and the management team is experienced
- Framework agreements provide revenue visibility; AMP8 water investment cycle is a structural tailwind
- Financial leverage is modest (Net Debt/EBITDA 1.03x) despite working capital intensity
- However: cash conversion is poor, margin is thin, and concentration risk is high

**Conditions:**
- Independent WIP review by KPMG (confirm margin recognition assumptions before drawdown)
- Updated retention ageing schedule showing no retention > 18 months outstanding
- National Highways framework documents to be provided and reviewed
- Quarterly management accounts with contract-level WIP and margin disclosure
- Cross-guarantee from Greenfield Holdings Ltd confirmed and valued (need Holdings balance sheet)
- Maximum bond facility drawdown of £15M in Year 1 until national Highways framework formally documented

## Examiner's Notes (What to Look For)

1. **WIP is the central analytical challenge.** Analysts must explain what WIP is, how it arises under IFRS 15, and why it is risky (judgemental, potential for overstatement).
2. **Cash conversion must be calculated.** OCF/EBITDA of 48% should be identified and explained.
3. **Retentions — what are they?** Analysts should define retentions, note the £8,620k balance, and question their recoverability (client insolvency risk; ageing).
4. **Bond facility — how does it work?** Analysts must explain that the bond facility is a contingent liability — not funded debt — and that the primary risk is the counter-indemnity being called.
5. **Contract concentration risk.** National Highways at 30% should be explicitly flagged as a concentration risk. What happens at re-tendering?
6. **ECL for contingent liabilities.** The calculation must acknowledge the distinction between funded and contingent exposure.
7. **Quick Ratio.** The quick ratio (excluding WIP and retentions) of 0.58x should be identified as a potential liquidity concern.
8. **Subcontractor creditor stretch.** £20.48M trade creditors (predominantly sub-contractors) at 62 days — analysts should note this is on the edge of what subs will tolerate.
