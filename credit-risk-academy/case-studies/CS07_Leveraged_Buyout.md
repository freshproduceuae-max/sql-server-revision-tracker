## Overview

Leveraged buyout (LBO) credit is the most complex area of corporate credit analysis. An LBO involves a private equity firm acquiring a company using primarily debt financing, creating a highly leveraged capital structure that is repaid through the acquired company's cash flows over a 4–7 year investment horizon. The analyst must model the full LBO — acquisition price, sources and uses of funds, debt waterfall, projected cash flows, DSCR, and exit analysis. Key expert skills tested: LBO mechanics, Term Loan B (TLB) features (covenant-lite, PIK toggle, portability), intercreditor agreements, mezzanine ranking, PE sponsor incentive alignment, and how to assess highly leveraged credits where traditional credit metrics are stretched far beyond normal acceptable levels. This case is designed to challenge experienced analysts and should provoke a robust debate about whether such a transaction should be approved.

## Business Risk Assessment

### Logistics Sector Risk Analysis

**1. Cyclicality**
UK road freight is closely correlated with GDP and consumer spending. A recession reduces both B2C and B2B freight volumes. Vertex's key customers are food retailers (relatively defensive) and e-commerce platforms (partially defensive — consumers trade down but still buy online). However, food retailer volumes could still decline in a severe recession, and e-commerce growth rates can slow materially.

**2. Fuel Cost Exposure**
Fuel is a major cost. Vertex's contracts include fuel escalation clauses (linked to DERV price index) — this provides partial protection. However, escalation clauses typically have a 30-60 day lag and cap the pass-through to clients. A sudden fuel price spike is immediately costly.

**3. Driver Shortages / Labour Inflation**
Post-Brexit, UK HGV driver shortages are structural. Driver wage inflation has been 8-12% per annum since 2021. Vertex employs 680 drivers. A 5% driver wage increase = approximately £2.0M additional cost (impacting EBITDA by 7-8%).

**4. E-commerce Last-Mile Competition**
Amazon's own logistics network (Amazon Logistics UK) is increasingly competitive for last-mile. If Amazon in-sources delivery further, Vertex's two largest e-commerce customers may reduce contracted volume.

**5. Asset Intensity**
The road freight business requires significant ongoing capital investment (truck fleet replacement cycle approximately 7 years). Maintenance capex is substantial (assumed 3% of revenue). The analyst must satisfy themselves that 3% of revenue is genuinely maintenance capex and not understated.

**6. Integration Risk**
Aries' thesis involves bolt-on acquisitions on top of Vertex. This creates integration risk and potential for goodwill impairment if acquisitions are overpriced. More concerning for the bank: additional acquisitions may require additional debt (M&A accordion in the credit agreement — see covenant discussion).

## Covenant-Lite Structure — Key Legal Features

This is a **Covenant-Lite (Cov-Lite)** Term Loan B. Understanding cov-lite is essential at expert level.

**What Cov-Lite Means:**
- **No maintenance financial covenants on the TLB.** Traditional loans test leverage, DSCR, and interest cover quarterly. A cov-lite TLB has NO such quarterly tests on the term loan itself.
- **One springing covenant on the RCF only:** Senior Secured Net Leverage Ratio (SSNLR = Senior Secured Net Debt / EBITDA) ≤ 6.5x. This covenant is "springing" — it only tests when the RCF is drawn by more than 40% (>£6M drawn).
- **Incurrence covenants** apply: the borrower must comply with leverage tests only when taking on NEW debt or making restricted payments (dividends, acquisitions). This is far less restrictive than maintenance covenants.
- **Bank's position in cov-lite:** The bank has significantly less ability to monitor the credit and take early action. The first formal sign of distress may be a payment default (missed interest or principal), by which time the business may already be severely impaired.

**PIK Toggle:**
- The TLB has a PIK toggle option: if EBITDA falls below a threshold, the borrower may elect to pay interest in PIK (additional notes) rather than cash. This prevents a cash payment default but increases total debt.
- PIK election is typically available twice over the life of the loan, for a maximum of 4 interest payment periods (2 years equivalent).
- PIK toggle adds approximately 1-2% per annum when elected (to compensate lenders for deferred cash).

**Portability:**
- The TLB is portable — it can be assumed by a new buyer of Vertex if the leverage test is met (SSNLR ≤ 5.5x). This is a feature that benefits the PE sponsor (allows them to sell without requiring a refinancing) but may disadvantage lenders if the new buyer is a less creditworthy entity.

## Intercreditor Agreement

**The intercreditor agreement (ICA) establishes the ranking and rights of each creditor:**

| Creditor             | Security Ranking | Cash Pay Priority | Vote Priority in Default | Enforcement Rights |
|----------------------|------------------|-------------------|--------------------------|--------------------|
| RCF Lenders (super senior) | First lien on all assets | First (ahead of TLB) | First (can block enforcement for 90 days) | Immediate |
| TLB Lenders (senior secured) | First lien (pari passu with RCF for security, but subordinated for payment) | Second | Second | After standstill |
| Mezzanine Lenders (second lien) | Second lien on all assets | Third | Can only vote when senior defaulted | 90-day standstill post-senior default |
| Seller Note | Unsecured subordinated | Fourth | PIK; very limited rights | No enforcement while TLB/Mezz outstanding |
| Equity (Aries + Management) | Residual claim | Last | Shareholders meeting only | Post-insolvency only |

**Standstill Provisions:**
- Mezz lenders cannot enforce their second lien security until 90 days after a senior default event
- During the 90-day standstill, senior lenders can pursue enforcement without mezz lenders blocking
- After 90 days, if senior lenders have not enforced, mezz lenders may act

**Key ICA Risk for the Bank:**
As a TLB lender, the bank must understand that the RCF (if fully drawn) ranks ahead of the TLB for payment. In a distress scenario where the waterfall matters, TLB lenders recover only after RCF lenders. If the security realisations are insufficient to repay all creditors, TLB lenders (the bank's primary exposure) bear the bulk of the loss.

## ECL Calculation (IFRS 9)

**Stage Assessment: Stage 1 at origination — but SICR management is critical**

**IFRS 9 Note — Leveraged Lending Challenge:**  
Many banks classify highly leveraged credits (>5x leverage) as Stage 2 at origination on the grounds that the SICR threshold (significant increase in credit risk vs a "normal" counterparty) is already breached. Banks must have clear policies on this.

**Stage 1 ECL (12-month — if bank classifies as Stage 1):**
- PD (12-month): 5.0% (leveraged buyout; thin DSCR; cov-lite reduces early warning)
- LGD: 45% (tangible asset coverage below loan; goodwill has no realisable value; second lien recovery uncertain)
- EAD: £85,000k (TLB full drawn amount)
- **12-month ECL = 5.0% × 45% × £85,000k = £1,913k**

**Stage 2 ECL (Lifetime — if classified Stage 2 at origination):**
- Lifetime PD (7 years): 22% (consistent with B/B- rating over a 7-year TLB life)
- LGD: 45%
- EAD: £85,000k
- **Lifetime ECL = 22% × 45% × £85,000k = £8,415k**

**This ECL difference is material.** The classification decision at origination significantly impacts P&L. Banks' leveraged lending teams frequently debate this and it is a key area of regulatory scrutiny (PRA, EBA guidelines on LBO lending).

## Stress Test

### Scenario 1: Revenue Contraction –8% Year 1 (Mild Recession)
- Revenue: £191,762k
- EBITDA at 11.0% margin: £21,094k (margin contracts slightly as fixed costs are high)
- Cash DS: £15,179k
- DSCR: £21,094k / £15,179k = **1.39x** (adjusted for £5.7M maintenance capex and lease interest)
- Revised DSCR (adjusted): (£21,094k – £5,753k – £1,840k) / £15,179k = **0.89x** — DSCR COVENANT BREACH (if applicable)
- Under cov-lite: no DSCR covenant on TLB — breach not technically triggered; bank has limited recourse
- SSNLR (TLB only £83.15M / £21,094k): 3.94x — below the springing covenant (6.5x) but RCF likely undrawn so covenant not tested

### Scenario 2: Driver Wage Inflation +10% (Two Years Sustained)
- Additional cost Year 1: 680 drivers × average £40k salary × 10% = £2.72M
- EBITDA falls: £24,596k – £2,720k = £21,876k
- DSCR (adjusted): (£21,876k – £6,253k – £1,840k) / £15,179k = **0.91x** — breach of underlying debt service capacity

### Scenario 3: E-commerce Client Exits (25% Revenue Loss)
- Revenue: £156,328k
- EBITDA at 10% margin: £15,633k (margin compression as volume falls and fixed costs don't move proportionally)
- DSCR: Extremely stressed; free cash flow turns negative
- Conclusion: A significant volume loss from one e-commerce customer is potentially fatal for the debt structure
- This risk should be a condition of approval: contractual stability / minimum volume guarantees from e-commerce customers

### Scenario 4: Oil/Fuel Spike (+40% DERV, contracts pass-through lags 60 days)
- Additional fuel cost (unhedged 60-day lag): ~£5.2M
- EBITDA: £24,596k – £5,200k = £19,396k
- DSCR (adjusted): ~0.78x — significant shortfall

**Stress Test Conclusion:** The credit has very thin margins for error in Year 1 (DSCR 1.09x). Multiple mild stress scenarios bring DSCR below 1.0x. The cov-lite structure means the bank cannot rely on covenant triggers to force a discussion with the sponsor early. The RCF drawdown is likely to be used as a buffer, but £15M is limited headroom against £200M+ of cost base.

## Decision and Rationale

**Recommendation: APPROVE SUBJECT TO SIGNIFICANT CONDITIONS AND CREDIT COMMITTEE ESCALATION**

*Note: This case is intentionally designed so that a reasonable analyst could conclude either "approve" or "decline." The examiner should accept either recommendation if adequately supported.*

**Arguments for approval:**
- Aries Capital Partners is a credible PE sponsor with a track record
- UK 3PL sector is supported by e-commerce growth; defensive food retailer exposure provides some protection
- Management team is strong; EBITDA margin improvement is realistic (not extreme)
- Freehold property (£42M) provides tangible security even if goodwill is zero
- The cov-lite structure is now market standard for TLBs; declining this deal purely on cov-lite grounds would be a market positioning issue (other banks would take the deal)
- DSCR improves materially from Year 2 if operational improvements are achieved

**Arguments for decline:**
- Year 1 DSCR of 1.09x is dangerously thin — effectively zero margin for error
- Leverage of 5.66x on bank normalised EBITDA (7.32x including leases) is beyond acceptable for a cyclical sector
- Cov-lite structure means the bank cannot act until a payment default — by which time recovery value may be severely impaired
- EBITDA normalisation: the difference between reported (£17.98M) and Aries' pro forma (£28.6M) is extreme; the bank is effectively betting on synergies that have not been delivered
- E-commerce customer concentration (one customer potentially 20%+ of revenue) is unmitigated
- EV fleet transition represents an unquantified long-term capex risk
- Total debt including PIK accrual grows over time — even without operational deterioration, total debt increases each year

**Credit Committee Escalation is required** (most institutions have a mandatory escalation for: leverage > 5.0x; cov-lite; PIK instruments; acquisition financing above a size threshold). This case triggers all four.

**Minimum conditions if approved:**
1. E-commerce customer contract review — evidence of contractual stability and volume guarantees
2. Fuel hedging programme — minimum 50% of fuel forecast hedged for 12 months
3. Management accounts monthly for Year 1 (vs standard quarterly)
4. Confirmation that maintenance capex assumption (3% of revenue) has been stress-tested by independent consultant
5. ICA reviewed and confirmed; standstill provisions and enforcement rights clearly understood by bank's legal team

## Examiner's Notes (What to Look For)

1. **EBITDA normalisation must be performed.** Analysts who accept Aries' £28.6M at face value have not done the work. The bank must use its own normalised EBITDA.
2. **Year 1 DSCR of 1.09x must be identified and commented upon.** This is the central risk metric and it is very thin.
3. **Cov-lite must be explained.** Analysts must explain what cov-lite means, how it differs from a maintenance covenant structure, and why it increases the bank's risk.
4. **The intercreditor agreement must be understood.** Analysts should explain the debt waterfall and the standstill provision — and what they mean for the bank's recovery position.
5. **PIK toggle and PIK seller note must be addressed.** Total debt grows over time with PIK — this must be modelled and commented upon.
6. **The LBO model must be built or referenced correctly.** Analysts should show revenue projections, EBITDA, debt service, and DSCR for at least three years.
7. **IFRS 9 classification must be addressed.** The Stage 1 vs Stage 2 debate is important and expected at expert level.
8. **Credit committee escalation should be proposed.** Leveraged credits almost always require senior credit committee sign-off; analysts who treat this as a standard bilateral credit have missed the institutional dimension.
9. **PE sponsor incentives.** The IRR analysis (14.4% base case vs 20-25% target) implies the PE sponsor needs to outperform — which creates risk for the bank. Analysts should comment on this.
10. **Goodwill impairment risk.** If Vertex fails to perform, the £124.2M of goodwill will be impaired — destroying equity but not immediately triggering a payment default. Analysts should note that negative equity (after goodwill impairment) does not constitute a default event under the TLB terms.
