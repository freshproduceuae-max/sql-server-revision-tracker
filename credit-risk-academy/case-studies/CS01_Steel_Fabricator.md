# CS01 — Midlands Steel Fabrications Ltd | Manufacturing (Steel) | Beginner

---

## Overview

This case study introduces the foundational credit analysis skills required for a straightforward mid-market UK manufacturing credit. Analysts are expected to spread financials, compute key ratios, assess business risk, structure a facility, and produce a credit recommendation. The borrower exhibits classic signs of margin pressure from input cost inflation and import competition, creating a manageable but real credit story. The beginner analyst must identify the key risks, mitigants, and covenants appropriate for this type of lending.

---

## Company Background

**Legal Name:** Midlands Steel Fabrications Ltd  
**Registered Number:** 04521887  
**Registered Office:** Unit 14, Forge Industrial Estate, Wolverhampton, WV2 3BQ  
**Incorporated:** 2001  
**Ownership:** 100% owned by Derek Ashford (founder/Managing Director), aged 58  
**Employees:** 142 full-time, 18 part-time  
**Sector:** SIC 25110 — Manufacture of metal structures and parts  
**Banking Relationship:** 7 years with Midlands Commercial Bank  
**Auditors:** Harrison & Cole LLP, Birmingham  
**Legal Advisers:** Trent Solicitors LLP  

**Business Description:**  
Midlands Steel Fabrications Ltd (MSF) manufactures structural steel components, bespoke fabrications, and modular steel frames for the UK construction, industrial, and energy sectors. Founded by Derek Ashford in 2001 with two plasma cutters and a 4,000 sq ft workshop, the business now operates from a 35,000 sq ft facility owned by the company (acquired 2008, valued £3.2M in 2023 RICS survey).

The company's order book is typically 3–5 months forward, driven by framework agreements with two Tier-1 construction contractors (accounting for approximately 55% of revenue). The remainder of revenue comes from direct industrial clients (25%) and spot/one-off fabrication orders (20%).

MSF has invested in CNC plasma cutting and robotic welding equipment over the past decade but faces increasing pressure from Chinese and Eastern European fabricators offering comparable quality at significantly lower prices. Derek Ashford manages the business day-to-day with the support of a Production Director (employed 12 years) and a Finance Manager (employed 4 years). There is no formal succession plan. Derek has indicated he intends to retire in approximately 8–10 years and has a son who works in the business in a junior capacity.

## Financial Spreading Exercise

Analysts should spread the financials above into their institution's standard spreading template. Key tasks:

1. Normalise EBITDA — identify any one-off items (none reported here, but note energy spike in FY2022 may be treated as partly exceptional).
2. Identify the classification of finance lease obligations: under IFRS 16 / FRS 102 (small company), all HP/lease obligations appear on balance sheet. In this case MSF reports under FRS 102 (small/medium entities) so treatment may differ from large-company IFRS 16 approach.
3. Confirm that trade receivables are gross and that bad debt provisions are disclosed in the notes (assume £65k provision in FY2023).
4. Restate the balance sheet for a net debt calculation:
   - Cash: £710k
   - RCF drawn: £(900k)
   - Term Loan: £(900k)
   - Finance Leases: £(1,040k)
   - **Net Debt: £(2,130k)**
5. Calculate Net Debt / EBITDA on restated basis: £2,130k / £1,670k = **1.27x** (FY2023)

## Business Risk Assessment

### SWOT Analysis

**Strengths:**
- Long-established business (22 years), strong local reputation
- Owner-managed with skin in the game; personal guarantee offered
- Freehold property provides tangible security with meaningful LTV
- Framework agreements with Tier-1 contractors provide revenue visibility
- CNC/robotic investment gives some productivity edge over smaller rivals

**Weaknesses:**
- Key-man risk: Derek Ashford, aged 58, no formal succession plan
- Margin compression from energy and input cost inflation
- Revenue concentration: 55% from two contractors
- Single-site operations; no geographic diversification
- No hedging of steel or energy costs

**Opportunities:**
- Government infrastructure pipeline (HS2 supply chain, offshore wind)
- Energy cost normalisation post-2023 could restore margins
- Potential export of specialist fabrications (niche opportunities)
- Management Buy-Out (MBO) by Production Director could resolve succession

**Threats:**
- Chinese and Eastern European import competition intensifying
- UK construction slowdown in residential/commercial reducing demand
- Energy price volatility (business runs energy-intensive plasma cutting)
- Trade finance tightening for UK SME manufacturers
- Potential decline in UK steel industry overall

### Key Risk Flags:
1. **Key-man risk** — Derek Ashford is the only director. Key-man life insurance should be assigned to the bank.
2. **Energy cost risk** — FY2022 demonstrated vulnerability. No hedging programme in place.
3. **Customer concentration** — Two clients = 55% revenue. Loss of one would be material.
4. **Succession risk** — business value heavily tied to the founder's relationships and technical knowledge.
5. **Margin trajectory** — has not recovered to pre-2022 levels; new debt increases vulnerability.

## Industry Context

**UK Steel Fabrication Market (2023):**
- UK structural steel market: approximately £4.2Bn revenue
- Market structure: fragmented, with Severfield and BHC at the top, hundreds of SMEs below
- Input cost dynamics: steel prices fell significantly H2 2023 after 2022 peak; energy prices declining from 2022 highs but above pre-2021 levels
- Demand drivers: UK construction output, infrastructure spending (positively exposed to govt capex), industrial capex
- Import competition: Chinese fabrications, primarily for standard sections, undercutting on price. Bespoke/specialist fabrications remain domestically sourced
- Outlook: cautious optimism — infrastructure pipeline supports demand but margin pressure continues

**Regulatory environment:** UK REACH, environmental permits for cutting/welding. No immediate regulatory threats identified.

## ECL Calculation (IFRS 9)

**Stage Assessment:**  
Current credit quality is performing (Stage 1) — no past-due amounts, no deterioration in credit quality since origination. However, the margin compression and DSCR pressure post-new-facility should be flagged as a potential SICR (Significant Increase in Credit Risk) trigger.

**SICR Triggers to Monitor:**
- DSCR falls below 1.20x covenant
- Three consecutive months of RCF at or near limit
- Adverse change in order book (>15% reduction)

**Stage 1 ECL (12-month):**
- Probability of Default (PD, 12-month): 2.5% (based on internal rating 4/10, manufacturing sector)
- Loss Given Default (LGD): 35% (considering freehold security, personal guarantee, expected recovery)
- Exposure at Default (EAD): £7,000k (assuming full TL drawn, RCF fully drawn for stressed EAD)
- **ECL = PD × LGD × EAD = 2.5% × 35% × £7,000k = £61.25k**

**Note:** If business deteriorates and is reclassified Stage 2 (SICR), lifetime PD applies (assume 12% over 5-year horizon) and ECL would increase to approximately £294k.

## Stress Test

### Scenario 1: Energy Cost Re-Spike (Back to FY2022 Levels)
- EBITDA falls to £1,470k
- Pro Forma DSCR: £1,470k / £1,878k = **0.78x** — covenant breach
- Action: Covenant breach triggers review; bank would seek short-term waiver and restructuring discussion

### Scenario 2: Loss of One Tier-1 Framework Customer (25% Revenue Lost)
- Revenue falls to £18,765k
- Assume EBITDA margin holds at 6.7%: EBITDA = £1,257k
- Pro Forma DSCR: £1,257k / £1,878k = **0.67x** — significant breach
- Action: Very material stress; security and guarantee become important

### Scenario 3: Mild Recession — Revenue -10%, Margin -1%
- Revenue: £22,518k
- EBITDA at 5.7%: £1,284k
- Pro Forma DSCR: £1,284k / £1,878k = **0.68x** — breach
- Action: Demonstrates limited headroom; mitigant is security quality

**Stress Test Conclusion:** The proposed facility has very limited headroom under adverse scenarios. The analyst should note this in the credit memo and consider whether the £5M TL is appropriate, or whether a smaller facility or phased drawdown (e.g., tranches linked to equipment delivery/installation milestones) would be more prudent.

## Decision and Rationale

**Recommendation: APPROVE WITH CONDITIONS AND ENHANCED MONITORING**

**Rationale:**
- MSF is an established business with a 22-year track record and a 7-year banking relationship
- Freehold property (£3.2M) and personal guarantee provide meaningful downside protection
- The investment in new equipment is strategically sound and necessary to maintain competitiveness
- DSCR is tight on a pro-forma basis but relies on a modest and achievable recovery in EBITDA margins (from 6.7% to 8.5%) as energy costs normalise
- Phased drawdown should be considered: Tranche 1 (£3M) on signing; Tranche 2 (£2M) subject to delivery and commissioning of equipment
- Covenants provide adequate early warning triggers
- Key conditions: key-man insurance, personal guarantee, updated property valuation, management accounts quarterly

**Credit Grade: 4 / Pass — Standard monitoring**

## Examiner's Notes (What to Look For)

1. **Did the analyst identify the DSCR breach on a pro-forma basis?** This is the critical finding. A beginner who overlooks this should be marked down.
2. **Did the analyst correctly calculate Net Debt including HP/finance leases?** Many beginners forget to include lease obligations in net debt.
3. **Was key-man risk identified and a mitigant proposed?** Key-man insurance and personal guarantee are both required.
4. **Did the analyst question the dividend policy?** Paying £400k dividends while DSCR is under pressure is a red flag.
5. **Was the margin deterioration properly contextualised?** The energy cost spike is largely external but the analyst should note margins have not fully recovered.
6. **Did the analyst propose a covenant package?** Minimum expectation: DSCR covenant and TNW covenant.
7. **Was the stress test reasonable?** At least two downside scenarios should be modelled.
8. **Was the ECL calculation attempted?** Even an approximate ECL is acceptable at beginner level.
