## Overview

Reserve Based Lending (RBL) is one of the most technically sophisticated areas of corporate credit. Unlike conventional corporate lending (which relies on earnings multiples and balance sheet leverage), RBL is structured against the Net Present Value (NPV) of proven and probable oil and gas reserves, using a borrowing base mechanism that limits the loan to a percentage of the risk-adjusted NPV of future production. Analysts must understand production decline curves, commodity price hedging, the Windfall Tax regime, the Debt Service Reserve Account (DSRA), and the step-in dynamics of an RBL security package. This expert case requires analysts to build a borrowing base, assess hedging adequacy, stress-test oil price scenarios, and evaluate the complex security package specific to upstream oil and gas lending.

## Financial Spreading Exercise

**Specific spreading requirements for oil & gas / RBL:**

1. **Use EBITDAX (Earnings Before Interest, Tax, Depreciation, Depletion, Amortisation, and Exploration).** This is the standard operating earnings metric for E&P companies; DD&A is large and non-cash; exploration expense is a policy choice (some companies capitalise, others expense).

2. **Tax is severe and must be modelled carefully.** Effective tax rate of 58-63% means that for every £1.00 of incremental profit, the government takes £0.58–0.63. The RBL cash flow model must use post-tax cash flows.

3. **Decommissioning provision:** £45.68M long-term + £0.52M current = £46.2M. This is a significant obligation — when the fields stop producing, North Sea Energy must fund the decommissioning of the infrastructure (wells, platforms, pipelines). Decommissioning costs are partially offset by a tax deduction (50% RFCT rate against decomm costs).

4. **DSRA (Debt Service Reserve Account):** £4.64M on balance sheet — this is cash ring-fenced to cover the next 6 months of debt service (principal + interest). It is pledged to the bank and cannot be used for other purposes. Standard in project and RBL finance.

5. **Negative equity in FY2022 — context is essential.** The analyst must explain that this is an accounting artefact (hedge losses on derivatives) not a solvency issue. Operational performance in FY2022 was exceptional.

## Business Risk Assessment

### Oil & Gas Specific Risk Framework

**1. Oil Price Risk (Primary Risk)**
The borrower's revenue is directly tied to Brent crude oil prices. Every $1/bbl change in the oil price affects annual revenue by approximately £1.47M (based on 1.47 MMbbl 2024 production). At the bank's $70/bbl deck, the facility is secure. At $50/bbl, the borrowing base collapses materially.

**2. Production Decline Risk**
Oil field production declines naturally over time (production decline curves). The D&M audit assumes 12-15% annual decline for Gannet Alpha. If actual decline rates are faster (due to reservoir management issues, water breakthrough, or unexpected geological changes), reserve estimates and the borrowing base will be revised down. Reserve downgrades trigger a borrowing base redetermination and potentially a mandatory prepayment.

**3. Commodity Hedging Risk**
Insufficient hedging increases price volatility risk. The RBL typically requires minimum hedging of 50% of forecast production for the next 18-24 months. North Sea Energy currently hedges 50% (at the minimum level). Hedging via fixed-price swaps protects downside but sacrifices upside — the FY2022 hedging loss of £18.42M (when oil went to $120+/bbl against $75 hedges) illustrates the economic cost of hedging.

**4. North Sea Windfall Tax (EPL) Risk**
The Energy Profits Levy, introduced in May 2022 and currently at 35% (on top of RFCT 30% and SC 10%), brings the effective UK upstream tax rate to 75%. This is a political instrument and subject to change. The EPL currently has a sunset clause of March 2028 (policy at this case date). A change in government or an extension/increase in EPL would materially reduce after-tax cash flows. The 2024 UK general election creates political uncertainty.

**5. Regulatory / Licence Risk**
UKCS oil and gas production requires licences granted by the North Sea Transition Authority (NSTA, formerly the OGA). Licence breaches (e.g., failure to meet decommissioning obligations, environmental incidents) can result in licence revocation — an extreme but non-zero risk.

**6. Energy Transition Risk**
Long-term structural risk: if energy transition accelerates beyond current expectations, oil demand may decline faster than anticipated, reducing oil prices and potentially stranding reserves. RBL facilities on short-to-medium tenor (5-7 years) are less exposed than long-dated financing, but the Kestrel development (if approved) would require a 20-year production life to justify its investment.

**7. Operator Risk (Harbour Energy)**
North Sea Energy holds working interests but Harbour Energy is the technical operator. If Harbour Energy's financial position deteriorates, there could be operational disruption or disputes over operating costs. Non-operators have limited ability to control pace and cost of operations.

**8. Decommissioning Liability**
The £46.2M decommissioning provision is a significant future cash obligation. The NSTA requires evidence that licence holders can fund decommissioning. Decommissioning security (in the form of a decommissioning security agreement — DSA) may require cash to be held in escrow, consuming cash flow.

## ECL Calculation (IFRS 9)

**Stage Assessment: Stage 1 at drawdown of facility (if reduced to £25M)**

- EAD: £25,000k (reduced facility)

**SICR triggers:**
- Oil price falls below $50/bbl and hedges expire without replacement
- Borrowing base redetermination results in loan > borrowing base and borrower cannot prepay

- Harbour Energy (operator) faces financial distress

## Stress Test

### Scenario 1: Oil Price Decline to $50/bbl (Beyond Hedge Book)
- Assume hedges cover 50% of production at £52/bbl equivalent
- Unhedged 50%: realised at $50/bbl = £40/bbl
- Blended revenue per bbl: (50% × £52) + (50% × £40) = £46/bbl
- 2024 revenue: 1.47 MMbbl × £46/bbl = £67.6M (vs base £82.3M)
- EBITDAX reduced by ~£14.7M; post-tax CF significantly reduced
- NPV of reserves at $50/bbl bank deck: approximately £29M (at 10% discount)
- Borrowing Base: £29M × 65% – decomm = approximately £14.2M
- If loan balance = £25M, loan exceeds borrowing base by £10.8M — mandatory prepayment required

**Conclusion:** At $50/bbl sustained, mandatory prepayment event is triggered. Company would need to repay £10.8M from cash (£14.28M available) or additional sponsor equity. Feasible but highly stressful.

### Scenario 2: Reserve Downgrade (2P → 1P, -38% reserves)
- 1P NPV: approximately £25M (based on 1P reserves being ~38% less than 2P)
- Borrowing Base on 1P: £25M × 65% – decomm = approximately £11.5M
- Mandatory prepayment of approximately £13.5M (if facility was £25M)
- Severity: very significant; would likely require new equity from Northbrook or refinancing

### Scenario 3: EPL Extended and Increased to 40% (Total Rate 80%)
- Additional 5% on ring fence profits
- FY2023 pre-tax profit: £53.6M; additional tax = £2.68M per annum
- Cumulative effect over 5 years: £13.4M of additional tax
- Post-tax NPV impact: approximately –£9.5M (discounted)
- Borrowing base impact: –£9.5M × 65% = –£6.2M reduction in borrowing base
- If borrowing base falls from £24.9M to £18.7M, existing £25M loan would breach — mandatory prepayment required

### Scenario 4: Harbour Energy Insolvency / Operational Disruption
- Production ceases or severely curtailed for 6 months while new operator is appointed
- Revenue loss: £82.3M × 50% × 50% = £20.6M (half year × half capacity)
- DSRA (£4.64M) covers 6 months debt service only
- If disruption extends beyond 6 months, bank would need to step in under its security
- Complexity: NSTA must approve licence transfers; typical timeline 6-12 months

## Decision and Rationale

**Recommendation: APPROVE AT £25M (NOT £45M AS REQUESTED)**

**Rationale:**
- The borrowing base calculation (£24.9M) does not support a £45M facility
- Approving above the borrowing base violates the fundamental principle of RBL
- At £25M, the facility is within the borrowing base, consistent with the security package, and adequately hedged
- The existing £26M RBL can be reduced to £25M using Northbrook's equity (they must inject £1M or allow partial repayment from cash)
- The "new money" element of £19M is not supportable; Northbrook must provide equity for exploration/development costs above the borrowing base

**Conditions for £25M approval:**
1. Borrowing base confirmed at ≥ £25M by independent engineer review
2. Minimum hedging programme: 50% of production for 24 months at minimum $60/bbl floor — evidence of executed hedges as condition precedent
3. DSRA pre-funded to 6 months (approximately £4.7M) from drawdown proceeds or existing cash
4. Decommissioning security agreement (DSA) reviewed and bank security over DSA escrow confirmed
5. Semi-annual borrowing base redetermination with right of mandatory prepayment if breached
6. Quarterly production reporting and annual D&M audit

## Examiner's Notes (What to Look For)

1. **The borrowing base must be calculated and must conclude that the £45M facility cannot be supported.** The central finding of this case is that the requested amount exceeds the borrowing base. Analysts who approve £45M without challenging this have failed.
2. **EBITDAX — not EBITDA or EBIT — must be used as the primary earnings metric.** Analysts who use EBITDA without removing exploration expense are making an error.
3. **Tax rate of 75% (effective) must be recognised.** Many analysts underestimate the UK upstream tax burden. Post-tax cash flows are dramatically lower than pre-tax.
4. **The NPV calculation must be understood conceptually.** Analysts need not calculate it precisely but should explain the methodology: price deck × volumes × opex × capex → post-tax cash flows → discounted at 10%.
5. **Hedging is a covenant, not just a feature.** The analyst should propose a minimum hedging covenant with a price floor.
6. **PE sponsor risk.** The £36M extraction in FY2022 is a data point about sponsor behaviour. Analysts should comment on the distribution restriction covenant.
7. **Decommissioning.** The £46.2M provision should be noted and the NSTA decommissioning security requirement explained.
8. **The DSRA.** Analysts should explain what a DSRA is and why it is standard in RBL and project finance.
9. **FY2022 negative equity.** Analysts must be able to explain that this is a hedge mark-to-market effect, not a solvency concern.
