# M33 — Governance Committees

## 1. Business Purpose

Credit governance is the system of committees, delegated authorities, escalation processes, and controls that ensures credit risk is taken deliberately, within approved risk appetite, and with appropriate oversight. It exists because credit decisions are individually made by humans with commercial incentives — relationship managers want to do deals, sectors heads want to grow their portfolios — and governance provides the counterbalance of independent challenge and risk discipline.

**Why governance matters:**

*Prevention of excessive risk-taking:* Without clear delegation limits and committee oversight, individual bankers could approve credits that are too large, too complex, or too risky for the bank's balance sheet. The 2008 financial crisis demonstrated that many banks had weak or captured governance: credit committees were dominated by commercial bankers, risk officers lacked veto authority, and concentration limits were either absent or routinely waived.

*Regulatory expectation:* The PRA (UK), ECB/SSM (Eurozone), and FSB (globally) have set explicit expectations for credit governance. The PRA's SS5/21 (Risk Management Arrangements) and the FSB's Principles for Sound Governance of Banks require that: risk management has genuine independence, Board Risk Committees have real oversight, and credit approval functions are separate from business origination.

*Audit trail and accountability:* Every credit decision should be documented, including the names of approvers, the basis for approval, dissenting views, conditions attached, and subsequent covenant compliance. This documentation serves as the audit trail for internal audit, regulatory examiners, and — in cases of failure — litigation and regulatory enforcement.

*Risk appetite enforcement:* The Board approves a Risk Appetite Statement (RAS) that includes credit risk metrics: maximum portfolio PD, sector concentration limits, minimum credit quality for new business, maximum LTV for secured lending. Governance committees translate the RAS into operational limits and monitor compliance. Without governance infrastructure, the RAS is aspirational rather than binding.

*Capital discipline:* Credit committees incorporate capital considerations: does this credit consume an acceptable level of RWA? Does it generate a satisfactory RORWA? This links individual credit decisions to the bank's capital position, ensuring that growth strategies are capital-sustainable.

## 2. Accounting Concepts

**Credit Committee Records and Accounting Implications**

Credit committee minutes serve as the governance record for accounting purposes. When a provision (ECL) is recorded or reversed, the audit trail must demonstrate that the provisioning decision was appropriately approved and challenged. Under IFRS 9, significant management overlays (e.g., a top-up provision for a sector with elevated but not yet crystallised risk) must be approved by an appropriate committee to demonstrate that they represent a considered estimate rather than management bias.

**Management Overlays and Committee Approval**

Large ECL management overlays — additions to model-generated ECL to reflect factors the model does not capture — must have documented committee approval (typically the Credit Risk Committee or the Group Model Risk Committee) and be justified by specific evidence. Auditors and the PRA examine overlay approval trails as part of ECL opinion forming.

**Delegated Authority and Financial Reporting**

Large exposures (>10% of Tier 1 capital) must be reported in COREP LE templates. The committee structure must ensure someone has explicit authority to approve large exposures and that breach prevention is monitored. If a large exposure limit is breached without appropriate escalation, it represents not just a regulatory breach but a governance failure that must be disclosed to the regulator immediately.

**Pillar 3 Governance Disclosures**

Under CRR Art. 435, banks must disclose information about their risk management framework including: the number, nature, and frequency of meetings of the management body's risk committee, the number of directorships held by members, and the process for selecting Board members. These disclosures are found in the Pillar 3 report, adjacent to credit risk quantitative disclosures.

## 3. Financial Concepts

**Delegated Credit Authority (DCA)**

The DCA framework specifies who can approve what size and type of credit. A typical hierarchy:

| Level | Authority | Typical Limit |
|-------|-----------|---------------|
| Relationship Manager (RM) | Renewals of existing credits within covenant | £0 new money |
| Credit Manager / Senior RM | Small credits within policy | Up to £500k |
| Credit Director | Moderate credits | Up to £2m |
| Credit Committee (2 signatories) | Normal credits | Up to £10m |
| Group Credit Committee | Large credits | £10m–£50m |
| Group Credit Risk Committee (GCRC) | Very large credits | £50m–£150m |
| Chief Risk Officer (CRO) + CEO | Exceptionally large credits | Above £150m |
| Board Risk Committee | Very large or complex | Above £250m or policy exceptions |

These limits vary enormously by institution size. A major UK bank (HSBC, Barclays) will have credit committee limits 10-50x higher than a mid-sized regional lender.

**Risk-Adjusted Return on Capital in Committee Decisions**

Well-governed credit committees do not approve or decline credits solely on credit quality grounds — they also assess economic merit. A BB-rated borrower is not automatically approvable if the spread is insufficient to cover EL + operating costs + capital cost. Committees should see RORWA / ROCE analysis as standard for larger credits.

**Financial Covenants and Monitoring**

Credit committees set covenants as conditions of approval. The governance framework must ensure covenants are: (1) set at appropriate levels relative to current performance and headroom, (2) tested regularly (quarterly or semi-annual financial testing), (3) reported to committee when breached, and (4) appropriately waived or restructured through committee approval when the borrower's circumstances change.

## 4. Statistical Concepts

**Monte Carlo Use in Committee Decision-Making**

For large credits (typically >£25m), some banks now require credit submissions to include scenario analysis: best case, base case, and downside case for the borrower's financials over the loan term. While not always formal Monte Carlo simulation, these scenarios should be based on statistical distributions of the key drivers (revenue growth, EBITDA margin, capex). A well-governed committee will challenge whether the downside scenario is genuinely severe or optimistically calibrated.

**Portfolio Limits Based on Confidence Intervals**

Sector and single-name limits are sometimes set with reference to portfolio risk models: "The CRE sector limit is set such that a 10% CRE loss rate in a 1-in-25-year scenario would not consume more than 20% of annual pre-provision income." This statistical basis for limit-setting is a sign of mature governance — limits are not arbitrary but calibrated to the bank's risk capacity.

**Grade Migration Statistics in Committee Reporting**

Monthly committee reports typically include a migration matrix analysis: how many borrowers migrated from one grade to a worse grade in the prior month? High migration rates (especially from performing to Stage 2 or from watch list to impaired) are leading indicators of deteriorating portfolio quality and should trigger committee discussion of provisioning adequacy and risk appetite.

## 5. Regulatory Framework

**PRA Supervisory Statement SS5/21 — Internal Governance**

SS5/21 sets out PRA expectations for internal governance of UK banks, including:
- The management body (Board) must have effective oversight of risk, including credit risk
- A Board Risk Committee (BRC) is required for PRA-regulated firms with >£25bn assets or complex business
- Senior Managers (under SMCR) must be clearly assigned responsibility for credit risk
- Credit risk governance arrangements must be documented and periodically reviewed

**Senior Managers and Certification Regime (SMCR)**

Under SMCR (FCA/PRA, 2019), specific Senior Management Functions (SMFs) carry responsibility for credit risk:
- SMF4 (Chief Risk Officer): Responsible for overall risk management framework
- SMF7 (Group Entity Senior Manager): Responsible for group-level risk governance
- SMF12 (Chair of Risk Committee): Responsible for Board risk oversight

These individuals must take reasonable steps to prevent regulatory breaches in their areas. A credit governance failure can attract personal regulatory action, including fines and prohibition orders.

**EBA Guidelines on Internal Governance (EBA/GL/2021/05)**

The EBA's governance guidelines apply to EU banks and cover: composition and functioning of the management body, risk culture, risk management framework, internal controls, and information systems. Key credit risk governance requirements include: the requirement for a formal credit risk framework (policies, appetite, limits), the independence of credit risk management from business origination, and annual review of credit risk policies.

**Three Lines of Defence Model**

Mandated by EBA guidelines and PRA expectations:
- **First Line (1LoD):** Business origination — relationship managers, coverage bankers. Responsible for credit quality of their originations, initial credit analysis, and covenant monitoring.
- **Second Line (2LoD):** Credit Risk Management — independent of business. Sets credit policy, approves credits above 1LoD limits, monitors portfolio quality, validates models, and challenges 1LoD assessments.
- **Third Line (3LoD):** Internal Audit — independent of 1LoD and 2LoD. Audits the effectiveness of the control framework including credit origination quality, model governance, provisioning adequacy.

**BCBS Principles for the Sound Management of Operational Risk** — Article 8 applies to credit risk governance as well: the Board must approve and regularly review risk appetite, and senior management must translate risk appetite into operational limits.

## 7. How Analysts Actually Work

**Preparing a Credit Committee Paper**

When a relationship manager brings a new credit to the Credit Manager for approval, the Credit Manager (or a dedicated credit analyst) prepares the credit committee paper. This document typically includes:

1. **Executive summary:** Borrower name, facility details, proposed terms, recommendation (Approve/Decline/Refer)
2. **Borrower profile:** Business description, history, ownership, management track record
3. **Financial analysis:** 3-year historical financials, ratio analysis, financial covenant headroom, sensitivity analysis
4. **Risk assessment:** Key risks, mitigants, security assessment
5. **Credit grade:** PD rating assigned and rationale
6. **Covenant package:** Proposed financial covenants and maintenance schedule
7. **Capital and returns:** RWA, RORWA, ROCE analysis
8. **Recommendation and conditions:** Committee-specific conditions, specialist sign-offs required (property, legal, environmental)

**The Escalation Journey**

A credit request for £30m to a manufacturing company at a mid-sized UK bank might travel:
1. RM → Credit Director (bilateral review, any policy issues?)
2. Credit Director → Local Credit Committee (approves up to £20m; refers with recommendation to Group CC)
3. Group Credit Committee (approves with conditions: quarterly financial covenant testing, annual review)
4. RM notified → legal documentation → drawdown

**Watch List Governance**

Credits on the watch list are reviewed in dedicated Watch List Committees (monthly or more frequently for active restructurings). These committees track: covenant compliance, financial performance updates, external advisors' reports, recovery/exit strategy, and provisioning adequacy. Minutes from watch list committees are key audit evidence for provisioning decisions.

## 9. SQL Implementation

```sql
-- =================================================================
-- Credit Governance — Committee and DCA Monitoring
-- =================================================================

-- 1. DCA Breach Analysis — credits approved at wrong level
WITH dca_check AS (
    SELECT
        cd.credit_decision_id,
        cd.decision_date,
        cd.obligor_name,
        cd.facility_amount,
        cd.approving_officer,
        cd.approving_level,
        da.max_approval_amount,
        da.level_name,
        CASE
            WHEN cd.facility_amount > da.max_approval_amount
            THEN 'DCA BREACH'
            ELSE 'OK'
        END AS dca_status,
        -- What level SHOULD have approved this?
        CASE
            WHEN cd.facility_amount <= 500000    THEN 'Credit Manager'
            WHEN cd.facility_amount <= 2000000   THEN 'Credit Director'
            WHEN cd.facility_amount <= 10000000  THEN 'Credit Committee'
            WHEN cd.facility_amount <= 50000000  THEN 'Group Credit Committee'
            WHEN cd.facility_amount <= 150000000 THEN 'GCRC'
            ELSE 'CRO/Board'
        END AS required_level
    FROM credit_decisions cd
    JOIN delegated_authority da ON da.officer_id = cd.approving_officer
    WHERE cd.decision_date >= DATEADD(MONTH, -3, GETDATE())
),

-- 2. Sector concentration vs. limit monitoring
sector_limits AS (
    SELECT
        sl.sector_code,
        sl.sector_name,
        sl.approved_limit,
        sl.effective_date,
        sl.approved_by,
        COALESCE(p.current_ead, 0)       AS current_ead,
        COALESCE(pip.pipeline_ead, 0)    AS pipeline_ead
    FROM sector_credit_limits sl
    LEFT JOIN (
        SELECT sector_code, SUM(ead) AS current_ead
        FROM portfolio_facilities
        WHERE facility_status = 'ACTIVE'
        GROUP BY sector_code
    ) p ON p.sector_code = sl.sector_code
    LEFT JOIN (
        SELECT sector_code, SUM(approved_amount) AS pipeline_ead
        FROM credit_pipeline
        WHERE pipeline_status IN ('APPROVED', 'LEGAL', 'NOT_YET_DRAWN')
        GROUP BY sector_code
    ) pip ON pip.sector_code = sl.sector_code
    WHERE sl.is_current = 1
),

limit_utilisation AS (
    SELECT
        sector_name,
        approved_limit,
        current_ead,
        pipeline_ead,
        current_ead + pipeline_ead                      AS total_committed,
        (current_ead + pipeline_ead) / approved_limit   AS utilisation_rate,
        CASE
            WHEN (current_ead + pipeline_ead) / approved_limit >= 1.0
            THEN 'BREACH — IMMEDIATE ACTION'
            WHEN (current_ead + pipeline_ead) / approved_limit >= 0.90
            THEN 'RED — CRO sign-off required'
            WHEN (current_ead + pipeline_ead) / approved_limit >= 0.75
            THEN 'AMBER — approaching limit'
            ELSE 'GREEN'
        END AS limit_rag
    FROM sector_limits
)

-- Output 1: DCA breaches for escalation
SELECT * FROM dca_check WHERE dca_status = 'DCA BREACH'
ORDER BY decision_date DESC;

-- Output 2: Sector limit dashboard
SELECT
    sector_name,
    ROUND(approved_limit / 1e6, 0)      AS limit_mm,
    ROUND(current_ead / 1e6, 0)         AS current_ead_mm,
    ROUND(pipeline_ead / 1e6, 0)        AS pipeline_mm,
    ROUND(total_committed / 1e6, 0)     AS committed_mm,
    ROUND(utilisation_rate * 100, 1)    AS utilisation_pct,
    limit_rag
FROM limit_utilisation
ORDER BY utilisation_rate DESC;


-- 3. Committee approval statistics by month
SELECT
    FORMAT(cd.decision_date, 'yyyy-MM')  AS month,
    cd.approving_level,
    COUNT(*)                             AS submissions,
    SUM(CASE WHEN cd.decision = 'APPROVED' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END)  AS approved,
    SUM(CASE WHEN cd.decision = 'DECLINED' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END)  AS declined,
    SUM(CASE WHEN cd.decision = 'DEFERRED' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END)  AS deferred,
    AVG(cd.facility_amount) / 1e6        AS avg_facility_mm,
    AVG(DATEDIFF(DAY, cd.submission_date, cd.decision_date))    AS avg_turnaround_days,
    SUM(CASE WHEN cd.policy_exception = 1 THEN 1 ELSE 0 END)   AS exceptions_granted,
    ROUND(
        SUM(CASE WHEN cd.policy_exception = 1 THEN 1.0 ELSE 0 END) / COUNT(*) * 100, 1
    )                                    AS exception_rate_pct
FROM credit_decisions cd
WHERE cd.decision_date >= DATEADD(YEAR, -1, GETDATE())
GROUP BY FORMAT(cd.decision_date, 'yyyy-MM'), cd.approving_level
ORDER BY month DESC, approving_level;
```

import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
from datetime import datetime, timedelta
import warnings
warnings.filterwarnings('ignore')

np.random.seed(42)


# ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
# 1. SIMULATE CREDIT DECISION DATA
# ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
n = 500  # credit decisions over past year

approval_levels = ['Credit Manager', 'Credit Director', 'Credit Committee',
                   'Group CC', 'GCRC', 'CRO/Board']
level_limits = {
    'Credit Manager':  500_000,
    'Credit Director': 2_000_000,
    'Credit Committee':10_000_000,
    'Group CC':        50_000_000,
    'GCRC':           150_000_000,
    'CRO/Board':      999_999_999,
}

decisions = pd.DataFrame({
    'decision_date':    [datetime(2025, 1, 1) + timedelta(days=np.random.randint(0, 365))
                         for _ in range(n)],
    'obligor_name':    [f'Obligor_{i:04d}' for i in range(n)],
    'facility_amount': np.random.lognormal(np.log(3e6), 1.2, n).clip(50_000, 200_000_000),
    'sector':          np.random.choice(['CRE', 'Manufacturing', 'Retail', 'TMT', 'Healthcare'], n),
    'decision':        np.random.choice(['APPROVED', 'DECLINED', 'DEFERRED'], n, p=[0.75, 0.15, 0.10]),
    'policy_exception':np.random.choice([0, 1], n, p=[0.92, 0.08]),
    'turnaround_days': np.random.exponential(4, n).astype(int) + 1,
})

# Assign approving level (with some DCA breaches)
def assign_level(amount):
    """Correct DCA level for this amount."""
    for lvl, lim in level_limits.items():
        if amount <= lim:
            return lvl
    return 'CRO/Board'

decisions['correct_level'] = decisions['facility_amount'].apply(assign_level)
# Simulate 5% DCA breaches (approved at too low a level)
breach_mask = np.random.choice([True, False], n, p=[0.05, 0.95])
level_order = list(level_limits.keys())
decisions['approving_level'] = decisions['correct_level'].copy()
decisions.loc[breach_mask, 'approving_level'] = decisions.loc[breach_mask, 'correct_level'].apply(
    lambda lvl: level_order[max(0, level_order.index(lvl) - 1)]
)
decisions['dca_breach'] = decisions['approving_level'] != decisions['correct_level']


# ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
# 2. DCA BREACH SUMMARY
# ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
print("=" * 60)

    breach_count=('dca_breach', 'sum'),
    total_facility_m=('facility_amount', lambda x: x.sum() / 1e6),
).reset_index()
print(f"Total credits reviewed: {n}")
print(f"DCA breaches detected:  {decisions['dca_breach'].sum()} ({decisions['dca_breach'].mean()*100:.1f}%)")

## 11. Interview Questions

1. **"Describe the three lines of defence model in credit risk."**
   1LoD = business/origination (RMs, coverage teams) — own credit quality at origination. 2LoD = Credit Risk Management — independent policy setting, approval above DCA limits, portfolio monitoring, model ownership. 3LoD = Internal Audit — independent assurance over effectiveness of 1LoD and 2LoD controls.

2. **"How does a credit travel from the RM to a credit committee?"**
   RM identifies opportunity, prepares credit memo with 2LoD support. Credit Manager reviews and grades. If within DCA, approves with conditions. If above DCA, escalates to Credit Committee with recommendation. CC reviews, challenges assumptions, approves/declines/conditions. For large credits, escalates to GCRC or CRO/Board.

3. **"What makes credit governance weak? Give examples."**
   Weak governance: credit committees dominated by relationship managers (commercial bias), CRO lacks veto power, rubber-stamping of RM recommendations, DCA limits too high for individual officers, policy exceptions approved routinely without challenge, committee attendance poor (no quorum), minutes not recording dissenting views.

4. **"What is SMCR and how does it affect credit governance?"**
   Senior Managers and Certification Regime assigns personal accountability to named individuals for specific risk management functions. The CRO (SMF4) is personally accountable for the credit risk framework. If credit governance fails materially, the CRO must demonstrate they took reasonable steps to prevent it. Creates personal incentive for robust governance.

5. **"What is the quorum requirement for a credit committee?"**
   Varies by institution but typically: minimum 2 approvers; at least 1 must be from the independent credit risk function (2LoD). Some institutions require that the 2LoD credit officer has effective veto over the commercial banker. Minutes must record who attended and confirm quorum was met.

## 12. Common Mistakes

**Mistake 1: Confusing authority levels for different products**
DCA limits often differ by product type — a £5m real estate loan may require committee approval, while a £5m fully secured overdraft backed by cash collateral requires only Credit Director approval. Credit teams must apply the right DCA matrix for the specific product, not a generic facility limit.

**Mistake 2: Pipeline not counted in sector limits**
Sector limits must include pipeline (approved but undrawn commitments), not just current drawn exposure. A sector at 85% utilisation on drawn EAD might already be in breach when pipeline is included. Portfolio risk teams must include pipeline in their monitoring.

**Mistake 3: Covenant waiver without committee approval**
Relationship managers sometimes informally waive covenant testing or agree to adjust financial thresholds without formal committee approval. This is a DCA breach and a governance failure. All covenant waivers must be documented and approved at the same authority level as the original credit.

**Mistake 4: Committee minutes not recording dissenting views**
If a committee member objects to an approval but is overruled, the dissenting view must be recorded in the minutes. Minutes that record only decisions without discussion are inadequate from a governance perspective and will be flagged in audit.

**Mistake 5: Escalation thresholds not keeping pace with portfolio growth**
DCA limits set 10 years ago may be inappropriate for a bank that has doubled in size. If the Credit Committee limit (£10m) is below the average facility size (£15m), every credit goes to Group CC, creating a bottleneck. DCA limits should be reviewed annually.

## 13. Case Studies

**Case Study 1: Governance Failures at HBOS**
The FSA/PRA report on the failure of HBOS (2015) identified catastrophic governance failures in credit risk. The Corporate division grew aggressively with weak credit governance: concentrations in CRE and leveraged lending were not controlled by effective portfolio limits; the Credit Risk function was under-resourced and lacked authority to challenge the Corporate division; policy exceptions were routinely granted without appropriate scrutiny. The Board Risk Committee did not have sufficient information to understand the risk profile. Result: £47bn of impairment losses. The lesson: governance is not bureaucracy — it is the mechanism that prevents catastrophic risk accumulation.

**Case Study 2: Effective Governance — BBVA's Three Lines Model**
BBVA's credit governance is cited as a best-practice example. Their "admission function" (equivalent to 2LoD) has explicit veto authority over all credits above certain thresholds, regardless of commercial pressure. Portfolio limits are integrated into the credit approval workflow — the system blocks new credit submissions that would push a sector above 90% of its limit. Committee minutes are detailed and reviewed by Internal Audit annually. Post-2008, BBVA's credit losses were significantly lower than peers despite similar geographic exposure.

**Case Study 3: DCA Breach and Personal Accountability under SMCR**
A UK bank's credit team approved several large leveraged loans at Credit Director level (DCA: £2m) when the loans were £8-12m, which required Credit Committee approval. The internal audit identified this pattern. Under SMCR, the Head of Credit was required to explain to the PRA what steps had been taken to prevent recurrence and to demonstrate personal awareness of DCA compliance. The incident resulted in enhanced DCA monitoring controls and a formal remediation plan submitted to the PRA.
