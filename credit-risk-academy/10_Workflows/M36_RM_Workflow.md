# M36 — Relationship Manager Workflow

## 1. Business Purpose

The Relationship Manager (RM) is the primary interface between a corporate bank and its clients. In commercial and corporate banking, the RM is responsible for managing a portfolio of client relationships, generating revenue through lending, transactional banking, and ancillary products, while simultaneously serving as the bank's first line of defence against credit risk.

The RM's commercial purpose is to grow wallet share within an assigned portfolio, win new mandates from target prospects, and deepen existing relationships through cross-sell of treasury, trade finance, foreign exchange, and capital markets products. Revenue is measured via net interest income (NII) on drawn balances, fee income from facility arrangement and commitment fees, and non-credit income from FX and cash management.

The credit risk purpose of the RM function is critical and often misunderstood: the RM is not merely a sales person. In well-run banks, the RM is accountable for the quality of credit extended to their clients. This means the RM must understand the borrower's business deeply enough to identify deterioration before it becomes a problem, must structure credit facilities appropriately, and must not advocate for facilities that the bank should not extend. The RM's performance assessment includes credit losses attributed to their portfolio, not just revenue generated.

Client coverage models vary by institution, but in corporate banking a typical RM carries a portfolio of 15 to 40 clients, depending on complexity. Larger, more complex relationships require more intensive coverage. Mid-market RMs may cover 50 to 80 clients. The portfolio is constructed to be manageable, meaning the RM should be able to meet every client at least twice per year and complete annual reviews on schedule.

The annual review cycle is the structural backbone of credit risk management in commercial banking. Facilities approved more than 12 months ago, or sooner if a material event occurs, must be formally reviewed. Material events include: acquisition by the borrower, significant deterioration in financial performance, a covenant breach, a change in ownership or key management, or a significant shift in market conditions affecting the borrower's industry.

## 2. Accounting Concepts

RMs must be able to read and interpret the full financial statements of their clients to prepare for client meetings and to identify emerging credit concerns.

**Income Statement:** The RM focuses on revenue trends (is the top line growing, flat, or shrinking?), gross margin (does the business have pricing power?), EBITDA margin (is the business operationally profitable?), and interest coverage (can the business service its debt from operating earnings?). For leveraged borrowers, EBITDA is the primary metric. For investment-grade corporates, free cash flow generation is the primary metric.

**Balance Sheet:** The RM examines net asset strength (tangible net worth excluding goodwill), working capital trends (current ratio, quick ratio), debt quantum and structure (senior secured vs subordinated, short-term vs long-term), and off-balance-sheet exposures (operating leases pre-IFRS 16, contingent liabilities). A deteriorating balance sheet is characterised by rising gearing, shrinking equity (net losses or excessive dividends), and growing intangibles that inflate reported net worth.

**Cash Flow Statement:** The most important statement for credit. The RM distinguishes between cash generated from operations (OCF), capital expenditure requirements (capex), and free cash flow (FCF = OCF minus capex). A business that generates positive EBITDA but negative FCF after debt service is a credit concern regardless of its profitability metrics. The RM should be able to explain the cash conversion cycle for the borrower's industry.

**IFRS vs UK GAAP:** Many UK corporate clients file under FRS 102 (UK GAAP), not IFRS. The RM must understand key differences: FRS 102 does not require IFRS 9 expected credit loss provisioning, treatment of lease liabilities differs, and financial instrument classification differs. When spreading financials, the RM must note the accounting standard used.

**Consolidated vs Holding Company Accounts:** Many borrowers are part of group structures. The RM must identify whether the borrowing entity is the operating company, a holding company, or an intermediate holdco. Lending to a holdco when cash sits in opcos creates structural subordination risk. The credit analysis must cover the entity that will repay the debt, not just the group as a whole.

## 3. Financial Concepts

**Client Coverage Model:** Clients are allocated to RMs based on revenue potential, complexity, and geography. In UK corporate banking, clients are typically segmented by turnover: SME (£1m–£25m), mid-market (£25m–£250m), and large corporate (£250m+). Each segment has different product needs, risk profiles, and margin expectations. RMs are assigned to a segment and are expected to understand sector dynamics within that segment.

**Facility Types:** The RM must understand the full range of credit products: revolving credit facilities (RCFs), term loans (TL), overdraft facilities (OD), invoice finance (IF), trade finance (letters of credit, guarantees), asset finance, and bilateral vs syndicated structures. Each facility type has different risk characteristics, pricing, and monitoring requirements.

**Pricing:** Loan pricing is typically SONIA (in the UK, replacing LIBOR) plus a credit margin. The credit margin reflects: the credit risk of the borrower (driven by internal risk rating), the tenor and structure of the facility, any security provided, the overall relationship value, and competitive dynamics. RMs negotiate margins within bands set by credit policy. A lower-risk borrower attracts a lower margin, but the RM must ensure that the relationship is profitable on a risk-adjusted return on capital (RAROC) basis.

**RAROC:** Return on risk-adjusted capital. The RM uses RAROC to assess whether a relationship is adding value. RAROC = (NII + fees − expected losses − operating costs) / economic capital. A facility that earns a 2% margin but requires 15% capital allocation due to high credit risk may generate a RAROC below the bank's hurdle rate. The RM should understand this and price accordingly.

**Relationship Profitability:** The full relationship includes: drawn loan income, commitment fees on undrawn facilities, cash management fees, FX income, trade finance fees, and advisory fees. A borrower with a modest loan but high transactional volumes may be more profitable than a larger borrower with high credit utilisation and no ancillary income.

**Pipeline Management:** The RM maintains a pipeline of prospective new business, categorised by probability of closing. Stage 1: initial conversation (10–20% probability); Stage 2: mandate won, terms being agreed (50%); Stage 3: credit approved, documentation underway (90%); Stage 4: drawn down (100%). Monthly pipeline reviews with the sector head or head of corporate banking ensure accountability.

## 4. Statistical Concepts

**Probability of Default (PD):** The RM interacts with PD in the context of internal credit ratings. Each borrower is assigned a risk rating (e.g., 1 to 10 or AAA to D on an internal scale) that maps to a PD band. The RM's portfolio average PD is a key performance metric. A deteriorating portfolio shows an increasing weighted average PD over time.

**Expected Loss (EL):** EL = PD × LGD × EAD. The RM's portfolio generates expected losses that are charged against relationship profitability. Understanding EL helps the RM appreciate why high-risk borrowers must be priced at higher margins — the premium compensates for expected losses.

**Concentration Risk:** An RM's portfolio should not be overly concentrated in a single sector or a single borrower. Banks set single-name concentration limits (typically 10–25% of capital for a single obligor) and sector limits. An RM who covers only retail borrowers has a concentrated portfolio that is vulnerable to a retail sector downturn.

**Correlation:** During economic downturns, default rates are correlated — companies in the same sector or subject to the same macro shock default together. This is why diversification across sectors and geographies matters. The RM should understand that in a recession, their entire portfolio may come under stress simultaneously.

## 5. Regulatory Framework

**Senior Management Accountability (SMCR):** In UK banking, the Senior Managers and Certification Regime requires individuals in specified roles to be approved by the PRA/FCA. RMs who hold prescribed responsibilities must understand their personal accountability for credit decisions and client outcomes.

**Know Your Customer (KYC) and AML:** The RM is responsible for ensuring KYC documentation is complete and current for all clients. This includes beneficial ownership verification, source of funds, and sanctions screening. A lapsed KYC file can block a drawdown request. Anti-money laundering training is mandatory, and the RM has an obligation to file a suspicious activity report (SAR) if they observe suspicious behaviour.

**Consumer Duty / Fair Treatment:** While primarily a retail/SME concept, the spirit of fair treatment applies in commercial banking. The RM must not mis-sell products or provide advice that is not in the client's interest.

**Credit Policy Compliance:** The RM operates within the bank's credit policy, which sets out: eligible borrower types, acceptable sectors (and excluded sectors), maximum loan-to-value ratios for asset finance, minimum DSCR requirements, mandatory covenant sets, and approval authority limits. Departures from policy require formal credit policy exceptions, which must be approved at a higher level.

## 6. Data Required

To manage a client portfolio effectively, an RM requires access to:

- **Financial statements:** Annual accounts (last three years minimum), management accounts (quarterly or monthly for higher-risk clients), forecasts and business plans.
- **Credit file:** Facility agreement, security documentation, latest credit approval, covenant compliance records, internal risk rating history.
- **Utilisation data:** Current drawn balances, undrawn availability, facility limits, fee accrual.
- **Behavioural data:** Account conduct (overdraft excesses, returned items, late payments), payment history, early warning indicators from transaction monitoring.
- **External data:** Credit bureau reports, Companies House filings, land registry searches for property security, sector intelligence reports.
- **Pipeline data:** CRM system entries for prospects and new business in development.
- **Covenant tracking:** Excel or system-based covenant compliance tracker showing the last test date, test result, and next test date for every covenanted facility.

## 7. How Analysts Actually Work

The RM's day typically begins with reviewing overnight alerts: covenant breaches, excesses on accounts, news alerts on clients (Google Alerts, Bloomberg), and emails from clients or internal teams.

**Client Meeting Preparation:** Before any significant client meeting, the RM prepares a call report template covering: purpose of meeting, key topics, financial update expected, any credit concerns to probe, and cross-sell opportunities to explore. Post-meeting, the RM completes a call report and files it on the client relationship management (CRM) system. This creates an audit trail.

**Annual Review Trigger:** The RM receives a system-generated notification when an annual review is due (12 months from the last credit approval date, or sooner if triggered by a material event). The RM is responsible for gathering updated financials from the client, completing an internal credit request form, and submitting to the credit team within the bank's turnaround window (typically 5–10 business days before the review deadline).

**New Facility Origination:** A new facility request follows a structured workflow:
1. Client identifies a need (e.g., acquisition finance, working capital, capex).
2. RM conducts an indicative term sheet discussion with the client.
3. RM completes an internal credit request, including proposed structure, amount, tenor, pricing, security, and financial covenants.
4. Credit analyst is assigned and produces a credit paper.
5. Credit paper is submitted for approval (delegated authority or credit committee).
6. On approval, legal documentation is instructed.
7. Conditions precedent to drawdown are satisfied.
8. Drawdown occurs and loan is booked in the system.

**Early Warning Identification:** The RM is the first line of defence. Warning signs the RM watches for include: reduced sales volumes from client conversations, supply chain disruption mentions, management changes, missed covenant tests, utilisation patterns changing (chronic full utilisation of RCF), late payment of interest, requests to waive or amend covenant thresholds.

**Cross-Selling:** The RM identifies opportunities for FX hedging (borrowers with foreign currency revenues or costs), cash management products (liquidity management, sweeping structures), trade finance (LCs, guarantees for international suppliers), and interest rate hedging (swap referrals to the treasury desk). Cross-sell income materially improves relationship RAROC.

## 9. SQL Implementation

```sql
-- ===== RM PORTFOLIO UTILISATION REPORT =====
SELECT
    c.client_name,
    c.sector,
    c.rm_name,
    f.facility_type,
    f.approved_limit,
    f.current_drawn,
    ROUND(CAST(f.current_drawn AS FLOAT) / NULLIF(f.approved_limit, 0) * 100, 1)
        AS utilisation_pct,
    c.internal_rating,
    c.annual_review_due,
    DATEDIFF(DAY, GETDATE(), c.annual_review_due) AS days_to_review,
    CASE
        WHEN c.annual_review_due < GETDATE() THEN 'OVERDUE'
        WHEN DATEDIFF(DAY, GETDATE(), c.annual_review_due) <= 30 THEN 'DUE SOON'
        ELSE 'CURRENT'
    END AS review_status
FROM clients c
JOIN facilities f ON c.client_id = f.client_id
WHERE c.rm_id = @rm_id
  AND f.status = 'ACTIVE'
ORDER BY c.annual_review_due ASC;

-- ===== PIPELINE SUMMARY BY RM =====
SELECT
    rm_name,
    stage,
    COUNT(*) AS deal_count,
    SUM(deal_amount_m) AS total_amount_m,
    SUM(deal_amount_m * probability_pct / 100.0) AS weighted_amount_m
FROM pipeline
WHERE is_active = 1
GROUP BY rm_name, stage
ORDER BY rm_name, stage;

-- ===== EARLY WARNING INDICATORS =====
-- Clients with chronic high utilisation (>90% for last 3 months)
SELECT
    c.client_name,
    c.rm_name,
    c.internal_rating,
    AVG(u.utilisation_pct) AS avg_util_3m,
    MAX(u.utilisation_pct) AS max_util_3m
FROM clients c
JOIN utilisation_history u ON c.client_id = u.client_id
WHERE u.snapshot_date >= DATEADD(MONTH, -3, GETDATE())
GROUP BY c.client_name, c.rm_name, c.internal_rating
HAVING AVG(u.utilisation_pct) > 90
ORDER BY avg_util_3m DESC;

-- ===== COVENANT COMPLIANCE STATUS =====
SELECT
    c.client_name,
    c.rm_name,
    cv.covenant_type,
    cv.threshold_value,
    cv.last_test_actual,
    cv.last_test_actual - cv.threshold_value AS headroom,
    cv.last_test_date,
    cv.next_test_date,
    CASE
        WHEN cv.last_test_actual < cv.threshold_value THEN 'BREACH'
        WHEN (cv.last_test_actual - cv.threshold_value) / cv.threshold_value < 0.20
            THEN 'TIGHT'
        ELSE 'COMPLIANT'
    END AS covenant_status
FROM clients c
JOIN covenants cv ON c.client_id = cv.client_id
WHERE cv.is_active = 1
ORDER BY covenant_status DESC, c.rm_name;

-- ===== RM PORTFOLIO CREDIT QUALITY TREND =====
SELECT
    YEAR(snapshot_date) AS yr,
    QUARTER(snapshot_date) AS qtr,
    rm_name,
    AVG(CAST(internal_rating AS FLOAT)) AS avg_rating,
    SUM(CASE WHEN internal_rating >= 7 THEN current_drawn ELSE 0 END)
        / NULLIF(SUM(current_drawn), 0) * 100 AS watch_list_pct_drawn
FROM portfolio_snapshots ps
JOIN clients c ON ps.client_id = c.client_id
GROUP BY YEAR(snapshot_date), QUARTER(snapshot_date), rm_name
ORDER BY yr, qtr, rm_name;
```

## 10. Python Implementation

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
from datetime import date, timedelta
import warnings
warnings.filterwarnings('ignore')

# ===== RM PORTFOLIO MANAGEMENT TOOLKIT =====

class RMPortfolio:
    """Tools for relationship manager portfolio management."""

    def __init__(self, portfolio_df: pd.DataFrame):
        """
        portfolio_df columns expected:
        client_name, sector, rm_name, facility_type, approved_limit,
        current_drawn, internal_rating, annual_review_due, pd_pct, lgd_pct
        """
        self.df = portfolio_df.copy()
        self.df['annual_review_due'] = pd.to_datetime(self.df['annual_review_due'])
        self._compute_derived_fields()

    def _compute_derived_fields(self):
        today = pd.Timestamp(date.today())
        self.df['utilisation_pct'] = (
            self.df['current_drawn'] / self.df['approved_limit'].replace(0, np.nan)
        ) * 100
        self.df['days_to_review'] = (self.df['annual_review_due'] - today).dt.days
        self.df['review_status'] = self.df['days_to_review'].apply(
            lambda d: 'OVERDUE' if d < 0 else ('DUE SOON' if d <= 30 else 'CURRENT')
        )
        self.df['el_amount'] = (
            self.df['current_drawn']
            * self.df['pd_pct'] / 100
            * self.df['lgd_pct'] / 100
        )

    def review_dashboard(self) -> pd.DataFrame:
        """Highlight upcoming and overdue annual reviews."""
        cols = ['client_name', 'sector', 'rm_name', 'internal_rating',
                'annual_review_due', 'days_to_review', 'review_status']
        return (self.df[cols]
                .sort_values('days_to_review')
                .reset_index(drop=True))

    def utilisation_report(self) -> pd.DataFrame:
        """Show facility utilisation across portfolio."""
        cols = ['client_name', 'facility_type', 'approved_limit',
                'current_drawn', 'utilisation_pct']
        return self.df[cols].sort_values('utilisation_pct', ascending=False)

    def early_warning_screen(self,
                              util_threshold: float = 90.0,
                              rating_threshold: int = 7) -> pd.DataFrame:
        """Flag clients with high utilisation or weak ratings."""
        high_util = self.df['utilisation_pct'] >= util_threshold
        weak_rating = self.df['internal_rating'] >= rating_threshold
        flagged = self.df[high_util | weak_rating].copy()
        flagged['warning_flag'] = np.where(
            high_util & weak_rating, 'HIGH UTIL + WEAK RATING',
            np.where(high_util, 'HIGH UTILISATION', 'WEAK RATING')
        )
        return flagged[['client_name', 'rm_name', 'utilisation_pct',
                         'internal_rating', 'warning_flag']]

    def portfolio_el_summary(self) -> pd.DataFrame:
        """Expected loss summary by sector."""
        return (self.df.groupby('sector')
                .agg(
                    total_drawn=('current_drawn', 'sum'),
                    total_el=('el_amount', 'sum'),
                    client_count=('client_name', 'count'),
                    avg_rating=('internal_rating', 'mean')
                )
                .assign(el_rate=lambda x: x['total_el'] / x['total_drawn'] * 100)
                .sort_values('total_el', ascending=False))


# ===== RAROC CALCULATOR =====

def calculate_raroc(
    facility_amount: float,
    margin_pct: float,
    commitment_fee_pct: float,
    arrangement_fee_pct: float,
    pd_pct: float,
    lgd_pct: float,
    operating_cost_pct: float,
    capital_pct: float,
    hurdle_rate: float = 0.12
) -> dict:
    """Calculate risk-adjusted return on capital for a facility."""
    nii = facility_amount * margin_pct / 100
    fees = facility_amount * (commitment_fee_pct + arrangement_fee_pct) / 100
    expected_loss = facility_amount * pd_pct / 100 * lgd_pct / 100
    op_cost = facility_amount * operating_cost_pct / 100
    net_income = nii + fees - expected_loss - op_cost
    capital = facility_amount * capital_pct / 100
    raroc = net_income / capital if capital > 0 else 0

    return {
        'facility_amount': facility_amount,
        'nii': round(nii, 4),
        'fees': round(fees, 4),
        'expected_loss': round(expected_loss, 4),
        'op_cost': round(op_cost, 4),
        'net_income': round(net_income, 4),
        'capital': round(capital, 4),
        'raroc_pct': round(raroc * 100, 2),
        'meets_hurdle': raroc >= hurdle_rate
    }


# ===== PIPELINE PROBABILITY-WEIGHTED ANALYSIS =====

def pipeline_analysis(pipeline_df: pd.DataFrame) -> dict:
    """Summarise RM pipeline by stage and sector."""
    pipeline_df = pipeline_df.copy()
    pipeline_df['weighted_amount'] = (
        pipeline_df['deal_amount_m'] * pipeline_df['probability_pct'] / 100
    )
    by_stage = pipeline_df.groupby('stage').agg(
        deal_count=('deal_amount_m', 'count'),
        gross_amount_m=('deal_amount_m', 'sum'),
        weighted_amount_m=('weighted_amount', 'sum')
    )
    by_sector = pipeline_df.groupby('sector').agg(
        weighted_amount_m=('weighted_amount', 'sum')
    ).sort_values('weighted_amount_m', ascending=False)

    return {
        'by_stage': by_stage,
        'by_sector': by_sector,
        'total_gross_m': pipeline_df['deal_amount_m'].sum(),
        'total_weighted_m': pipeline_df['weighted_amount'].sum()
    }


# ===== EXAMPLE USAGE =====
if __name__ == '__main__':
    sample_data = pd.DataFrame({
        'client_name': ['Alpha Ltd', 'Beta plc', 'Gamma Corp', 'Delta Ltd'],
        'sector': ['Manufacturing', 'Retail', 'Manufacturing', 'Property'],
        'rm_name': ['J. Smith'] * 4,
        'facility_type': ['RCF', 'TL', 'RCF', 'TL'],
        'approved_limit': [10.0, 5.0, 8.0, 15.0],
        'current_drawn': [9.5, 5.0, 3.0, 14.0],
        'internal_rating': [5, 7, 4, 8],
        'annual_review_due': [
            date.today() - timedelta(days=10),
            date.today() + timedelta(days=15),
            date.today() + timedelta(days=60),
            date.today() + timedelta(days=5)
        ],
        'pd_pct': [0.5, 2.1, 0.3, 3.5],
        'lgd_pct': [45.0, 60.0, 40.0, 35.0]
    })

    portfolio = RMPortfolio(sample_data)
    print("=== REVIEW DASHBOARD ===")
    print(portfolio.review_dashboard().to_string())
    print("\n=== EARLY WARNING ===")
    print(portfolio.early_warning_screen().to_string())
    print("\n=== RAROC EXAMPLE ===")
    result = calculate_raroc(10.0, 2.5, 0.75, 0.25, 1.5, 45.0, 0.4, 10.0)
    for k, v in result.items():
        print(f"  {k}: {v}")
```

## 11. Interview Questions

**Q1: You have a client whose annual review is due next week and they have been slow to provide updated financials. What do you do?**
A: Escalate immediately. Contact the client's CFO or Finance Director directly, explain the regulatory and policy requirement, and set a firm deadline. In parallel, notify the credit team that financials are delayed and assess whether a short-term waiver is appropriate. Do not allow a facility to roll without updated financials unless formally approved by credit.

**Q2: A long-standing client requests a 30% increase in their revolving credit facility. How do you approach the credit request?**
A: Understand the purpose — is it genuine working capital growth or is the client trying to borrow more because cash flow is deteriorating? Review the most recent financials, understand the cash conversion cycle, model the new facility against DSCR and leverage covenants. If the credit case is sound, prepare a formal credit request with supporting analysis. If there are concerns, probe the purpose before submitting.

**Q3: What is the difference between an RM's role and a credit analyst's role?**
A: The RM is a client advocate who structures the deal and maintains the relationship. The credit analyst provides independent analysis of the credit risk. The RM should not write the credit paper in a way that cherry-picks data to support approval — the credit analyst's job is to challenge assumptions and provide an unbiased assessment. Both roles are necessary; neither should substitute for the other.

**Q4: How do you calculate RAROC and why does it matter?**
A: RAROC = (NII + fees − expected losses − operating costs) / economic capital. It matters because raw margin does not reflect risk. A 3% margin on a CCC-rated borrower requiring 25% capital allocation may generate lower RAROC than a 1.5% margin on an investment-grade borrower requiring 4% capital. RAROC allows comparison across facilities on a risk-adjusted basis.

**Q5: What early warning indicators would prompt you to place a client on your watch list?**
A: Chronic high utilisation of RCF (suggests cash flow stress), missed or waived covenant tests, deteriorating EBITDA margins in management accounts, management changes, legal proceedings, supplier or customer concentration losses, negative press coverage, and requests for facility amendments that weaken creditor protections.

## 12. Common Mistakes

**Mistake 1: Submitting a credit request with stale financials.** Annual accounts more than 18 months old are not sufficient for credit decisions. If audited accounts are late, management accounts must bridge the gap.

**Mistake 2: Conflating the borrower and the group.** When the borrowing entity is a holding company, cash flow analysis must trace cash upstreaming from operating subsidiaries. Dividend blocks, intercompany loans, and structural subordination must be addressed.

**Mistake 3: Failing to document the purpose of the facility.** Credit policy requires purpose to be documented. "General corporate purposes" is not acceptable for anything beyond a basic overdraft. Purpose matters because it drives structure — a capex loan should amortise, not revolve.

**Mistake 4: Overlooking the security position.** RMs sometimes treat security as an afterthought. Security must be valued, perfected, and documented before drawdown. A charge that has not been registered at Companies House within 21 days is void.

**Mistake 5: Not filing call reports.** An RM who does not document client meetings leaves the bank exposed if the client later claims the bank gave advice, promised certain terms, or failed to disclose risks. Call reports are a compliance requirement, not optional.

**Mistake 6: Advocacy over analysis.** An RM who is too close to their clients loses objectivity. The RM should be able to say "I cannot support this credit request" when the numbers do not support lending. Advocacy-driven credit requests that go wrong damage the RM's career and the bank's capital.

## 13. Case Studies

**Case Study 1: The Working Capital Trap**
A mid-market manufacturer requests a 50% increase in their RCF from £10m to £15m, citing growth in order book. The RM submits the credit request highlighting the growing order book. The credit analyst's review reveals that while revenues have grown 20%, accounts receivable days have stretched from 45 to 82 days, suggesting a deteriorating debtor book or aggressive revenue recognition. The growth is real, but the business is struggling to convert revenue to cash. The correct structure is a combination of selective invoice finance against verified receivables, not a larger uncommitted RCF. The RM, on reflection, agrees this better serves the client and reduces the bank's unsecured exposure.

**Case Study 2: The Connected Party Lending Problem**
An RM has a property developer client who requests a £5m term loan to acquire a commercial property. During client meeting preparation, the RM notes that the vendor of the property is the client's brother-in-law. This is a connected party transaction requiring enhanced due diligence. An independent valuation is required. The RM flags this to compliance and credit before submitting the credit request. The loan proceeds with an independent valuation showing a 15% below-market price — a genuine transaction that nonetheless required proper process.

**Case Study 3: Early Warning — the Retail Tenant**
An RM covers a property investment company whose primary income is lease rentals from retail tenants. In 2019, the RM notes that two anchor tenants have entered CVAs. Management accounts show rental income down 8% year-on-year. The RM proactively places the client on the internal watch list, requests monthly management accounts instead of quarterly, and begins preparing a facility review ahead of the scheduled date. By acting early, the bank is able to agree an orderly reduction in the RCF limit before the 2020 market downturn fully impacts.

## 14. Iterative Reinforcement

**Week 1 — Foundation:** Shadow an RM on client visits for three days. Map every client in the RM's portfolio to: facility type, utilisation, risk rating, and annual review date. Build the portfolio view in Excel.

**Week 2 — Process:** Assist an RM in preparing a credit request for a straightforward annual review. Gather financials, update the covenant tracker, and draft the credit request form.

**Week 3 — Analysis:** For one client, build a full financial spread covering three years of accounts, compute DSCR and leverage ratios, and compare to covenant thresholds. Present your findings to the RM.

**Week 4 — Integration:** Attend a client meeting with the RM. Observe how the RM probes for information relevant to the credit assessment. Write the meeting call report.
