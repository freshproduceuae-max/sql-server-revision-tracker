# M39 — Risk Management Reviews

## 1. Business Purpose

The Risk Management function's review role is the second line of defence (2LoD) in the bank's three-lines-of-defence model. While the first line (Relationship Managers, Credit Analysts, Credit Managers) originates and approves credit, the second line independently monitors portfolio quality, challenges underwriting standards, escalates deteriorating credits, and provides senior management and the Board with an accurate picture of credit risk.

The 2LoD credit risk function exists because of an inherent tension in commercial banking: the people closest to the clients (RMs and first-line CMs) have commercial incentives that can — even unintentionally — influence their credit assessments. The 2LoD provides an independent, portfolio-level view that is not subject to these incentives.

Portfolio review meetings operate on monthly and quarterly cycles. Monthly reviews focus on early warning indicators, watch list movements, and recent defaults. Quarterly reviews examine portfolio-level trends, risk appetite consumption, model performance, and sector exposures. Annual reviews inform ICAAP preparation and Board risk appetite setting.

The watch list is the 2LoD's primary tool for managing credits in pre-default deterioration. A watch-listed credit receives enhanced monitoring, more frequent management accounts, and regular review by both the RM and the 2LoD. OLEM (Other Loans Especially Mentioned) is a formal classification used in some banks for credits that are not yet impaired but show early signs of weakness — the category between standard and watch.

The credit risk reporting function aggregates data from the loan management system, the credit management system, and the ratings model to produce management information (MI) packs that are used at portfolio review meetings, presented to the Group Credit Risk Committee (GCRC), and ultimately reported to the Board Risk Committee.

## 2. Accounting Concepts

**IFRS 9 — Expected Credit Loss (ECL):** The accounting standard that governs how banks provision for loan losses. IFRS 9 requires banks to recognise expected credit losses — not just incurred losses — based on a three-stage model:
- **Stage 1:** Performing loans with no significant increase in credit risk since origination. 12-month ECL is recognised.
- **Stage 2:** Loans where there has been a significant increase in credit risk since origination. Lifetime ECL is recognised (materially higher provision).
- **Stage 3:** Credit-impaired loans (default has occurred). Lifetime ECL, calculated based on actual expected recovery.

The 2LoD monitors the distribution of loans across IFRS 9 stages as a portfolio health indicator. Movements from Stage 1 to Stage 2 (called "staging migrations") are an early warning signal that precedes defaults, often by 12 to 18 months.

**Provision Coverage Ratio:** Total loan loss provisions / total non-performing loans (NPL). The 2LoD monitors this ratio to ensure that provisions are adequate relative to the NPL stock. A declining coverage ratio without a corresponding improvement in recovery rates is a concern.

**SICR (Significant Increase in Credit Risk):** IFRS 9 requires banks to move loans from Stage 1 to Stage 2 when there has been a SICR. Banks use quantitative criteria (e.g., rating deterioration of two notches or more, PD more than doubling since origination) and qualitative criteria (30 days past due on any obligation, credit distress flag raised by RM, forbearance provided). The 2LoD monitors the bank's SICR triggers and challenges whether they are being applied consistently.

**Write-offs and Recoveries:** The 2LoD tracks the stock of written-off debt and recoveries received. Write-offs occur when a loan is deemed unrecoverable and is removed from the balance sheet (though collection efforts may continue). Recoveries are amounts subsequently received on written-off loans. The loss given default (LGD) experienced on completed defaults is compared to the model-assumed LGD to calibrate model accuracy.

## 3. Financial Concepts

**Portfolio Credit Quality Distribution:** The 2LoD reports the distribution of exposures by internal risk rating. Key metrics include: weighted average rating, percentage of portfolio in each rating band, and trend over time. A portfolio where the proportion of credits rated 7 or above (speculative to distressed) is increasing is deteriorating in quality.

**Risk-Weighted Assets (RWA) and Capital Consumption:** Each loan generates RWA based on the regulatory formula (standardised approach: fixed risk weights by asset class; IRB approach: PD, LGD, and EAD-driven risk weights). The 2LoD monitors total credit RWA against the bank's capital plan. Significant RWA growth requires corresponding capital generation or retention.

**Expected vs Actual Losses:** The 2LoD compares actual credit losses (defaults, provisions, write-offs) to the expected loss predicted by the bank's models. Consistent under-prediction means the models are too optimistic — either PD, LGD, or EAD assumptions are wrong. Over-prediction is less common but indicates overly conservative provisioning. This comparison (model back-testing) is presented at quarterly risk committee meetings.

**Concentration Risk Metrics:** The Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI) measures portfolio concentration. A portfolio evenly distributed across many borrowers has a low HHI; a portfolio concentrated in a few large exposures has a high HHI. The 2LoD calculates HHI by sector, geography, and single obligor. Single-name concentration is also tracked via the top 10 and top 20 obligor lists (expressed as a percentage of total exposure).

**Net Interest Margin (NIM) vs Risk Cost:** The 2LoD provides context to the income line by showing the net margin after deducting expected credit losses. A portfolio earning 2.5% average margin with 0.8% average expected loss has a risk-adjusted margin of 1.7%. If the portfolio is generating actual losses of 1.5%, the risk-adjusted margin is only 1.0%.

## 4. Statistical Concepts

**Probability of Default (PD) Model Performance:** The 2LoD runs back-tests of the PD model using the Gini coefficient and the Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic curve (AUROC). A Gini coefficient of 0.60 or above indicates a discriminatory model; below 0.40 indicates poor discrimination. The 2LoD presents model performance statistics at the quarterly model oversight committee.

**Through-the-Cycle vs Point-in-Time PD:** Banks calibrate PD on either a through-the-cycle (TtC) basis (long-run average default rate, less volatile) or a point-in-time (PiT) basis (current economic conditions, more sensitive to the cycle). IFRS 9 requires PiT PDs for provisioning. Regulatory capital under Basel IRB uses TtC PDs. The 2LoD understands both and ensures the correct calibration is used for each purpose.

**Loss Given Default (LGD) Calibration:** LGD is estimated from historical recovery data on completed defaults. LGD varies by: security type (unsecured ~ 55–65%, secured on property ~ 25–35%), seniority (senior secured < senior unsecured < subordinated), and macroeconomic conditions (LGD is higher in downturns, when asset values are depressed and buyers are scarce — this is called "downturn LGD"). The 2LoD monitors the difference between model LGD and realised LGD.

**Vintage Analysis:** Default rates by loan origination cohort. If loans originated in 2021–2022 (when rates were low and underwriting standards may have been looser) show higher default rates than earlier vintages, this signals underwriting quality degradation that must be investigated and reported.

**Stress Testing:** The 2LoD coordinates the annual credit risk stress test. Scenarios are applied to the portfolio (e.g., GDP declines 3%, unemployment rises to 8%, commercial property values fall 20%). The modelled impact on PD, LGD, RWA, provisions, and capital ratios is presented to the Board and provided to regulators as part of the ICAAP submission.

## 5. Regulatory Framework

**ICAAP (Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process):** The bank's own assessment of the capital it needs to support its risks. The 2LoD provides the credit risk section of the ICAAP, including: Pillar 1 credit RWA calculation, Pillar 2 add-ons for model risk and concentration risk, and stress test results showing capital adequacy under severe economic scenarios. The PRA reviews the ICAAP and may impose a Pillar 2A or Pillar 2B capital requirement if they believe the bank's self-assessment is insufficient.

**Pillar 1 vs Pillar 2:** Pillar 1 is the minimum regulatory capital requirement under Basel III (credit risk, market risk, operational risk). Pillar 2A is an additional requirement set by the PRA to cover risks not adequately captured in Pillar 1 (model risk, concentration risk, interest rate risk in the banking book). Pillar 2B is the capital conservation buffer set by stress testing. The 2LoD is responsible for calculating and defending the Pillar 2A credit risk add-on.

**Credit Risk Appetite Statement:** The Board approves a Credit Risk Appetite Statement that sets maximum tolerable levels of: non-performing loan ratio, credit loss rate, watch list percentage, concentration in any single sector or obligor, and portfolio average risk rating. The 2LoD reports monthly on appetite consumption against each metric. Breaches of appetite trigger escalation to the Board Risk Committee.

**Group Credit Risk Committee (GCRC):** The GCRC is the senior governance body for credit risk. Typically chaired by the Chief Risk Officer (CRO), with members including the CRO, CFO, Head of Corporate Banking, and Head of Credit Risk. The GCRC reviews and approves: credit risk appetite, credit policy changes, large individual credits above delegated authority, portfolio risk reports, and model changes. The 2LoD produces the MI pack for the GCRC.

**Model Risk Management:** The PRA's SS1/23 on model risk management requires banks to maintain a model inventory, perform regular model validation, and report model performance to a model risk committee. The 2LoD's credit risk models (PD model, LGD model, IFRS 9 staging model) must be validated annually by an independent model validation team.

**Basel III / CRR II:** The capital adequacy framework sets minimum capital ratios: Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) at least 4.5% of RWA (plus buffers, typically 7%+ in practice), Tier 1 at least 6%, and Total Capital at least 8%. The 2LoD's credit RWA calculations directly determine the denominator in these ratios.

## 6. Data Required

The 2LoD produces its MI pack from the following data sources:

- **Loan management system (LMS):** Facility-level data — obligor, limit, outstanding, maturity, product type, pricing, security reference.
- **Credit management system (CMS):** Internal risk rating per obligor, IFRS 9 stage per facility, ECL amount per facility, watch list and OLEM flags.
- **General ledger (GL):** Interest income, provision charges, write-offs, recoveries.
- **RWA calculation engine:** Risk-weighted assets by facility, aggregated by portfolio.
- **Covenant tracking system:** Current compliance status of all covenanted facilities.
- **EWI system (Early Warning Indicators):** Automated alerts from account conduct data (excesses, late payments, returned direct debits), external data feeds (credit bureau, press monitoring).
- **Historical default database:** Record of all defaults, recoveries, and completed workouts — used for model calibration and back-testing.

## 7. How Analysts Actually Work

**Monthly Portfolio Review Meeting Preparation:** The 2LoD analyst pulls the MI pack template 5–7 business days before the monthly review meeting. They run the standard SQL queries to extract: portfolio summary by rating band, new defaults since last meeting, IFRS 9 staging movements, watch list changes, and covenant breach summary. Data is validated for completeness and reconciled to the GL balance sheet.

**Watch List Management:** The 2LoD maintains the watch list — a curated list of credits requiring enhanced monitoring. New additions to the watch list are triggered by: RM request, covenant breach, 30+ days past due, rating downgrade to 7+, or 2LoD's own identification from MI. Each watch-listed credit has an action plan and a target date for resolution (return to standard monitoring, restructure, or referral to the workout team).

**Quarterly Credit Risk Report to GCRC:** A comprehensive 30–50 page report covering: portfolio performance vs risk appetite, sector deep-dives, model performance, regulatory capital adequacy, ICAAP updates, and stress test results. The 2LoD Head presents this to the GCRC and must be able to answer detailed questions on any aspect of the portfolio.

**ICAAP Preparation:** The credit risk section of the ICAAP is prepared annually by the 2LoD. It includes the PD and LGD model methodology and validation results, the stress test results, the concentration risk add-on calculation, and the model risk capital add-on. This document is reviewed by the Board, submitted to the PRA, and forms the basis for supervisory dialogue.

**New Business Quality Monitoring:** The 2LoD reviews a sample of newly approved credits each month to assess underwriting quality. The sample is drawn from recent approvals and assessed against a quality scorecard: are all required documents present, is the analysis adequate, are covenants appropriately set, are risk ratings reasonable? Results are reported to the Head of Credit Risk and fed back to Credit Managers.

**Model Performance Reporting:** Each quarter, the 2LoD runs the Gini coefficient and AUROC analysis on the PD model, comparing predicted defaults to actual defaults for the most recent 12-month window. Results are presented to the model risk committee. A deteriorating Gini coefficient triggers a model review process.

```excel
' Sheet: Rating_Distribution

' Columns: A=Rating, B=No of Clients, C=Limit(£m), D=Drawn(£m),

=D2-F2   ' Positive = more drawn in this rating band vs prior quarter

' Watch list % (ratings 7+)
=SUMIF(A:A,">="&7,D:D)/SUM(D2:D11)

' ===== VINTAGE ANALYSIS TABLE =====
' Sheet: Vintage
' Columns: A=Origination Year, B=Total Originations(£m), C=12m Default Rate,
'          D=24m Default Rate, E=36m Default Rate

' Default rate formula (for 12m)
=COUNTIFS(originations[Year],A2,defaults[Year],A2,
          defaults[Months_to_default],"<=12") / B2

' ===== ECL STAGING ANALYSIS =====
' Sheet: IFRS9_Staging
' Columns: A=Stage, B=Current Drawn(£m), C=Prior Quarter(£m),
'          D=ECL Coverage%, E=Prior ECL Coverage%

' ECL coverage calculation
=ecl_amount/drawn_amount * 100

' Stage 1→Stage 2 migration (concerning if large)
=B_Stage2_current - C_Stage2_prior

' ===== STRESS TEST IMPACT =====
' Sheet: Stress_Test

' Base case vs stressed
B5: Base PD (%)           = 0.80%
B6: Stressed PD (%)       = 2.40%   ' 3x base in severe scenario
B7: Base LGD (%)          = 40.00%
B8: Stressed LGD (%)      = 55.00%  ' higher in downturn
B9: Total Drawn (£m)      [input]
B10: Base EL (£m)         =B9*B5/100*B7/100
B11: Stressed EL (£m)     =B9*B6/100*B8/100
B12: Additional Provision =B11-B10
B13: Impact on CET1 (%)   =B12/total_rwa*100

' ===== GINI COEFFICIENT CALCULATION =====
' Sort by predicted PD descending, cumulate defaulters and non-defaulters
' Column A=Rank, B=PD, C=Actual Default(0/1), D=Cum Defaulters%,
'          E=Cum Total Population%, F=Random Model Cum%

' AUROC approximation using trapezoidal rule across ranked population
=SUMPRODUCT((E3:E100-E2:E99),(D3:D100+D2:D99)/2)
' Gini = 2*AUROC - 1
```

## 9. SQL Implementation

```sql
-- ===== PORTFOLIO RISK RATING DISTRIBUTION =====
SELECT
    c.internal_rating,
    COUNT(DISTINCT c.client_id) AS client_count,
    SUM(f.approved_limit) AS total_limit_m,
    SUM(f.current_drawn) AS total_drawn_m,
    SUM(f.current_drawn) / SUM(SUM(f.current_drawn)) OVER () * 100
        AS drawn_pct_of_portfolio,
    SUM(f.ecl_amount) AS total_ecl_m,
    SUM(f.ecl_amount) / NULLIF(SUM(f.current_drawn), 0) * 100
        AS ecl_coverage_pct
FROM clients c
JOIN facilities f ON c.client_id = f.client_id
WHERE f.status = 'ACTIVE'
GROUP BY c.internal_rating
ORDER BY c.internal_rating;

-- ===== IFRS 9 STAGE DISTRIBUTION AND MIGRATION =====
WITH current_period AS (
    SELECT
        client_id, facility_id,
        ifrs9_stage, ecl_amount, current_drawn,
        snapshot_date
    FROM ifrs9_snapshots
    WHERE snapshot_date = (SELECT MAX(snapshot_date) FROM ifrs9_snapshots)
),
prior_period AS (
    SELECT
        client_id, facility_id,
        ifrs9_stage AS prior_stage, ecl_amount AS prior_ecl,
        current_drawn AS prior_drawn
    FROM ifrs9_snapshots
    WHERE snapshot_date = (
        SELECT MAX(snapshot_date) FROM ifrs9_snapshots
        WHERE snapshot_date < (SELECT MAX(snapshot_date) FROM ifrs9_snapshots)
    )
)
SELECT
    pp.prior_stage,
    cp.ifrs9_stage AS current_stage,
    COUNT(*) AS facility_count,
    SUM(cp.current_drawn) AS drawn_m,
    SUM(cp.ecl_amount) AS ecl_m,
    SUM(cp.ecl_amount) - SUM(pp.prior_ecl) AS ecl_movement_m
FROM current_period cp
LEFT JOIN prior_period pp
    ON cp.client_id = pp.client_id AND cp.facility_id = pp.facility_id
GROUP BY pp.prior_stage, cp.ifrs9_stage
ORDER BY pp.prior_stage, cp.ifrs9_stage;

-- ===== WATCH LIST REPORT =====
SELECT
    c.client_name,
    c.rm_name,
    c.sector,
    c.internal_rating,
    wl.watch_list_date,
    wl.watch_list_reason,
    wl.action_plan,
    wl.target_resolution_date,
    f.current_drawn AS total_drawn_m,
    f.ecl_amount AS ecl_provision_m,
    DATEDIFF(DAY, wl.watch_list_date, GETDATE()) AS days_on_watch_list
FROM watch_list wl
JOIN clients c ON wl.client_id = c.client_id
JOIN (
    SELECT client_id,
           SUM(current_drawn) AS current_drawn,
           SUM(ecl_amount) AS ecl_amount
    FROM facilities
    WHERE status = 'ACTIVE'
    GROUP BY client_id
) f ON c.client_id = f.client_id
WHERE wl.is_active = 1
ORDER BY wl.watch_list_date ASC;

-- ===== MODEL PERFORMANCE: DEFAULT PREDICTION ACCURACY =====
-- Compare predicted PD at origination/last review with actual default outcome
SELECT
    CASE
        WHEN c.pd_at_origination < 0.5 THEN '0-0.5%'
        WHEN c.pd_at_origination < 1.0 THEN '0.5-1.0%'
        WHEN c.pd_at_origination < 2.0 THEN '1.0-2.0%'
        WHEN c.pd_at_origination < 5.0 THEN '2.0-5.0%'
        ELSE '5%+'
    END AS pd_band,
    COUNT(*) AS population,
    SUM(CASE WHEN d.default_id IS NOT NULL THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS actual_defaults,
    ROUND(SUM(CASE WHEN d.default_id IS NOT NULL THEN 1.0 ELSE 0 END)
          / COUNT(*) * 100, 2) AS actual_default_rate_pct,
    AVG(c.pd_at_origination) AS avg_model_pd_pct
FROM clients c
LEFT JOIN defaults d
    ON c.client_id = d.client_id
    AND d.default_date BETWEEN c.origination_date
        AND DATEADD(YEAR, 1, c.origination_date)
WHERE c.origination_date BETWEEN DATEADD(YEAR, -3, GETDATE())
    AND DATEADD(YEAR, -1, GETDATE())
GROUP BY
    CASE
        WHEN c.pd_at_origination < 0.5 THEN '0-0.5%'
        WHEN c.pd_at_origination < 1.0 THEN '0.5-1.0%'
        WHEN c.pd_at_origination < 2.0 THEN '1.0-2.0%'
        WHEN c.pd_at_origination < 5.0 THEN '2.0-5.0%'
        ELSE '5%+'
    END
ORDER BY avg_model_pd_pct;

-- ===== CONCENTRATION RISK: TOP 20 OBLIGORS =====
SELECT TOP 20
    c.client_name,
    c.sector,
    c.internal_rating,
    SUM(f.approved_limit) AS total_limit_m,
    SUM(f.current_drawn) AS total_drawn_m,
    SUM(f.current_drawn) / (SELECT SUM(current_drawn) FROM facilities
                             WHERE status = 'ACTIVE') * 100
        AS pct_of_total_portfolio
FROM clients c
JOIN facilities f ON c.client_id = f.client_id
WHERE f.status = 'ACTIVE'
GROUP BY c.client_name, c.sector, c.internal_rating
ORDER BY total_drawn_m DESC;
```

## 10. Python Implementation

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
from sklearn.metrics import roc_auc_score
import matplotlib
matplotlib.use('Agg')
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from typing import Optional, List, Tuple


# ===== PORTFOLIO RISK REPORTING ENGINE =====

class PortfolioRiskReport:
    """Second-line portfolio risk monitoring and reporting tools."""

    def __init__(self, portfolio_df: pd.DataFrame):
        """
        portfolio_df columns: client_id, client_name, sector, internal_rating,
        pd_pct, lgd_pct, approved_limit, current_drawn, ifrs9_stage, ecl_amount,
        watch_listed (bool), rm_name
        """
        self.df = portfolio_df.copy()

    def rating_distribution(self) -> pd.DataFrame:
        grp = self.df.groupby('internal_rating').agg(
            client_count=('client_id', 'nunique'),
            total_limit_m=('approved_limit', 'sum'),
            total_drawn_m=('current_drawn', 'sum'),
            total_ecl_m=('ecl_amount', 'sum')
        ).reset_index()
        total_drawn = grp['total_drawn_m'].sum()
        grp['drawn_pct'] = grp['total_drawn_m'] / total_drawn * 100
        grp['ecl_coverage_pct'] = grp['total_ecl_m'] / grp['total_drawn_m'].replace(0, np.nan) * 100
        return grp

    def ifrs9_stage_summary(self) -> pd.DataFrame:
        return (self.df.groupby('ifrs9_stage')
                .agg(
                    facility_count=('client_id', 'count'),
                    drawn_m=('current_drawn', 'sum'),
                    ecl_m=('ecl_amount', 'sum')
                )
                .assign(ecl_coverage=lambda x: x['ecl_m'] / x['drawn_m'] * 100))

    def sector_concentration(self,
                              sector_limits: Optional[dict] = None) -> pd.DataFrame:
        total = self.df['current_drawn'].sum()
        sec = (self.df.groupby('sector')
               .agg(drawn_m=('current_drawn', 'sum'),
                    client_count=('client_id', 'nunique'))
               .assign(pct_of_portfolio=lambda x: x['drawn_m'] / total * 100)
               .sort_values('drawn_m', ascending=False))
        if sector_limits:
            sec['limit_pct'] = sec.index.map(lambda s: sector_limits.get(s, 20.0))
            sec['headroom_pct'] = sec['limit_pct'] - sec['pct_of_portfolio']
            sec['status'] = sec['headroom_pct'].apply(
                lambda h: 'BREACH' if h < 0 else 'WARNING' if h < 3 else 'OK'
            )
        return sec

    def watch_list_report(self) -> pd.DataFrame:
        wl = self.df[self.df['watch_listed']].copy()
        return wl[['client_name', 'sector', 'rm_name', 'internal_rating',
                    'current_drawn', 'ecl_amount', 'ifrs9_stage']].sort_values(
                        'internal_rating', ascending=False)

    def top_obligors(self, n: int = 20) -> pd.DataFrame:
        total = self.df['current_drawn'].sum()
        top = (self.df.groupby(['client_name', 'sector', 'internal_rating'])
               .agg(drawn_m=('current_drawn', 'sum'))
               .reset_index()
               .sort_values('drawn_m', ascending=False)
               .head(n)
               .assign(pct_of_portfolio=lambda x: x['drawn_m'] / total * 100))
        top['cumulative_pct'] = top['pct_of_portfolio'].cumsum()
        return top

    def risk_appetite_dashboard(self,
                                 appetite: dict) -> pd.DataFrame:
        """
        appetite keys: max_watch_list_pct, max_stage2_pct, max_npl_ratio,
                       max_ecl_coverage_min, max_avg_rating
        """
        total = self.df['current_drawn'].sum()
        watch_pct = self.df[self.df['watch_listed']]['current_drawn'].sum() / total * 100
        stage2_pct = self.df[self.df['ifrs9_stage'] == 2]['current_drawn'].sum() / total * 100
        npl_pct = self.df[self.df['ifrs9_stage'] == 3]['current_drawn'].sum() / total * 100
        avg_rating = (self.df['internal_rating'] * self.df['current_drawn']).sum() / total
        ecl_coverage = self.df['ecl_amount'].sum() / self.df['current_drawn'].sum() * 100

        metrics = {
            'Watch List %': (watch_pct, appetite.get('max_watch_list_pct', 15.0)),
            'Stage 2 %': (stage2_pct, appetite.get('max_stage2_pct', 20.0)),
            'NPL Ratio %': (npl_pct, appetite.get('max_npl_ratio', 3.0)),
            'Wtd Avg Rating': (avg_rating, appetite.get('max_avg_rating', 5.5)),
            'ECL Coverage %': (ecl_coverage, appetite.get('min_ecl_coverage', 1.0))
        }
        rows = []
        for metric, (actual, limit) in metrics.items():
            breached = actual > limit if metric != 'ECL Coverage %' else actual < limit
            rows.append({
                'Metric': metric,
                'Actual': round(actual, 2),
                'Appetite Limit': limit,
                'Status': 'BREACH' if breached else 'OK'
            })
        return pd.DataFrame(rows)


# ===== MODEL PERFORMANCE: GINI AND AUROC =====

def calculate_gini(y_true: np.ndarray, y_scores: np.ndarray) -> float:
    """
    Calculate Gini coefficient for a PD model.
    y_true: binary array (1=default, 0=non-default)
    y_scores: predicted PD scores (higher = more risky)
    """
    auroc = roc_auc_score(y_true, y_scores)
    return 2 * auroc - 1


def pd_model_backtest(predictions_df: pd.DataFrame,
                       pd_col: str = 'predicted_pd',
                       default_col: str = 'actual_default') -> dict:
    """
    Back-test PD model performance.
    predictions_df: must contain pd_col (predicted PD%) and default_col (0/1)
    """
    df = predictions_df[[pd_col, default_col]].dropna()
    y_true = df[default_col].values
    y_scores = df[pd_col].values / 100.0  # convert % to proportion

    gini = calculate_gini(y_true, y_scores)
    auroc = roc_auc_score(y_true, y_scores)

    # PD band comparison
    df = df.copy()
    df['pd_band'] = pd.cut(df[pd_col],
                            bins=[0, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 5.0, 100],
                            labels=['0-0.5%', '0.5-1%', '1-2%', '2-5%', '5%+'])
    band_summary = df.groupby('pd_band').agg(
        population=(default_col, 'count'),
        actual_defaults=(default_col, 'sum'),
        avg_predicted_pd=(pd_col, 'mean')
    )
    band_summary['actual_default_rate'] = (
        band_summary['actual_defaults'] / band_summary['population'] * 100
    )

    return {
        'gini_coefficient': round(gini, 4),
        'auroc': round(auroc, 4),
        'model_grade': ('Good' if gini >= 0.6
                        else 'Acceptable' if gini >= 0.4
                        else 'Poor'),
        'band_comparison': band_summary
    }


# ===== STRESS TEST ENGINE =====

def credit_stress_test(
    portfolio_df: pd.DataFrame,
    scenarios: dict
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Apply macroeconomic stress scenarios to the portfolio.

    scenarios: dict of {scenario_name: {pd_multiplier, lgd_add, drawn_growth}}
    portfolio_df: must have current_drawn, pd_pct, lgd_pct, ecl_amount
    """
    base_el = (portfolio_df['current_drawn']
               * portfolio_df['pd_pct'] / 100
               * portfolio_df['lgd_pct'] / 100).sum()

    results = [{'Scenario': 'Base Case',
                 'Total Drawn (£m)': portfolio_df['current_drawn'].sum(),
                 'Expected Loss (£m)': round(base_el, 2),
                 'EL Rate (%)': round(base_el / portfolio_df['current_drawn'].sum() * 100, 2)}]

    for scenario_name, params in scenarios.items():
        stressed_drawn = portfolio_df['current_drawn'] * (1 + params.get('drawn_growth', 0))
        stressed_pd = portfolio_df['pd_pct'] * params.get('pd_multiplier', 1.0)
        stressed_lgd = (portfolio_df['lgd_pct']
                        + params.get('lgd_add', 0)).clip(upper=100)
        stressed_el = (stressed_drawn * stressed_pd / 100 * stressed_lgd / 100).sum()
        results.append({
            'Scenario': scenario_name,
            'Total Drawn (£m)': round(stressed_drawn.sum(), 2),
            'Expected Loss (£m)': round(stressed_el, 2),
            'EL Rate (%)': round(stressed_el / stressed_drawn.sum() * 100, 2)
        })

    return pd.DataFrame(results)
```

## 11. Interview Questions

**Q1: What is the difference between Pillar 1 and Pillar 2 credit risk capital?**
A: Pillar 1 is the minimum regulatory capital calculated using the standardised or IRB formula under Basel III/CRR. It covers expected credit risk based on PD, LGD, and EAD for each facility. Pillar 2A is an additional supervisory capital requirement set by the PRA to cover risks not fully captured by Pillar 1 — specifically model risk (if the bank's models underestimate true risk), concentration risk (single-name or sector), and interest rate risk in the banking book. The 2LoD is responsible for calculating and defending both.

**Q2: How do you determine whether a loan should move from IFRS 9 Stage 1 to Stage 2?**
A: Stage 2 is triggered by a significant increase in credit risk (SICR) since origination. Quantitative triggers include: internal rating deteriorating by two or more notches, PD more than doubling relative to origination PD, or 30 days past due on any obligation. Qualitative triggers include: forbearance granted, watch list placement, or RM raising a distress flag. The key question is whether the bank's ability to collect the contractual cash flows has materially worsened since the loan was made.

**Q3: What does a Gini coefficient of 0.35 on your PD model tell you, and what do you do about it?**
A: A Gini of 0.35 indicates poor discrimination — the model is barely better than random at ranking borrowers by default likelihood. This is a material model risk issue. I would report it immediately to the model risk committee, escalate to the CRO, and initiate a model review. In the interim, I would recommend applying a conservative PD scalar (increasing all PDs by a factor) and increasing the Pillar 2A model risk add-on. I would also investigate whether the poor performance is due to model design issues, data quality, or a structural change in the economy that the model was not calibrated for.

**Q4: Describe how you would prepare the credit risk section of the ICAAP.**
A: I would: (1) calculate Pillar 1 credit RWA for all portfolios; (2) run the annual stress test and document the methodology, scenario design, and results; (3) calculate Pillar 2A add-ons for concentration risk and model risk; (4) assess the adequacy of the IFRS 9 provision coverage; (5) document model performance statistics; and (6) present a summary showing that the bank's capital, including buffers, is sufficient to absorb losses under the stress scenario with a margin above regulatory minima.

**Q5: A new sector emerges (e.g., renewable energy project finance) where the bank has limited historical data. How does the 2LoD manage the credit risk?**
A: With limited internal data, the PD and LGD models cannot be reliably calibrated. The 2LoD should: recommend a more conservative internal rating floor for the sector; apply external data (Moody's sector default statistics, comparable peer portfolios); set a lower sector concentration limit until sufficient internal data is accumulated; require enhanced credit paper quality for transactions in the sector; and initiate a data collection program to build the internal track record over time.

## 12. Common Mistakes

**Mistake 1: Confusing IFRS 9 Stage 2 with "bad" credits.** Stage 2 is not default — it simply means credit risk has increased significantly since origination. Many Stage 2 loans pay as agreed and never default. The 2LoD must not stigmatise Stage 2 borrowers but must use the classification to ensure adequate monitoring and provisioning.

**Mistake 2: Back-testing models on the same data used for calibration.** The Gini coefficient must be calculated on an out-of-sample population — not the development sample. Using the development sample overstates model performance. The 2LoD must maintain a hold-out validation dataset.

**Mistake 3: Reporting averages without distributions.** Average portfolio rating of 4.5 masks whether the portfolio is a healthy distribution centred on 4–5, or a bimodal distribution with many 1–2 credits and many 8–9 credits. The distribution provides more insight than the average.

**Mistake 4: Treating the watch list as static.** A credit placed on the watch list six months ago with an action plan that has not been executed is a much larger concern than a recently added credit with a clear path to resolution. The watch list must be actively managed, not just maintained.

**Mistake 5: Letting concentration limits become nominal.** If sector limits are consistently breached without consequence, they cease to be effective risk management tools. The 2LoD must enforce concentration limits or formally recommend revising them, not simply note breaches and move on.

## 13. Case Studies

**Case Study 1: The Model Failure**
A bank's PD model for the commercial property sector shows a Gini of 0.62 over 2015–2019 (pre-pandemic). During 2020–2021, the 2LoD runs the back-test and finds the Gini has fallen to 0.28. Investigation reveals the model was heavily weighted on interest coverage ratio, which remained healthy for property companies even as occupancy rates collapsed (because interest rates fell, reducing interest costs). The model failed to capture occupancy/rental income stress. The 2LoD recommends a model override: all property sector ratings are manually reviewed with an explicit assessment of occupancy rates and lease expiry schedules.

**Case Study 2: The Concentration Creep**
A bank's sector limit for retail is 15% of total commercial lending. Over three years, the 2LoD's monthly reports show the retail concentration rising: 12% → 14% → 15.5% → 16.8%. Each month, the breach is noted in the portfolio review meeting minutes but no action is taken because the credits are individually performing. In 2023, a wave of retail CVAs triggers correlated losses across the portfolio. The 2LoD's post-mortem highlights that the concentration limit breach was identified 18 months before the losses materialised, but no corrective action was taken. The lesson: concentration limits must be enforced, not just monitored.
