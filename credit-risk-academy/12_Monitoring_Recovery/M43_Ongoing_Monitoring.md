# M43 — Ongoing Monitoring

## 1. Business Purpose

Ongoing monitoring is the process by which a bank maintains continuous oversight of credit exposures after initial drawdown. Lending is not a one-time decision: a borrower's financial condition, business outlook, and capacity to repay can change materially over the life of a loan. The business purpose of ongoing monitoring is to detect deterioration early enough to take protective action — whether that means requiring additional collateral, reducing exposure, tightening covenants, or escalating to the Restructuring & Recovery team.

From a commercial perspective, ongoing monitoring also supports relationship management. An RM who monitors a borrower closely is better positioned to identify cross-sell opportunities, understand the client's evolving needs, and maintain the trusted adviser relationship that generates repeat business.

Regulators mandate ongoing monitoring because undisclosed deterioration in a loan book can cause solvency crises. The 2008 financial crisis demonstrated what happens when banks fail to monitor commercial real estate and leveraged lending portfolios. The PRA, EBA, and FSB have all reinforced expectations around portfolio oversight since then.

The annual review cycle is the backbone of ongoing monitoring for most commercial credit facilities. A full credit re-assessment is performed every 12 months — or more frequently if the facility is large, the credit is weak, or the sector is volatile. In addition to the annual review, banks maintain a calendar of intermediate monitoring touchpoints throughout the year.

Key objectives:
- Maintain an accurate, up-to-date risk rating for every obligor
- Ensure IFRS 9 staging is correct on a monthly basis
- Detect covenant breaches or potential breaches before they crystallise
- Maintain accurate collateral valuations
- Identify SICR (Significant Increase in Credit Risk) triggers promptly
- Ensure the credit file contains current information for regulatory inspection
- Support portfolio-level reporting (sector concentration, average PD, migration matrices)

## 2. Accounting Concepts

**IFRS 9 Staging.** Under IFRS 9 Financial Instruments (effective 1 January 2018), banks classify financial assets into three stages:
- Stage 1: No significant increase in credit risk since origination. 12-month ECL provision.
- Stage 2: Significant increase in credit risk (SICR) since origination but not yet credit-impaired. Lifetime ECL provision.
- Stage 3: Credit-impaired (defaulted). Lifetime ECL provision on an individual basis (specific provision).

Ongoing monitoring must feed into the monthly staging assessment. The monitoring team must flag any SICR indicators identified through the monitoring process so that the finance function can reclassify exposures from Stage 1 to Stage 2.

**Provision accounting.** A collective provision (Stage 1 and 2) is calculated using PD × LGD × EAD. An individual/specific provision (Stage 3) is the difference between the gross carrying amount and the present value of estimated future cash flows discounted at the original effective interest rate. When an ongoing review reveals deterioration, the credit officer must estimate revised cash flows and update the specific provision.

**Effective Interest Rate (EIR).** Under IFRS 9, interest income on Stage 3 assets is recognised on the net carrying amount (gross minus provision), whereas Stage 1 and 2 interest is recognised on the gross amount. A move from Stage 1 to Stage 3 therefore reduces reported interest income even before a write-off occurs.

**Loan origination fees and transaction costs** are amortised over the life of the loan under IFRS 9 using the EIR method. Monitoring must track the amortisation schedule when facilities are extended or restructured.

**Credit loss allowance movement** is reported in the P&L as 'impairment charge on financial instruments.' A bank's monitoring process directly drives the quarterly P&L line for impairments.

## 3. Financial Concepts

**Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR).** DSCR = EBITDA (or Net Operating Income for property) / (interest + scheduled principal repayments). A DSCR below 1.0x means the borrower cannot service its debt from operating cash flows. Monitoring involves calculating DSCR from each set of financial statements received.

**Leverage Ratio.** Total Net Debt / EBITDA. The appropriate covenant level depends on sector: a stable utility may carry 5x leverage; a cyclical manufacturer should be below 2.5x. Monitoring tracks the trend: leverage increasing over successive periods signals deterioration even if a covenant has not yet been breached.

**Interest Coverage Ratio (ICR).** EBITDA / Net Interest Expense. For real estate, the Net Interest Coverage Ratio (NICR) is commonly used.

**Loan-to-Value (LTV).** For secured lending, LTV = Outstanding Loan Balance / Current Market Value of Security. Monitoring requires periodic revaluation of security to ensure LTV remains within agreed limits. Property values can change significantly; a 30% fall in commercial property values (as seen in 2007-2009) can move LTV from 65% to 93%.

**Current Account / Revolving Facility Utilisation.** For revolving credit facilities, monitoring tracks daily utilisation as a percentage of the facility limit. An obligor consistently drawing near their full limit indicates potential liquidity stress.

**Cash flow forecasting.** The annual review includes a forward-looking assessment of cash generation. Monitoring during the year checks whether actual performance is tracking the forecast. Material adverse variance should trigger an out-of-cycle review.

**Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) and Days Payable Outstanding (DPO).** Extending DSO (debtors taking longer to pay) or shortening DPO (paying suppliers faster) can indicate working capital stress. Monitoring should pick up deteriorating working capital metrics even when headline EBITDA looks stable.

## 4. Statistical Concepts

**PD migration matrices.** Credit risk management teams track how obligors migrate between risk rating grades over time. A migration matrix shows, for example, that 5% of Grade 3 obligors in year 1 were downgraded to Grade 5 by year 2. Monitoring feeds the migration matrix: every rating change triggered by a periodic review is captured.

**Vintage analysis.** Loans are grouped by the year of origination (vintage). By comparing default rates across vintages, the bank can identify whether underwriting standards declined in a particular period. The monitoring data provides the performance observations for each vintage.

**Exposure at Default (EAD) for revolvers.** For revolving credit facilities, EAD is not simply the drawn balance. Under Basel/IFRS 9, the Credit Conversion Factor (CCF) is applied to the undrawn portion to estimate how much of the headroom will be drawn before default. Monitoring of daily utilisation provides the data needed to calibrate CCF models.

**Time series analysis.** A borrower's financial metrics over time (revenue growth, margin trajectory, leverage trend) are more informative than any single data point. Analysts should use simple trend analysis to contextualise each new set of accounts.

**Percentile-based early warning thresholds.** Rather than fixed covenant levels, sophisticated monitoring systems also flag obligors whose metrics fall below sector-specific percentiles. An obligor whose DSCR drops to the 10th percentile for its sector is a concern even if the absolute level remains above covenant.

**Concentration risk metrics.** Portfolio monitoring tracks concentration by sector, geography, product type, and single-name exposure. Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI) is sometimes used to measure concentration. Monitoring data feeds these metrics.

## 5. Regulatory Framework

**PRA SS3/19 (Supervisory Statement on Internal Ratings-Based models).** Requires banks to maintain adequate model validation and back-testing, which depends on good monitoring data.

**EBA Guidelines on Loan Origination and Monitoring (EBA/GL/2020/06).** Chapter 7 covers ongoing monitoring requirements in detail. Key requirements:
- Institutions shall implement a monitoring framework to identify, at an early stage, any deterioration in the credit quality of borrowers.
- Monitoring shall be proportionate to the size, nature, complexity and risk profile of the credit facility.
- Quarterly monitoring at minimum for all credit facilities above materiality thresholds.
- Annual full credit review for performing exposures; more frequent for non-performing.
- Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) risk factors must be considered in monitoring (EBA/GL/2021/07).

**IFRS 9 (as adopted by the UK and EU).** The staging assessment is a monthly obligation. Paragraph 5.5.4 specifies that an entity shall assess whether credit risk has increased significantly at each reporting date. In practice, banks run monthly staging reviews using automated models for homogeneous portfolios and judgment-based reviews for large individual exposures.

**Basel III / CRR2 (Capital Requirements Regulation).** Article 172 (IRB approach) requires continuous monitoring of risk estimates and back-testing. Banks must review their risk grade assignments at least annually.

**UK Corporate Insolvency and Governance Act 2020.** Introduced the new Restructuring Plan (Part 26A Companies Act 2006) and Moratorium mechanism. Banks monitoring distressed credits must understand these tools as potential routes for borrower restructuring.

**Money Laundering Regulations 2017.** Ongoing monitoring includes an AML/KYC dimension: banks must conduct ongoing due diligence on business relationships, scrutinise transactions for consistency with knowledge of the customer, and keep documents and information up to date.

## 6. Data Required

**From the borrower (contractual obligations in the facility agreement):**
- Annual statutory accounts (within 120–180 days of year end)
- Quarterly management accounts (within 45 days of quarter end)
- Quarterly compliance certificate confirming covenant compliance
- Annual budget and updated financial forecasts
- Details of any material adverse change, litigation, or disposal
- Updated director/shareholder information
- Annual insurance confirmation for secured assets

**From internal bank systems:**
- Daily utilisation data from the core banking system (drawn balance, available headroom)
- Payment history (interest payments, principal repayments — on time or late)
- Limit and facility structure data (maturity, covenants, security)
- Current risk rating and last review date
- IFRS 9 stage and provision amount
- Collateral values and valuation dates

**From external data sources:**
- Companies House: annual accounts filings, confirmation statements, director changes, charges registered
- Credit bureau data: credit scores, trade payment data (Dun & Bradstreet, Experian)
- News monitoring: Google Alerts, RefinitivWorld-Check, LexisNexis
- Land Registry: property title searches (for secured lending)
- Market data: stock price (for listed borrowers), CDS spreads, bond prices
- Sector data: industry reports, trade body publications
- Central bank data: Bank of England base rate, credit conditions surveys

**For property-secured lending:**
- RICS Red Book valuation (instructed annually or more frequently in volatile markets)
- Rental income schedules and tenant covenant reviews
- Planning permission status
- Structural surveys (on acquisition)
- Environmental searches

## 7. How Analysts Actually Work

**Annual Review Cycle.** The portfolio manager (RM or credit analyst depending on bank structure) starts the annual review 8-10 weeks before the facility's anniversary date. A checklist is generated from the credit system:
1. Request financial statements and compliance certificate from the borrower
2. Chase if not received within agreed deadlines
3. Analyse financials: build or update the financial model
4. Calculate covenant compliance
5. Update the risk rating using the bank's internal rating model
6. Write the credit paper (typically 5-30 pages depending on facility size)
7. Submit to credit committee for approval
8. Update the credit system with new rating, stage, and review date

**Covenant monitoring between annual reviews.** Quarterly compliance certificates arrive from the borrower. The credit analyst:
1. Extracts key financial metrics from the certificate
2. Independently calculates covenants from the management accounts (to verify borrower's arithmetic)
3. Calculates headroom vs. covenant thresholds
4. Updates the covenant monitoring system
5. If headroom < 15% of the covenant threshold, escalates to a senior credit officer

**Utilisation monitoring.** For revolving credit facilities (overdrafts, revolving credit facilities), the system generates daily reports:
- Facilities drawn > 80% of limit: amber flag
- Facilities drawn > 95% of limit: red flag, escalate to RM
- Facilities consistently at limit for > 5 consecutive days: flag for review

**Site visits.** RMs are typically required to visit each borrower at least annually (more frequently for watchlist names). The site visit report is filed in the credit system and feeds into the annual review. A good site visit covers: business performance vs. plan, management changes, operational observations (is the car park full? is production running?), sector outlook.

**Out-of-cycle reviews.** Triggered by:
- EWI alerts (see M45)
- Covenant breach or near-breach
- Material adverse news (fire, product recall, key customer loss)
- Management change at senior level
- Missed or late payment
- Request from the borrower for waiver or amendment
- Downgrade of the borrower's credit rating by an external agency
- Sector-wide shock (pandemic, regulatory change, commodity price spike)

**Credit file maintenance.** Every material document must be uploaded to the credit system within agreed SLAs. Regulatory inspections check file completeness. Common file requirements:
- Signed facility agreement and all amendments
- Latest audited accounts
- Latest management accounts
- Latest valuation report
- Latest compliance certificate
- Latest credit paper and approval
- Site visit reports
- Any waiver or amendment letters
- Correspondence log

## 9. SQL Implementation

```sql
-- ============================================================
-- M43: Ongoing Monitoring SQL Queries
-- Database: SQL Server (T-SQL)
-- ============================================================

-- ------------------------------------------------------------
-- Query 1: Portfolio Monitoring Dashboard — All Active Facilities
-- Shows current status, stage, last review, next review due
-- ------------------------------------------------------------
SELECT
    f.facility_id,
    f.facility_reference,
    c.customer_name,
    c.sector_code,
    f.facility_type,
    f.limit_amount,
    f.drawn_balance,
    CAST(f.drawn_balance AS DECIMAL(18,4)) / NULLIF(f.limit_amount, 0) * 100 AS utilisation_pct,
    r.risk_grade,
    r.pd_estimate,
    s.ifrs9_stage,
    s.ecl_provision,
    rev.last_review_date,
    rev.next_review_due,
    DATEDIFF(DAY, GETDATE(), rev.next_review_due) AS days_to_next_review,
    CASE
        WHEN DATEDIFF(DAY, GETDATE(), rev.next_review_due) < 0  THEN 'OVERDUE'
        WHEN DATEDIFF(DAY, GETDATE(), rev.next_review_due) < 30 THEN 'DUE_SOON'
        ELSE 'ON_TRACK'
    END AS review_status,
    cov.covenant_status,
    ewi.ewi_alert_level
FROM Facilities f
JOIN Customers c ON f.customer_id = c.customer_id
JOIN RiskRatings r ON f.facility_id = r.facility_id
    AND r.effective_to IS NULL  -- current rating only
JOIN IFRS9Staging s ON f.facility_id = s.facility_id
    AND s.staging_date = (
        SELECT MAX(staging_date)
        FROM IFRS9Staging
        WHERE facility_id = f.facility_id
    )
JOIN ReviewSchedule rev ON f.facility_id = rev.facility_id
LEFT JOIN CovenantSummary cov ON f.facility_id = cov.facility_id
    AND cov.test_date = (
        SELECT MAX(test_date)
        FROM CovenantSummary
        WHERE facility_id = f.facility_id
    )
LEFT JOIN EWIAlerts ewi ON f.facility_id = ewi.facility_id
    AND ewi.alert_date = (
        SELECT MAX(alert_date)
        FROM EWIAlerts
        WHERE facility_id = f.facility_id
    )
WHERE f.facility_status = 'ACTIVE'
ORDER BY
    CASE WHEN rev.next_review_due < GETDATE() THEN 0 ELSE 1 END,
    rev.next_review_due ASC;


-- ------------------------------------------------------------
-- Query 2: Utilisation Monitoring — RCFs with High Utilisation
-- Run daily; flags facilities above threshold
-- ------------------------------------------------------------
WITH UtilisationCurrent AS (
    SELECT
        facility_id,
        balance_date,
        drawn_balance,
        facility_limit,
        CAST(drawn_balance AS DECIMAL(18,4)) / NULLIF(facility_limit, 0) * 100 AS util_pct
    FROM DailyBalances
    WHERE balance_date = CAST(GETDATE() AS DATE)
),
UtilisationTrailing AS (
    SELECT
        facility_id,
        AVG(CAST(drawn_balance AS DECIMAL(18,4)) / NULLIF(facility_limit, 0) * 100)
            AS avg_util_30d,
        MAX(CAST(drawn_balance AS DECIMAL(18,4)) / NULLIF(facility_limit, 0) * 100)
            AS peak_util_30d,
        COUNT(CASE WHEN drawn_balance >= facility_limit * 0.95 THEN 1 END)
            AS days_near_limit_30d
    FROM DailyBalances
    WHERE balance_date >= DATEADD(DAY, -30, CAST(GETDATE() AS DATE))
    GROUP BY facility_id
)
SELECT
    f.facility_reference,
    c.customer_name,
    f.facility_type,
    uc.drawn_balance,
    uc.facility_limit,
    uc.util_pct AS current_util_pct,
    ut.avg_util_30d,
    ut.peak_util_30d,
    ut.days_near_limit_30d,
    CASE
        WHEN uc.util_pct >= 95 THEN 'RED'
        WHEN uc.util_pct >= 80 THEN 'AMBER'
        ELSE 'GREEN'
    END AS utilisation_rag,
    f.expiry_date,
    rm.rm_name,
    rm.rm_email
FROM Facilities f
JOIN Customers c ON f.customer_id = c.customer_id
JOIN UtilisationCurrent uc ON f.facility_id = uc.facility_id
JOIN UtilisationTrailing ut ON f.facility_id = ut.facility_id
JOIN RelationshipManagers rm ON f.rm_id = rm.rm_id
WHERE f.facility_type IN ('RCF', 'OVERDRAFT', 'REVOLVER')
    AND f.facility_status = 'ACTIVE'
    AND uc.util_pct > 60  -- only show elevated utilisation
ORDER BY uc.util_pct DESC;


-- ------------------------------------------------------------
-- Query 3: IFRS 9 Stage Migration — Monthly Monitoring
-- Compares current stage to prior month stage
-- ------------------------------------------------------------
WITH CurrentMonth AS (
    SELECT facility_id, ifrs9_stage, ecl_provision
    FROM IFRS9Staging
    WHERE staging_date = EOMONTH(GETDATE(), -1)  -- prior month end
),
PriorMonth AS (
    SELECT facility_id, ifrs9_stage, ecl_provision
    FROM IFRS9Staging
    WHERE staging_date = EOMONTH(GETDATE(), -2)  -- two months ago
)
SELECT
    f.facility_reference,
    c.customer_name,
    pm.ifrs9_stage AS stage_prior_month,
    cm.ifrs9_stage AS stage_current_month,
    CASE
        WHEN cm.ifrs9_stage > pm.ifrs9_stage THEN 'DOWNGRADE'
        WHEN cm.ifrs9_stage < pm.ifrs9_stage THEN 'UPGRADE'
        ELSE 'NO_CHANGE'
    END AS migration_direction,
    pm.ecl_provision AS provision_prior,
    cm.ecl_provision AS provision_current,
    cm.ecl_provision - pm.ecl_provision AS provision_movement,
    f.drawn_balance
FROM Facilities f
JOIN Customers c ON f.customer_id = c.customer_id
JOIN CurrentMonth cm ON f.facility_id = cm.facility_id
JOIN PriorMonth pm ON f.facility_id = pm.facility_id
WHERE cm.ifrs9_stage <> pm.ifrs9_stage  -- only show migrations
ORDER BY
    CASE migration_direction WHEN 'DOWNGRADE' THEN 1 WHEN 'UPGRADE' THEN 2 ELSE 3 END,
    ABS(cm.ecl_provision - pm.ecl_provision) DESC;


-- ------------------------------------------------------------
-- Query 4: Annual Review Pipeline — Next 90 Days
-- Allows portfolio managers to plan workload
-- ------------------------------------------------------------
SELECT
    c.customer_name,
    f.facility_reference,
    f.drawn_balance + ISNULL(f.undrawn_commitment, 0) AS total_exposure,
    r.risk_grade,
    s.ifrs9_stage,
    rev.next_review_due,
    DATEDIFF(DAY, GETDATE(), rev.next_review_due) AS days_until_due,
    rev.last_review_date,
    rev.review_analyst,
    rev.review_status,
    -- Flag if accounts are due before review can be completed
    acc.next_accounts_due,
    CASE
        WHEN acc.next_accounts_due > rev.next_review_due
        THEN 'WARNING: accounts due after review'
        ELSE 'OK'
    END AS accounts_timing_flag
FROM Facilities f
JOIN Customers c ON f.customer_id = c.customer_id
JOIN RiskRatings r ON f.facility_id = r.facility_id
    AND r.effective_to IS NULL
JOIN IFRS9Staging s ON f.facility_id = s.facility_id
    AND s.staging_date = EOMONTH(GETDATE(), -1)
JOIN ReviewSchedule rev ON f.facility_id = rev.facility_id
LEFT JOIN AccountingDeadlines acc ON c.customer_id = acc.customer_id
WHERE rev.next_review_due BETWEEN GETDATE() AND DATEADD(DAY, 90, GETDATE())
    AND f.facility_status = 'ACTIVE'
ORDER BY rev.next_review_due ASC;


-- ------------------------------------------------------------
-- Query 5: Missing Financial Submissions
-- Tracks borrowers who have not submitted required documents
-- ------------------------------------------------------------
SELECT
    c.customer_name,
    f.facility_reference,
    doc.document_type,
    doc.due_date,
    doc.received_date,
    DATEDIFF(DAY, doc.due_date, GETDATE()) AS days_overdue,
    rm.rm_name,
    rm.rm_email,
    f.drawn_balance,
    r.risk_grade
FROM DocumentObligations doc
JOIN Facilities f ON doc.facility_id = f.facility_id
JOIN Customers c ON f.customer_id = c.customer_id
JOIN RelationshipManagers rm ON f.rm_id = rm.rm_id
JOIN RiskRatings r ON f.facility_id = r.facility_id
    AND r.effective_to IS NULL
WHERE doc.received_date IS NULL
    AND doc.due_date < GETDATE()
    AND f.facility_status = 'ACTIVE'
ORDER BY days_overdue DESC, f.drawn_balance DESC;
```

    FROM DailyBalances

        AND balance_date >= DATEADD(DAY, ?, CAST(GETDATE() AS DATE))
    ORDER BY balance_date
    """
    return pd.read_sql(query, conn, params=[facility_id, -lookback_days])


def load_stage_migrations(conn: pyodbc.Connection) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Load IFRS 9 stage changes in the current month."""
    query = """
    WITH curr AS (
        SELECT facility_id, ifrs9_stage, ecl_provision
        FROM IFRS9Staging
        WHERE staging_date = EOMONTH(GETDATE(), -1)
    ),
    prev AS (
        SELECT facility_id, ifrs9_stage, ecl_provision
        FROM IFRS9Staging
        WHERE staging_date = EOMONTH(GETDATE(), -2)
    )
    SELECT
        f.facility_reference,
        c.customer_name,
        p.ifrs9_stage AS stage_prev,
        cu.ifrs9_stage AS stage_curr,
        cu.ecl_provision - p.ecl_provision AS provision_movement,
        f.drawn_balance
    FROM curr cu
    JOIN prev p ON cu.facility_id = p.facility_id
    JOIN Facilities f ON cu.facility_id = f.facility_id
    JOIN Customers c ON f.customer_id = c.customer_id
    WHERE cu.ifrs9_stage <> p.ifrs9_stage
    """
    return pd.read_sql(query, conn)

class PortfolioMonitor:
    """
    Central class for ongoing portfolio monitoring analysis.
    Produces exception reports and monitoring metrics.
    """

    def __init__(self, portfolio_df: pd.DataFrame):
        self.df = portfolio_df.copy()
        self._add_derived_metrics()

    def _add_derived_metrics(self):
        """Add derived monitoring metrics to the portfolio DataFrame."""
        self.df['utilisation_pct'] = (
            self.df['utilisation'].fillna(0) * 100
        ).round(1)

        self.df['review_rag'] = pd.cut(
            self.df['days_to_review'],
            bins=[-np.inf, 0, 30, np.inf],
            labels=['OVERDUE', 'DUE_SOON', 'ON_TRACK']
        )

        self.df['util_rag'] = pd.cut(
            self.df['utilisation_pct'],
            bins=[-np.inf, 60, 80, 95, np.inf],
            labels=['GREEN', 'MONITOR', 'AMBER', 'RED']
        )

    def summary_statistics(self) -> dict:
        """Return portfolio-level summary statistics."""
        total_exposure = self.df['drawn_balance'].sum()
        weighted_pd = np.average(
            self.df['pd_estimate'].fillna(0),
            weights=self.df['drawn_balance'].fillna(0)
        )
        return {
            'total_facilities': len(self.df),
            'total_exposure': total_exposure,
            'weighted_avg_pd': weighted_pd,
            'stage_1_pct': (self.df['ifrs9_stage'] == 1).mean() * 100,
            'stage_2_pct': (self.df['ifrs9_stage'] == 2).mean() * 100,
            'stage_3_pct': (self.df['ifrs9_stage'] == 3).mean() * 100,
            'total_ecl': self.df['ecl_provision'].sum(),
            'reviews_overdue': (self.df['review_rag'] == 'OVERDUE').sum(),
            'reviews_due_soon': (self.df['review_rag'] == 'DUE_SOON').sum(),
            'covenant_breaches': (
                self.df['covenant_status'] == 'BREACH'
            ).sum(),
            'red_ewi_alerts': (
                self.df['ewi_alert_level'] == 'RED'
            ).sum(),
        }

    def exception_report(self) -> pd.DataFrame:
        """Return facilities requiring immediate attention."""
        exceptions = self.df[
            (self.df['review_rag'] == 'OVERDUE') |
            (self.df['util_rag'].isin(['AMBER', 'RED'])) |
            (self.df['covenant_status'] == 'BREACH') |
            (self.df['ewi_alert_level'] == 'RED') |
            (self.df['ifrs9_stage'] >= 2)
        ].copy()

        exceptions['priority_score'] = (
            (exceptions['ifrs9_stage'] == 3).astype(int) * 100 +
            (exceptions['covenant_status'] == 'BREACH').astype(int) * 50 +
            (exceptions['ewi_alert_level'] == 'RED').astype(int) * 40 +
            (exceptions['review_rag'] == 'OVERDUE').astype(int) * 30 +
            (exceptions['util_rag'] == 'RED').astype(int) * 20
        )

        return exceptions.sort_values('priority_score', ascending=False)

    def stage_distribution(self) -> pd.Series:
        """Return exposure-weighted stage distribution."""
        return self.df.groupby('ifrs9_stage')['drawn_balance'].sum()

    def sector_heatmap_data(self) -> pd.DataFrame:
        """Return sector × risk grade exposure matrix."""
        return self.df.pivot_table(
            values='drawn_balance',
            index='sector_code',
            columns='risk_grade',
            aggfunc='sum',
            fill_value=0
        )

def analyse_utilisation_trend(util_df: pd.DataFrame) -> dict:
    """
    Analyse utilisation trend for a single facility.
    Returns summary statistics and trend direction.
    """
    if util_df.empty:
        return {}

    util_df = util_df.copy()
    util_df['balance_date'] = pd.to_datetime(util_df['balance_date'])
    util_df = util_df.sort_values('balance_date')

    current = util_df['utilisation_pct'].iloc[-1]
    avg_30d = util_df['utilisation_pct'].tail(30).mean()
    avg_90d = util_df['utilisation_pct'].mean()
    peak_90d = util_df['utilisation_pct'].max()
    days_near_limit = (util_df['utilisation_pct'] >= 95).sum()

    # Simple linear trend: positive coefficient = increasing utilisation
    x = np.arange(len(util_df))
    coeffs = np.polyfit(x, util_df['utilisation_pct'], 1)
    trend_per_day = coeffs[0]

    return {
        'current_util_pct': round(current, 1),
        'avg_30d': round(avg_30d, 1),
        'avg_90d': round(avg_90d, 1),
        'peak_90d': round(peak_90d, 1),
        'days_near_limit_90d': int(days_near_limit),
        'trend_per_day': round(trend_per_day, 3),
        'trend_direction': 'INCREASING' if trend_per_day > 0.1 else
                           'DECREASING' if trend_per_day < -0.1 else 'STABLE',
        'alert_level': 'RED' if current >= 95 else
                       'AMBER' if current >= 80 else 'GREEN'
    }

# 5. STAGE MIGRATION REPORT
# ============================================================

def stage_migration_report(migrations_df: pd.DataFrame) -> dict:
    """
    Summarise IFRS 9 stage migrations for the month.
    """
    if migrations_df.empty:
        return {'total_migrations': 0}

    migrations_df['direction'] = np.where(
        migrations_df['stage_curr'] > migrations_df['stage_prev'],
        'DOWNGRADE', 'UPGRADE'
    )

    downgrades = migrations_df[migrations_df['direction'] == 'DOWNGRADE']
    upgrades = migrations_df[migrations_df['direction'] == 'UPGRADE']

    return {
        'total_migrations': len(migrations_df),
        'downgrades': len(downgrades),
        'upgrades': len(upgrades),
        'provision_increase_from_downgrades': downgrades[
            'provision_movement'
        ].sum(),
        'provision_decrease_from_upgrades': upgrades[
            'provision_movement'
        ].sum(),
        'net_provision_movement': migrations_df['provision_movement'].sum(),
        'stage_1_to_2': len(
            migrations_df[
                (migrations_df['stage_prev'] == 1) &
                (migrations_df['stage_curr'] == 2)
            ]
        ),
        'stage_2_to_3': len(
            migrations_df[
                (migrations_df['stage_prev'] == 2) &
                (migrations_df['stage_curr'] == 3)
            ]
        ),
        'stage_2_to_1': len(
            migrations_df[
                (migrations_df['stage_prev'] == 2) &
                (migrations_df['stage_curr'] == 1)
            ]
        ),
    }

def run_daily_monitoring(server: str, database: str):
    """
    Main daily monitoring run.
    Loads portfolio, runs analysis, generates exceptions.
    """
    print(f"=== DAILY MONITORING RUN — {date.today()} ===\n")

    conn = get_connection(server, database)

    # Load data
    portfolio_df = load_portfolio(conn)
    migrations_df = load_stage_migrations(conn)

    # Analyse
    monitor = PortfolioMonitor(portfolio_df)
    summary = monitor.summary_statistics()
    exceptions = monitor.exception_report()
    migrations = stage_migration_report(migrations_df)

    # Print summary
    print("PORTFOLIO SUMMARY:")
    for k, v in summary.items():
        print(f"  {k}: {v:,.0f}" if isinstance(v, (int, float)) else
              f"  {k}: {v}")

    print(f"\nSTAGE MIGRATIONS THIS MONTH:")
    for k, v in migrations.items():
        print(f"  {k}: {v:,.0f}" if isinstance(v, (int, float)) else
              f"  {k}: {v}")

    print(f"\nEXCEPTIONS REQUIRING ACTION: {len(exceptions)}")
    if not exceptions.empty:
        cols = [
            'customer_name', 'facility_reference', 'drawn_balance',
            'ifrs9_stage', 'review_rag', 'util_rag',
            'covenant_status', 'ewi_alert_level', 'priority_score'
        ]
        print(exceptions[cols].to_string(index=False))

    conn.close()
    return summary, exceptions, migrations

## 11. Interview Questions

**Q1: What is the purpose of the annual credit review and what does it contain?**
A: The annual review is a full re-assessment of the credit quality of a borrower, performed every 12 months (or more frequently for higher-risk names). It contains: updated financial analysis based on recent accounts; covenant compliance assessment; risk rating review; collateral valuation update; assessment of any material changes to the borrower's business, management, or market; updated financial model with projections; and a recommendation to continue, modify, or exit the facility. The credit paper is submitted to credit committee for approval.

**Q2: How does ongoing monitoring feed into IFRS 9 staging?**
A: Monitoring provides the data inputs for the monthly staging assessment. If a monitoring event triggers an SICR indicator — such as a covenant breach, a missed payment, a material deterioration in financial metrics, or placement on the watch list — the credit officer flags this to the finance function, which reclassifies the exposure from Stage 1 to Stage 2. This increases the provision from 12-month ECL to lifetime ECL. The monitoring system must therefore have direct integration with the IFRS 9 provisioning system.

**Q3: What triggers an out-of-cycle credit review?**
A: EWI alerts, covenant breach or near-breach, missed or late payment, material adverse news (fire, litigation, key customer loss), senior management changes, request from borrower for waiver or amendment, external credit rating downgrade, sector-wide shock, significant change in collateral value, or instruction from the regulator.

**Q4: Why is utilisation monitoring particularly important for revolving credit facilities?**
A: Because the drawn balance can change daily, unlike a term loan. Consistently high utilisation (>90%) signals the borrower is highly dependent on the facility and may be unable to repay without renewal — this is a liquidity stress indicator. It also affects EAD for IFRS 9 provisioning: a borrower who normally draws 50% of their facility but is now drawing 95% increases the bank's exposure. The CCF (Credit Conversion Factor) model, which converts undrawn commitments to exposure, may need to be updated.

**Q5: What is the difference between a periodic review and a continuous monitoring system?**
A: A periodic review (annual, quarterly) provides a comprehensive point-in-time assessment. Continuous monitoring provides real-time or near-real-time exception alerts between periodic reviews. Both are necessary: periodic reviews ensure thoroughness; continuous monitoring ensures early warning. The two should be integrated — an EWI alert from continuous monitoring should feed into the periodic review calendar and potentially trigger an early review.

## 12. Common Mistakes

**Mistake 1: Treating ongoing monitoring as a compliance box-ticking exercise.** The annual review is sometimes completed mechanically without genuine analytical judgment. The risk is that deterioration is missed because the analyst is focussed on completing the paperwork rather than understanding the business. Mitigation: senior credit officer sign-off that includes a business narrative, not just financial ratios.

**Mistake 2: Accepting borrower-prepared covenant compliance certificates without independent verification.** Borrowers occasionally make errors (or misrepresent) their covenant calculations. Banks must independently calculate covenants from the underlying management accounts received. Differences should always be investigated.

**Mistake 3: Failing to update the credit system promptly after a monitoring event.** If the IFRS 9 stage is not updated until the next periodic review, the provision will be understated in the intervening period. This is a regulatory breach under IFRS 9.

**Mistake 4: Monitoring financial metrics but ignoring qualitative signals.** A borrower's DSCR may still be above covenant but the auditor has resigned and the CFO has left. These qualitative signals often precede financial deterioration by 6-12 months. The monitoring framework must capture non-financial data.

**Mistake 5: Allowing review overdue situations to accumulate.** Once reviews fall significantly overdue, the credit file deteriorates and the bank loses situational awareness. Regulators cite overdue reviews as a governance failure. Portfolio managers should be incentivised to keep the review book current.

**Mistake 6: Not integrating Companies House alerts.** Many deteriorating SME and mid-market borrowers exhibit identifiable early warning signs on Companies House before the bank becomes aware: late filing of accounts, new charges registered by other creditors, director resignations, change of registered office. Automated alerts from Companies House data feeds are low-cost and high-value.

## 13. Case Studies

**Case Study 1: Retail Sector Portfolio — COVID-19 Monitoring Response (2020)**

A mid-tier bank had a £450m portfolio of secured lending to UK retail operators. In March 2020, the national lockdown caused all physical retail to cease. The monitoring team:
1. Initiated out-of-cycle reviews for all retail exposures within 2 weeks
2. Requested COVID-19 impact assessments from all borrowers (weekly cash flow forecasts)
3. Suspended quarterly covenant testing for Q2 and Q3 2020 under FCA guidance
4. Applied a sectoral SICR override to move the entire retail portfolio to Stage 2
5. Increased provisioning by £38m in Q1 2020 based on revised forward-looking assumptions
6. Established a dedicated weekly monitoring call for the top 20 retail exposures

Lesson: A sector-wide shock requires a portfolio response, not just individual facility reviews. The ability to apply sectoral SICR overlays is crucial.

**Case Study 2: Construction Company — Monitoring Failure Leading to Surprise Default**

A £12m term loan to a regional housebuilder. Annual review was completed and rated Grade 4 (satisfactory). Six months later, the company defaulted. Investigation revealed:
- Management accounts for the intervening period had not been received (document obligation tracking was not in place)
- Utilisation of the RCF had increased from 45% to 98% over 4 months (daily utilisation reports were generated but not reviewed)
- Companies House showed two director resignations and a new charge registered by a trade creditor
- A key subcontractor had issued court proceedings — mentioned in a news article but not captured by monitoring

Lesson: Monitoring data is only valuable if it is actually reviewed. Exception reports must be actioned. The monitoring process must cover qualitative signals, not just financial ratios.
