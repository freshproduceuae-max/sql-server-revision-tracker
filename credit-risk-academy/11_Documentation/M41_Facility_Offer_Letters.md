# M41 — Facility Offer Letters

## 1. Business Purpose

The Facility Offer Letter (FOL) — also called a Commitment Letter, Term Sheet, or Heads of Terms depending on the institution — is the commercial document that sets out the key terms on which the bank is willing to provide a facility to a borrower. It is issued after credit approval and before the formal Facility Agreement is executed.

The FOL serves two purposes simultaneously. Commercially, it communicates the agreed terms to the borrower in a readable, summary format — without the complexity and length of the full legal agreement. From a credit risk perspective, the FOL is the last checkpoint before legal documentation commences: it must accurately reflect the terms that were approved, including the security required, the financial covenant set, and the conditions attached to the bank's willingness to lend.

The FOL is typically signed by both the bank and the borrower as a condition precedent to legal documentation. Once signed, it creates a commitment — the bank has committed to document the facility on the stated terms, and the borrower has accepted those terms. This makes the FOL a legally significant document even though it is not the Facility Agreement itself.

Errors in the FOL — whether omissions of required security, incorrect covenant thresholds, or missing conditions — can create contractual exposure for the bank. If the FOL is signed and subsequently the bank tries to add conditions in the Facility Agreement that were not in the FOL, the borrower may argue that the bank is in breach of the FOL commitment. Careful FOL drafting therefore directly reduces legal and credit risk.

The FOL is also a negotiation document. Borrowers often push back on pricing, covenant levels, information requirements, and conditions. The RM must understand which terms are negotiable (within the credit approval) and which are non-negotiable. Any material departure from the approved terms — whether upward (better for the borrower) or downward (weaker for the bank) — requires formal re-approval.

## 2. Accounting Concepts

**Commitment Accounting:** Once the FOL is signed, the bank must recognise the undrawn commitment in its regulatory capital calculations. An undrawn revolving credit facility attracts a credit conversion factor (CCF) — typically 20–75% depending on the facility type and original maturity — which is applied to the undrawn amount to calculate the credit equivalent exposure for RWA purposes. The FOL signing date is therefore the accounting trigger for regulatory capital consumption, even before the Facility Agreement is executed.

**Fee Income Recognition:** The FOL specifies fees payable by the borrower: arrangement fee (one-time, paid on signing or drawdown), commitment fee (ongoing, on undrawn amounts), agency fee (in syndicated deals), and utilisation fee (where applicable on high-utilisation RCFs). Under IFRS 9, arrangement fees that are integral to the effective interest rate of the loan (origination fees) must be deducted from the initial carrying amount of the loan and amortised over the life of the facility using the effective interest method. Front-end fees that do not relate to the credit are recognised as income immediately.

**Deferred Income:** Commitment fees (charged as a percentage of undrawn amounts) are recognised on a straight-line basis over the period to which they relate. If a borrower cancels the facility early, unused commitment fees cease to accrue.

**Impact on Borrower Accounts:** The borrower's accountant will review the FOL to determine the accounting classification of the facility (floating rate vs fixed rate, revolving vs term), the effective interest rate for amortised cost measurement, and whether any covenants or restrictions constitute "qualifying floating charges" that affect classification under IAS 1 (current vs non-current liability).

## 3. Financial Concepts

**Structure of a Facility Offer Letter:**

1. **Borrower / Obligor Details:** Legal name of the borrower, registered number, address. If there are guarantors or additional obligors, they are identified here. The credit approval specifies who the borrower must be; the FOL must match exactly.

2. **Facility Type and Amount:** RCF, term loan, overdraft, letter of credit facility. Amount in sterling (or multi-currency if agreed). Sub-limits where applicable (e.g., a £20m RCF with a £5m sub-limit for letters of credit).

3. **Purpose:** The stated purpose of the facility. Must match the purpose approved in the credit paper. "General corporate purposes" is not acceptable for term facilities — the purpose must be specific (e.g., "to finance the acquisition of [Target Company] per the SPA dated [date]").

4. **Tenor / Availability Period / Maturity Date:** For a term loan: the availability period (window during which the loan can be drawn), followed by the maturity date. For a revolving credit: the availability period (during which it can be used) which runs to the maturity date. For an overdraft: typically demand, reviewed annually.

5. **Pricing:** 
   - Margin: the credit spread above the reference rate (e.g., SONIA + 2.25% per annum).
   - Reference Rate: for GBP facilities, compounded SONIA; for USD, SOFR; for EUR, EURIBOR.
   - Commitment Fee: annual rate on undrawn amounts (typically 30–40% of the margin).
   - Arrangement Fee: one-time fee expressed as a percentage of the total facility.
   - Other Fees: agency fee, utilisation fee, amendment fee schedule.

6. **Availability Conditions:** Conditions that must be met before the facility is available (CPs to first utilisation). Reference to the full CP list in the Facility Agreement or annexure.

7. **Repayment Schedule:** For term loans: scheduled amortisation (quarterly, semi-annual, or bullet). Final repayment date. Any clean-down requirement for revolving facilities.

8. **Security Required:** Description of security to be taken: fixed and floating charge debenture, mortgage over specified property (with LTV requirement), personal guarantee (with amount cap, if any), pledge over shares. Each security item must match exactly what was approved in the credit paper.

9. **Financial Covenants:** The covenant set, with exact threshold levels, test frequency, and the defined financial terms (EBITDA, net debt, DSCR) to be specified. Covenant levels must match the approved credit paper, not the RM's initial term sheet (which may have been optimistically drafted).

10. **Information Undertakings:** Annual audited accounts within [x] days of financial year end; quarterly/monthly management accounts within [x] days of period end; compliance certificate with each financial covenant test; prompt notification of material events; board-approved budget/forecasts.

11. **Material Adverse Change:** A statement that the bank's willingness to proceed is conditional on no material adverse change in the borrower's financial condition, business, or prospects occurring between FOL signature and first drawdown.

12. **Governing Law:** The FOL is governed by English law (for UK deals). This has practical implications for jurisdiction of dispute resolution.

13. **Validity Period:** The FOL must be accepted by the borrower within a specified period (typically 10–30 business days). If not accepted within this period, the offer lapses.

14. **Conditions to Acceptance:** Actions the borrower must take to formally accept the FOL: execution and return of the FOL, payment of any upfront fees required on acceptance.

## 4. Statistical Concepts

**Pricing Models and RAROC:** The FOL pricing (margin, fees) is set based on the RAROC model. The RM negotiates within a margin range approved by the credit team; the final margin in the FOL is the agreed commercial outcome. A FOL that shows a margin lower than the approved range without formal re-approval is a policy breach.

**Covenant Setting Methodology:** Financial covenant thresholds are set with reference to the base case financial projections, applying a headroom percentage. Typical headroom methodology: DSCR covenant = base case DSCR × 80% (20% headroom to the base case); leverage covenant = base case leverage × 115% (15% headroom on leverage); interest cover = base case interest cover × 80%. This ensures the borrower has meaningful headroom in the base case, and that covenant breach is an early warning of significant deterioration rather than a minor miss.

**Fee Economics:** The effective yield on a loan includes the margin, the amortised arrangement fee, and commitment fee income. For a 5-year term loan with a 2.25% margin and a 1.5% arrangement fee amortised over 5 years, the effective yield is approximately 2.55% per annum — materially above the headline margin. Analysts model the all-in yield for RAROC purposes.

## 5. Regulatory Framework

**PRA Expectations on Documentation Standards:** The PRA's supervisory approach expects banks to document the terms of credit facilities clearly and completely. EBA Guidelines on Loan Origination and Monitoring (EBA/GL/2020/06) require that the terms and conditions of the facility are documented in writing before drawdown, including all the risk-relevant terms assessed in the credit underwriting process.

**Consumer Credit Act 1974 (for SME / sole trader lenders):** For some smaller business loans, particularly to sole traders and some micro-businesses, the Consumer Credit Act may apply — requiring prescribed information in the credit agreement, a right of withdrawal, and specific form requirements. The CCA does not apply to most corporate lending, but the bank must have a clear test for when CCA applies.

**Unfair Terms in Consumer Contracts Regulations (UTCCR):** Not directly applicable to sophisticated corporate borrowers, but concepts of good faith and fairness in contract apply to all commercial contracts under general contract law.

**SMCR and Accountability:** Under the Senior Managers and Certification Regime, the RM who signs or approves a Facility Offer Letter carries personal accountability for ensuring that the FOL accurately reflects the approved terms and does not commit the bank to terms beyond the credit approval.

**Competition and Market Authority (CMA):** The CMA has examined SME business banking and lending practices. Any FOL terms that could be characterised as anti-competitive (e.g., restrictions on the borrower refinancing to another bank without penalty, that are not disclosed clearly) must be reviewed by the bank's legal team.

## 6. Data Required

To prepare and review a FOL, the following information is required:

- The credit approval decision and conditions (the approved credit paper or credit approval memo)
- The term sheet or heads of terms negotiated with the borrower prior to credit approval
- The borrower's legal name and registered details (Companies House)
- The group structure chart (to identify any required guarantors)
- Security valuation reports (to confirm LTV calculations)
- The bank's standard FOL template for the relevant facility type (bilateral term loan, RCF, etc.)
- The bank's pricing schedule for commitment fees and arrangement fees
- Covenant definition annex (from the bank's standard credit documentation library)
- Any specific negotiated terms agreed with the borrower (and their credit approval status)

## 7. How Analysts Actually Work

**FOL Preparation:** In many banks, the FOL is prepared by the RM or a documentation specialist, using a standard template. The credit analyst reviews the draft FOL before it is issued to ensure that the terms match the credit approval. This review is not optional — it is the credit team's last opportunity to catch errors before the FOL is signed and the bank is committed.

**Credit Analyst Review of FOL:** The credit analyst compares the FOL to the credit approval memo on a line-by-line basis, checking:
- Is the borrower entity the approved borrower?
- Is the facility amount within the approved amount?
- Is the purpose consistent with the approved purpose?
- Is the security package consistent with what was approved?
- Are the covenant thresholds at or more conservative than approved?
- Are the information undertakings consistent with the approved monitoring requirements?
- Does the FOL include all conditions attached to the credit approval?

**Negotiation Points:** Borrowers commonly push back on: pricing (always), covenant headroom (they want more), reporting frequency (quarterly preferred over monthly), dividend restrictions (wanting to exclude dividends below a threshold), and acquisition permissions (wanting a higher threshold before bank consent is required). The RM must distinguish between negotiating points that can be conceded within the approved authority, and those that require credit re-approval.

**FOL as a Credit Risk Document:** The credit team should treat the FOL as a live credit risk document, not merely an administrative step. If a borrower is pushing hard to remove a key covenant or weaken the security package, this signals that the borrower anticipates the covenant will be tested or that the security is genuinely needed. The credit team should consider whether the borrower's negotiating position reveals information about the credit risk that was not apparent at the time of approval.

**Amendment FOLs:** If, after initial FOL signing, a borrower requests a change in terms (e.g., an increase in the facility amount, a waiver of a condition, or a change in the repayment schedule), an amendment to the FOL or a new FOL is required. All material amendments require credit re-approval at the appropriate authority level.

## 9. SQL Implementation

```sql
-- ===== FACILITY OFFER LETTER REGISTER =====
SELECT
    c.client_name,
    c.rm_name,
    fol.fol_date,
    fol.facility_type,
    fol.facility_amount_m,
    fol.margin_pct,
    fol.commitment_fee_pct,
    fol.arrangement_fee_pct,
    fol.maturity_date,
    fol.validity_expiry,
    fol.accepted_date,
    fol.fol_status,    -- 'ISSUED', 'ACCEPTED', 'EXPIRED', 'SUPERSEDED'
    DATEDIFF(DAY, fol.fol_date, ISNULL(fol.accepted_date, GETDATE())) AS days_to_acceptance,
    ca.approval_date AS credit_approval_date,
    ca.approved_by
FROM facility_offer_letters fol
JOIN clients c ON fol.client_id = c.client_id
LEFT JOIN credit_approvals ca ON fol.approval_id = ca.approval_id
ORDER BY fol.fol_date DESC;

-- ===== FOL vs APPROVAL VARIANCE CHECK =====
-- Flag where FOL terms differ materially from approved credit terms
SELECT
    c.client_name,
    fol.fol_date,
    -- Margin
    ca.approved_margin_pct AS approved_margin,
    fol.margin_pct AS fol_margin,
    CASE WHEN fol.margin_pct < ca.approved_margin_pct - 0.10
         THEN 'UNDER-PRICED vs APPROVAL' ELSE 'OK' END AS margin_status,
    -- Facility amount
    ca.approved_amount_m AS approved_amount,
    fol.facility_amount_m AS fol_amount,
    CASE WHEN fol.facility_amount_m > ca.approved_amount_m
         THEN 'EXCEEDS APPROVED AMOUNT' ELSE 'OK' END AS amount_status,
    -- DSCR covenant
    ca.approved_dscr_threshold AS approved_dscr_cov,
    fol.dscr_covenant_threshold AS fol_dscr_cov,
    CASE WHEN fol.dscr_covenant_threshold < ca.approved_dscr_threshold
         THEN 'COVENANT WEAKER THAN APPROVED' ELSE 'OK' END AS dscr_cov_status,
    -- Leverage covenant
    ca.approved_leverage_threshold AS approved_lev_cov,
    fol.leverage_covenant_threshold AS fol_lev_cov,
    CASE WHEN fol.leverage_covenant_threshold > ca.approved_leverage_threshold
         THEN 'COVENANT WEAKER THAN APPROVED' ELSE 'OK' END AS lev_cov_status
FROM facility_offer_letters fol
JOIN clients c ON fol.client_id = c.client_id
JOIN credit_approvals ca ON fol.approval_id = ca.approval_id
WHERE fol.fol_status IN ('ISSUED', 'ACCEPTED')
ORDER BY fol.fol_date DESC;

-- ===== NEGOTIATION CHANGE LOG =====
-- Track changes made during FOL negotiation
SELECT
    c.client_name,
    nl.change_date,
    nl.term_changed,
    nl.original_term,
    nl.revised_term,
    nl.change_reason,
    nl.credit_reapproval_required,
    nl.reapproval_obtained_date,
    nl.changed_by
FROM fol_negotiation_log nl
JOIN clients c ON nl.client_id = c.client_id
ORDER BY nl.change_date DESC;

-- ===== FOL PIPELINE CONVERSION RATE =====
-- What proportion of FOLs convert to executed Facility Agreements?
SELECT
    YEAR(fol_date) AS yr,
    QUARTER(fol_date) AS qtr,
    COUNT(*) AS fols_issued,
    SUM(CASE WHEN fol_status = 'ACCEPTED' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS accepted,
    SUM(CASE WHEN EXISTS (
        SELECT 1 FROM facility_agreements fa
        WHERE fa.fol_id = fol.fol_id
          AND fa.status = 'EXECUTED'
    ) THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS converted_to_executed_fa,
    ROUND(SUM(CASE WHEN fol_status = 'ACCEPTED' THEN 1.0 ELSE 0 END)
          / COUNT(*) * 100, 1) AS acceptance_rate_pct
FROM facility_offer_letters fol
WHERE fol_date >= DATEADD(YEAR, -2, GETDATE())
GROUP BY YEAR(fol_date), QUARTER(fol_date)
ORDER BY yr, qtr;
```

## 10. Python Implementation

```python
from dataclasses import dataclass, field
from typing import List, Optional, Dict
from datetime import date, timedelta
from enum import Enum
import pandas as pd


class FacilityType(Enum):
    TERM_LOAN = "Term Loan"
    RCF = "Revolving Credit Facility"
    OVERDRAFT = "Overdraft"
    LC_FACILITY = "Letter of Credit Facility"
    MULTI_OPTION = "Multi-Option Facility"


class FOLStatus(Enum):
    DRAFT = "DRAFT"
    ISSUED = "ISSUED"
    ACCEPTED = "ACCEPTED"
    EXPIRED = "EXPIRED"
    SUPERSEDED = "SUPERSEDED"


@dataclass
class CovenantTerm:
    name: str
    metric: str
    threshold: float
    direction: str    # 'MIN' or 'MAX'
    test_frequency: str
    definition_summary: str

    def check_compliance(self, actual_value: float) -> dict:
        if self.direction == 'MIN':
            compliant = actual_value >= self.threshold
            headroom = actual_value - self.threshold
        else:
            compliant = actual_value <= self.threshold
            headroom = self.threshold - actual_value
        return {
            'covenant': self.name,
            'threshold': self.threshold,
            'actual': actual_value,
            'headroom': round(headroom, 4),
            'status': 'COMPLIANT' if compliant else 'BREACH'
        }


@dataclass
class FacilityOfferLetter:
    # Parties
    borrower_name: str
    borrower_reg_number: str
    bank_name: str
    rm_name: str

    # Facility terms
    facility_type: FacilityType
    facility_amount_m: float
    purpose: str
    currency: str = "GBP"

    # Tenor
    availability_end_date: Optional[date] = None
    maturity_date: Optional[date] = None

    # Pricing
    reference_rate: str = "Compounded SONIA"
    margin_pct: float = 0.0
    commitment_fee_pct: float = 0.0
    arrangement_fee_pct: float = 0.0
    utilisation_fee_pct: float = 0.0     # for high-utilisation RCFs

    # Security
    security_description: List[str] = field(default_factory=list)

    # Covenants
    financial_covenants: List[CovenantTerm] = field(default_factory=list)

    # Information undertakings
    annual_accounts_days: int = 180
    management_accounts_frequency: str = "QUARTERLY"
    management_accounts_days: int = 45
    compliance_certificate_required: bool = True

    # Conditions
    mac_clause: bool = True
    cross_default_threshold_m: float = 1.0
    negative_pledge: bool = True

    # FOL administration
    fol_date: date = field(default_factory=date.today)
    validity_days: int = 20
    status: FOLStatus = FOLStatus.DRAFT
    accepted_date: Optional[date] = None

    @property
    def validity_expiry(self) -> date:
        return self.fol_date + timedelta(days=self.validity_days)

    @property
    def tenor_years(self) -> Optional[float]:
        if self.maturity_date and self.fol_date:
            return (self.maturity_date - self.fol_date).days / 365.25
        return None

    def effective_yield_pct(self, expected_utilisation_pct: float = 70.0) -> float:
        """Estimate all-in effective yield including arrangement fee amortisation."""
        if self.tenor_years is None or self.tenor_years == 0:
            return self.margin_pct
        utilisation = expected_utilisation_pct / 100
        annual_margin = self.facility_amount_m * utilisation * self.margin_pct / 100
        annual_commitment = self.facility_amount_m * (1 - utilisation) * self.commitment_fee_pct / 100
        annual_arrangement = self.facility_amount_m * self.arrangement_fee_pct / 100 / self.tenor_years
        total_annual = annual_margin + annual_commitment + annual_arrangement
        return round(total_annual / self.facility_amount_m * 100, 4)

    def covenant_compliance_check(self, actuals: Dict[str, float]) -> pd.DataFrame:
        results = []
        for cv in self.financial_covenants:
            if cv.metric in actuals:
                results.append(cv.check_compliance(actuals[cv.metric]))
            else:
                results.append({
                    'covenant': cv.name,
                    'threshold': cv.threshold,
                    'actual': None,
                    'headroom': None,
                    'status': 'NO DATA'
                })
        return pd.DataFrame(results)

    def generate_fol_summary(self) -> str:
        """Generate a text summary of the FOL for review."""
        lines = [
            f"FACILITY OFFER LETTER SUMMARY",
            f"{'='*60}",
            f"Borrower:          {self.borrower_name} ({self.borrower_reg_number})",
            f"Bank:              {self.bank_name}",
            f"RM:                {self.rm_name}",
            f"FOL Date:          {self.fol_date}",
            f"Validity Expiry:   {self.validity_expiry}",
            f"",
            f"FACILITY",
            f"-"*40,
            f"Type:              {self.facility_type.value}",
            f"Amount:            {self.currency} {self.facility_amount_m:.1f}m",
            f"Purpose:           {self.purpose}",
            f"Maturity:          {self.maturity_date}",
            f"Tenor:             {self.tenor_years:.1f} years" if self.tenor_years else "",
            f"",
            f"PRICING",
            f"-"*40,
            f"Reference Rate:    {self.reference_rate}",
            f"Margin:            {self.margin_pct:.2f}% per annum",
            f"Commitment Fee:    {self.commitment_fee_pct:.2f}% per annum on undrawn",
            f"Arrangement Fee:   {self.arrangement_fee_pct:.2f}% of facility amount",
            f"Effective Yield:   ~{self.effective_yield_pct():.2f}% (at 70% utilisation)",
            f"",
            f"SECURITY",
            f"-"*40,
        ]
        for sec in self.security_description:
            lines.append(f"  - {sec}")
        lines += [
            f"",
            f"FINANCIAL COVENANTS",
            f"-"*40,
        ]
        for cv in self.financial_covenants:
            dir_str = ">=" if cv.direction == 'MIN' else "<="
            lines.append(f"  - {cv.name}: {dir_str} {cv.threshold} ({cv.test_frequency})")
        lines += [
            f"",
            f"INFORMATION UNDERTAKINGS",
            f"-"*40,
            f"  - Annual accounts within {self.annual_accounts_days} days of FYE",
            f"  - Management accounts: {self.management_accounts_frequency}, "
            f"within {self.management_accounts_days} days",
            f"  - Compliance certificate: {'Required' if self.compliance_certificate_required else 'Not required'}",
            f"",
            f"OTHER TERMS",
            f"-"*40,
            f"  - MAC Clause:          {'Yes' if self.mac_clause else 'No'}",
            f"  - Negative Pledge:     {'Yes' if self.negative_pledge else 'No'}",
            f"  - Cross-Default:       Threshold {self.currency} {self.cross_default_threshold_m:.1f}m",
        ]
        return "\n".join(lines)


# ===== FOL vs CREDIT APPROVAL COMPARISON =====

def compare_fol_to_approval(fol: FacilityOfferLetter, approval: dict) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Compare FOL terms against the credit approval to identify discrepancies.
    approval dict keys: amount_m, margin_pct, dscr_threshold, leverage_threshold,
                        security_required, borrower_name
    """
    checks = []
    def check(term, approved_val, fol_val, direction='equal', tolerance=0.0):
        if direction == 'equal':
            ok = str(approved_val).strip().lower() == str(fol_val).strip().lower()
        elif direction == 'gte':  # fol must be >= approved (stricter than or equal to)
            ok = float(fol_val) >= float(approved_val) - tolerance
        elif direction == 'lte':  # fol must be <= approved
            ok = float(fol_val) <= float(approved_val) + tolerance
        checks.append({
            'Term': term,
            'Approved': approved_val,
            'FOL': fol_val,
            'Status': 'OK' if ok else 'MISMATCH — REVIEW REQUIRED'
        })

    check('Borrower Name', approval.get('borrower_name'), fol.borrower_name)
    check('Facility Amount (£m)', approval.get('amount_m'), fol.facility_amount_m, 'lte', 0.01)
    check('Margin (%)', approval.get('margin_pct'), fol.margin_pct, 'gte', 0.01)
    check('DSCR Covenant Threshold',
          approval.get('dscr_threshold'),
          next((cv.threshold for cv in fol.financial_covenants
                if 'DSCR' in cv.name.upper()), 'NOT SET'),
          'gte', 0.01)
    check('Max Leverage Covenant',
          approval.get('leverage_threshold'),
          next((cv.threshold for cv in fol.financial_covenants
                if 'LEVERAGE' in cv.name.upper() or 'NET DEBT' in cv.name.upper()), 'NOT SET'),
          'lte', 0.01)
    return pd.DataFrame(checks)
```

## 11. Interview Questions

**Q1: What is the difference between a Facility Offer Letter and a Facility Agreement?**
A: The FOL is the commercial summary of agreed terms — readable, concise, and signed by both parties to commit to the deal. It is pre-contractual documentation. The Facility Agreement is the full legal contract that governs the entire relationship, containing detailed definitions, extensive representations and warranties, undertakings, events of default, and governing law provisions. The FOL commits the bank to document the deal on the stated terms; the Facility Agreement is that documentation. If there is a conflict between the FOL and the Facility Agreement, the Facility Agreement prevails.

**Q2: How do you set a financial covenant threshold, and what headroom should be applied?**
A: I start from the base case financial projections in the credit paper. For a minimum DSCR covenant, I set the threshold at approximately 80% of the base case DSCR — providing 20% headroom. This means the borrower must underperform the base case by more than 20% before a covenant breach occurs. The logic is that covenants should be an early warning of material deterioration, not a technical trigger on minor underperformance. I also stress-test: under the downside scenario, does the borrower breach the covenant? If yes, the covenant is set appropriately. If the borrower breezes through even the severe downside without breaching, the covenant may be too loosely set to provide useful early warning.

**Q3: A borrower accepts a FOL but during documentation negotiation asks to remove the negative pledge clause, arguing it is commercially unnecessary as they have no plans to create additional security. How do you respond?**
A: The negative pledge is a key credit protection. The borrower having "no plans" to create additional security does not remove the need for the restriction. If the borrower later creates security for another lender without the bank's consent, the bank's unsecured position would be materially worse. I would explain that the negative pledge is a standard term that protects the bank's position and was part of the approved credit structure. Removing it requires credit re-approval, not just a documentation concession by the RM.

**Q4: What is a utilisation fee and why might it be included in an RCF?**
A: A utilisation fee is an additional fee charged when the RCF is heavily utilised — for example, a 0.25% fee if drawn above 75% of the facility limit. It discourages chronic high utilisation (which converts the RCF into a de facto term loan) and compensates the bank for the higher credit risk associated with a borrower who is consistently fully drawn on its committed facility. High utilisation is itself an early warning indicator, and the utilisation fee gives the borrower a financial incentive to manage utilisation levels.

**Q5: What happens if the bank issues a FOL but fails to execute the Facility Agreement before the validity period expires?**
A: If the borrower has accepted the FOL within the validity period, the bank has a contractual commitment to document the facility on the stated terms. The validity period is the period for the borrower to accept, not for the bank to execute. Once accepted, the commitment is binding until the conditions to documentation are satisfied (or fail). If the bank fails to execute the Facility Agreement without good cause (e.g., the borrower has met all conditions), the borrower may have a breach of contract claim. If conditions to documentation fail (e.g., legal opinion cannot be obtained, security cannot be perfected), the bank may withdraw without breach. The FOL should clearly specify the conditions on which the commitment is contingent.

## 12. Common Mistakes

**Mistake 1: Issuing the FOL before credit approval.** Some RMs issue a FOL to win the client's commitment before formal credit approval. This is a serious breach of credit policy — the bank is committing to lend before the credit has been assessed and approved. If the credit is subsequently declined, the bank may face a damages claim from the borrower.

**Mistake 2: Using generic covenant definitions without checking agreement with the Facility Agreement template.** A FOL that says "EBITDA leverage covenant of 3.5x" without defining EBITDA creates ambiguity in the subsequent Facility Agreement. The FOL should reference the definition annex or use defined terms consistent with the standard documentation.

**Mistake 3: Setting covenants identical to the base case forecast.** If the DSCR covenant is set at the exact projected DSCR, the borrower will breach on any underperformance. Covenants must provide headroom relative to the base case.

**Mistake 4: Omitting information undertakings from the FOL.** The borrower signs the FOL agreeing to the commercial terms. If the information undertakings are not in the FOL (left entirely for the Facility Agreement), the borrower may resist them in documentation. The FOL should summarise the key information requirements to avoid later negotiation.

**Mistake 5: Not including a MAC condition in the FOL.** Between FOL acceptance and first drawdown, the borrower's condition may deteriorate. Without a MAC condition (explicitly stated in the FOL), the bank may have limited ability to withdraw from the commitment if the borrower's financial condition materially worsens before drawdown.

## 13. Case Studies

**Case Study 1: The Covenant Mismatch**
A credit paper approves a leveraged buyout with a leverage covenant of 3.5x. The RM, under pressure from the private equity sponsor to show flexibility, includes a 4.0x leverage covenant in the FOL "by mistake". The credit analyst's review of the FOL identifies the discrepancy. The RM argues it was a clerical error, but the sponsor has now seen 4.0x in the FOL and is unwilling to accept 3.5x. The credit team requires re-submission for approval — the additional 0.5x leverage covenant headroom for the borrower is a material change in the bank's credit protection. The lesson: FOL review by the credit team is non-negotiable, and every term must match the approval.

**Case Study 2: The Missing Security**
A FOL is issued for a £5m term loan to a property company. The credit paper requires a mortgage over the commercial property being acquired (LTV covenant of 65%). The RM, in the rush to issue the FOL, lists security as "fixed and floating charge debenture" only, omitting the property mortgage. The borrower accepts the FOL. In documentation, the bank's solicitors insist on the mortgage. The borrower argues this was not in the FOL. A dispute arises; ultimately the mortgage is included, but the borrower relationship is damaged and the RM faces a formal review of FOL preparation processes.
